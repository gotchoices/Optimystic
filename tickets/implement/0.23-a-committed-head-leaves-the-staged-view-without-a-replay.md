GitHub #53. **This report is the condition the `NOTE:` at the `replay` step of `Collection.headCommitHandle` was waiting for**, and the tradeoff in [docs/offline-sync.md §Failure modes and accepted tradeoffs](../../docs/offline-sync.md#failure-modes-and-accepted-tradeoffs) names the same remedy: per-segment transforms that let a drained head be subtracted rather than replayed. This ticket takes that remedy, and also carries the final before-and-after measurement for the whole report.

Per-entry cost doubles when the queue doubles, so a drain is quadratic in its length, and 78–88% of it is replay.

What this means for the report's two effects:

- **"An offline commit with an empty queue costs 4× an online one"** is not cloning, and the commit alone is not the cause: with nothing sent, a commit (5.6 ms) costs less than an online one (7.7 ms). Two other things produce the 4×:
  - The journal flushes to disk three times per entry, and the commit itself waits for the first (docs/offline-sync.md §Write ordering). The report's run was 38% idle. Giving the memory journal a 20 ms flush (`FLUSH_MS=20`) produces 32.8 → 56.9 back to back and 93.8 per transaction at depth 1, the report's shape. 20 ms is an assumption: the reporter's disk was not measured, and a timer on this machine overshoots. These flushes are the acknowledgement's promise and are not removed here (see backlog `feat-a-committed-drain-entry-can-settle-without-waiting-for-disk`).
  - The baseline wrote nothing to disk: the script keeps blocks in `MemoryRawStorage`. With `STORAGE=file`, online costs 41–50 ms per transaction here, which is more than an offline acknowledgement.
- **"Each commit slows with depth"** is mostly this ticket. The commit runs beside the drain on one event loop, and the drain's replay takes the time: clones per transaction go 324 → 3,116 back to back against 96 → 225 with the gate closed. The rest of the commit's own growth is the two prerequisite tickets.
- **The report's guess, that the layering is offline-specific, does not hold.** Reads go `Atomic` → `Tracker` → `CacheSource` online and offline alike, at about 180 clones per transaction online. What is offline-specific is staging twice (the live tracker, then a head tracker) and replaying afterwards, which this ticket removes. The general copy-per-layer cost is backlog `debt-block-reads-copy-at-every-tracker-layer`.

## The design

A sealed segment keeps the transforms its own actions produced. A head commit sends those transforms, with the block ids the live tracker already holds, and then takes them out of the live tracker without running a handler.

**Why this is correct.** A segment's actions were staged over committed state plus the segments ahead of it. Once those have committed, with nothing from any other writer in between, committed state is exactly that base, so re-running the handlers would make the same decisions. Their only different output would be fresh ids for inserted blocks, which is the reason a replay is needed today: `Collection.buildHeadTracker` mints ids the live tracker does not hold. Any refresh that adopts another writer's revision still replays (`Collection.mustReplay`), which re-judges every guard and re-captures every segment's transforms. So the invariant "a pending action was applied against the revision it commits over" is kept.

**The state.**

- `SealedSegment.delta?: Transforms`: what its actions staged. `undefined` means not known, and every path below falls back to today's behaviour, so an omission is slow and never wrong.
- `SegmentList` gains `openDelta` and `openApplied` beside `open`, covering the open segment the same way. `Collection.actInternal` folds each action's transforms into `openDelta`, sums its tally into `openApplied`, and `seal` moves both onto the segment. Today `actInternal` discards the tally, and `Collection.buildHeadTracker` sets `SealedSegment.applied`; with no rebuild the tally has to come from staging, or `reportCommittedHead` logs `collection:committed-head-tally-missing`.
- Getting the delta costs no extra clone: `Atomic.commit` hands its transforms to `Tracker.absorb`, which clones what it keeps, so the collection can keep what `child.reset()` returned. Change `Collection.stageInto` to return the transforms beside the tally. The cost is memory, roughly the staged content twice while queued, recorded in a `NOTE:` at the field.
- A helper in `packages/db-core/src/transform/helpers.ts` folds one `Transforms` into another under `Tracker`'s semantics (an update of a block inserted in the target is applied into it; a delete removes the insert and any updates).
- `Collection.replayActions` re-captures every `delta`, `openDelta` and `openApplied` as it stages.
- Every place that sets `segments.open` directly goes through one `SegmentList` method taking the delta and tally, or `undefined`. That covers the `filterAgainstEntry` and `consumeOwnEntry` paths, `clearPendingActions`, `unstage` and `restorePending`. `CollectionSnapshot` gains the captured `openDelta` and `openApplied` (shared, per ticket `a-savepoint-snapshot-shares-staged-blocks-instead-of-copying-them`), and `unseal` moves the delta back.

**Sending.** `Collection.headTrackerFor` builds the head tracker from `segment.delta` when it can: a `Tracker` over the read cache with a copy of the delta, and pins taken from the live tracker's pins for the delta's update-only ids, so the moved-base logic keeps applying. It records the current **replay epoch**, a counter `Collection.replayActions` bumps. It uses today's `Collection.buildHeadTracker` instead when the delta is `undefined`, when the collection holds no committed revision (an invented collection's first commit has to carry the baseline), or when `Tracker.movedBases` reports a moved base. A `resumedAttempt` keeps its existing branch. The rule that it is built once per revision and reused across attempts stays, which is what `retry-resends-identical-transforms.spec.ts` pins.

**Removing the committed head.** The handle's `replay` step takes the new path only if the attempt was built from the delta and the replay epoch has not moved since (a reader's refresh during the network window may have replayed, and then the live ids differ from the attempt's). Otherwise it runs `Collection.replayActions`, as today. The new path:

1. For each id the head's delta touched that a later delta or the open delta updates, and that the read cache no longer retains (`CacheSource.retains`), read it through the cache now, awaited under the latch. `CacheSource.transformCache` folds updates only into a cached block, so an evicted one would keep a stale revision and its pin would name the wrong base.
2. Then in one synchronous step: call `viewChanged`, and hand the head's touched ids and the remaining deltas to a new `Tracker` method that clears those ids (`inserts`, `updates`, `deletes`, memo) and re-folds them from the remaining deltas in order, ending with the open delta. Blocks the head did not touch are not visited. For an id left update-only, the pin is rebased to what the cache now holds: the operations were computed against content that included the head, and that content is now committed at the head's revision. So this is not a moved base, and `Tracker.revalidatePin`'s rule is not bent, because nothing is re-pinned over operations built for another base.

`viewChanged` stays even though no id changes, because a read that fetched its base before the fold and applied the remaining operations after it would otherwise see content missing the head. It is the same signal readers already handle (docs/internals.md, "The drain changes a tree beneath its readers"), fired once per commit instead of once per unit re-staged.

Finding the deltas that touch an id scans the remaining segments: a map lookup per segment per touched id, so this keeps a small quadratic term in the drain. Leave a `NOTE:` naming it, with an id-to-segments index as the remedy.

**Rejected alternatives.**

- Trackers chained per segment, as the current `NOTE:` proposed. Popping is trivial, but every read walks one tracker per queued segment, which moves the cost onto the commit path this report is about.
- Deferring the replay until something next reads. It makes a drain with no local activity linear, but each commit made alongside a drain still pays a whole-queue replay, which is the report's back-to-back curve.
- Keeping the rebuild and copying its ids over. It would have to run the handlers per head anyway, and the rebuild's second staging is itself a measured cost at depth 1.

## Edge cases & interactions

- A rival's commit adopted by the head's opening refresh, or by a reader's refresh during the network window: `Collection.replayActions` runs, deltas are recaptured and the epoch moves. An attempt still in flight with the old ids then takes the full replay when it lands. Test, below.
- A torn head finished and consumed by a refresh (`Collection.consumeHead`): keeps forcing a replay. `packages/db-core/test/own-entry-completes-the-action.spec.ts`.
- A resumed attempt after a restart: the segments were restored by `Collection.restoreSealed`, which replays, and the recorded attempt's ids differ from the live tracker's, so it must take the replay. `packages/quereus-plugin-optimystic/test/offline-restore.spec.ts`.
- An invented collection's first head: rebuild and replay, as today. `packages/quereus-plugin-optimystic/test/offline-mode-invented-collection.spec.ts`.
- A refused segment: excluded from replay, holds no delta and is never committed. A later segment's delta was captured without it, because the refusal happens in a replay. `packages/db-core/test/collection-segments.spec.ts`.
- `dropSealed` of a non-head segment: replays, as today. Same spec.
- Rollback across a commit: `Collection.restorePending` sees the version moved and takes the replay arm. The verbatim arm restores `openDelta` and `openApplied` with the transforms. `packages/quereus-plugin-optimystic/test/savepoint-rollback.spec.ts`.
- Rows staged in the open segment while the head is on the network: the re-fold ends with the open delta. Test, below.
- A block the head inserted and a later segment updates, then deletes, then one that splits: `updates` over a now-committed block, or a `delete`. Test, below.
- A statement staged by several `Collection.act` calls that a savepoint partly rolls back: `openDelta` restored from the capture. `savepoint-rollback.spec.ts`.
- A multi-collection entry (`TransactionCoordinator.commitEntry`), which is the report's shape: one handle per participant, `fold` for all and then `replay` on each. A partial landing keeps the same steps per participant. `packages/db-core/test/coordinator-entry-commit.spec.ts` and `packages/db-p2p/test/offline-queue-mesh.spec.ts`.
- The tally reported when the head leaves (`Collection.reportCommittedHead`), now from staging rather than a rebuild: same value, since the state is the same. `already-applied` cases in `packages/db-core/test/offline-queue.spec.ts`.
- Unlatched reads during the swap: the same contract as the replay. `packages/quereus-plugin-optimystic/test/offline-insert-during-drain.spec.ts`, and `packages/db-core/test/tree-point-read-under-mutation.spec.ts`.
- `Block` size limit and digests: `describeTouchedBlocks` runs on the attempt as before, using pins that retain their block clone.

## Test

One property spec in `packages/db-core/test/` (say `head-commit-without-replay.spec.ts`), since the new branch must behave exactly like the one it replaces. Over a `Tree` with generated inserts, updates and deletes across enough keys to split and merge leaves, sealed into several entries, with rows also staged in the open segment during a head's send:

- after each head commit, every read through the live tree equals what a fresh handle replaying the same remaining segments reads;
- the committed tree, read from storage after the drain, equals the sequential result;
- a run with a rival commit injected mid-drain equals the sequential result with the rival first.
