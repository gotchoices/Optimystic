# `Collection.update()` reads the authoritative revision, then discards it

## What happens today

`Collection.update()` (`updateInternal`) refreshes a collection from the transactor in two steps:

1. It reads the collection's header, then the log's **tail block**, whose stored state carries
   `latest` — the identity and revision of the most recently committed action under this collection
   id. `bootstrapContext` puts that revision onto a **freshly constructed, throwaway**
   `TransactorSource`, whose only job is to let the subsequent chain walk see blocks that are
   committed but not yet folded in.

2. It then walks the log (`Log.getFrom`) starting from the revision the collection *currently*
   holds, and advances the collection's own revision to whatever that walk reports as newest —
   `Collection.advanceContext(this.source, this.id, latest?.context)`.

Step 1's number never reaches `this.source`. If step 2's walk yields a **lower** revision than the
tail already stated, or yields none at all, `advanceContext` quietly declines to move and
`update()` returns normally. The caller is told nothing. In a healthy single-lineage collection the
two numbers agree, so nothing is visibly wrong today.

`advanceContext` already logs `collection:context-not-lowered` for its own guard — a collection
refusing to move *backwards*. There is no counterpart for a collection failing to move *forwards*
past a revision it just read off the tail, which is the more interesting failure.

## Why it matters

This is the exact silence that makes the index-divergence investigation
(`tickets/implement/2-index-trace-cannot-tell-a-forked-collection-from-a-lagging-one`) so hard.
Downstream, a machine's index collection sat one revision behind its sibling, a live scan called
`update()` on it immediately before every read, roughly a hundred times over thirty seconds, and the
revision never moved once. From outside the class that is indistinguishable from "there was nothing
newer to adopt" — and `update()` had, on every one of those hundred calls, read a tail block that
either agreed (in which case the collection is genuinely current and the defect is elsewhere) or
disagreed (in which case the refresh is broken and we would have known immediately). Nobody can say
which, because the number was discarded before anything could compare it.

The same silence is what `tickets/backlog/feat-sync-fail-fast-on-hopeless-revision` describes from
the other side: a write that keeps retrying because "the refresh fails to move it". That ticket
proposes acting on a revision the *server* reports in a rejection. This one is about the revision
the client already read for itself, on the ordinary read path, with no rejection involved. Different
site, different trigger; they do not block each other.

## What to build

Compare, at the end of `updateInternal`, the revision the tail reported against the revision the
collection ended up holding, and make a shortfall visible.

- The tail's revision is already in hand — it is on the local throwaway source after
  `bootstrapContext`. Read it there rather than issuing another fetch. `bootstrapContext` leaves it
  unset when the header has no tail or the tail carries no `latest`; that is a legitimate
  "nothing committed yet" state and must not be reported as a shortfall.
- When the collection's revision after the refresh is **below** the tail's, emit one diagnostic line
  through the existing `db-core:collection` logger naming the collection id, the revision held
  before the refresh, the revision held after, and the revision the tail reported. Follow
  `collection:context-not-lowered`'s naming and shape so the two guards read as a pair.

**Log, do not throw.** A throw here would abort every caller of `update()`, including the
transaction coordinator's blanket refresh of all registered collections between commit retries —
the loud-failure tradeoff already documented at the header-vanished check a few lines above, which
is deliberately scoped to a contradiction that cannot be legitimate. A revision shortfall is not
yet known to be illegitimate, and turning it into an abort would convert an unproven diagnosis into
production behaviour. If a later investigation establishes that a shortfall is always a fault, that
decision can be made then, on evidence, from the line this ticket adds.

Whether to also *adopt* the tail's revision when the walk comes up short is deliberately **out of
scope** and must not be done here: the tail revision and the walk's revision are read through
different paths, and adopting the higher one would paper over precisely the disagreement we want to
see. Report first.

## Verifying it

The healthy path is the important assertion: an ordinary refresh, where the walk and the tail agree,
must emit nothing. A unit test can drive the shortfall directly by constructing a collection whose
held revision, tail state, and log chain are set up to disagree — `packages/db-core/test/collection.spec.ts`
already builds collections against controllable transactor doubles.
