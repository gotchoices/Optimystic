Third of three tickets for stage 2 of [docs/offline-sync.md §Implementation stages](../../docs/offline-sync.md#implementation-stages); it also marks the stage implemented. The design is [§The drain does not hold the collection latch across the network](../../docs/offline-sync.md#the-drain-does-not-hold-the-collection-latch-across-the-network) and [§One entry, one network transaction](../../docs/offline-sync.md#one-entry-one-network-transaction). The segment list, segment-aware replay, the invented baseline and the fold-before-replay order come from `offline-sync-segment-bookkeeping`; the version-aware snapshot from `offline-sync-open-segment-snapshot`.

## The member

```ts
export type HeadCommitOptions = SyncOptions & {
	/** The action id the attempt cycle runs under. The queue (stage 3) mints it, and re-supplies the SAME id when it re-drives an entry whose earlier attempt may have landed (docs/offline-sync.md §Identity across attempts). */
	actionId: ActionId;
	/** The entry's local commit time, stamped on the log entry in place of the time Collection.syncInternal mints for a sync. */
	timestamp: number;
};

/** Commit exactly the head sealed segment as one log entry under the given identity. Returns what sync returns: who holds the commit, or undefined when the refresh established the write was already saved without re-sending. */
commitHead(entryId: EntryId, options: HeadCommitOptions): Promise<WriteDurability | undefined>;
```

Preconditions, each a thrown `SealedSegmentsError` (extend it with a `reason` field: `'not-head'`, `'refused'`, `'in-flight'`): `entryId` must be the first sealed segment; it must not carry a recorded refusal (the queue drops a refused segment, it never commits it); no head commit may already be in flight on this instance.

## The attempt, and what it runs on

A `sync` attempt runs on a per-attempt tracker seeded with a copy of the live tracker's transforms. A head attempt cannot: the live tracker holds every sealed segment and the open one, cumulatively. It runs instead on a **head tracker** — a `Tracker` over `this.sourceCache` with its own `BasePins` (bound to the same cache), into which the head segment's actions alone are staged with `internalTransact`-style atomic staging, over the committed state. Its transforms are the head's transforms over committed state; its pins are the head's bases.

Build it **once per requested revision** and reuse it across attempts while `getNextRev()` is unchanged. A replay of the head's actions mints fresh ids for the blocks it inserts, so rebuilding per attempt would send different content under one `(action id, revision)` — the divergence `retry-resends-identical-transforms.spec.ts` pins and `logAppendBlockIds` exists to close. When the refresh between attempts adopts a newer revision, rebuild (the write is genuinely different at a new revision, as that spec's reasoning says). Before each attempt, re-judge the head tracker's pins the way `restageIfBasesMoved` does for the live queue (`unretainedBases` read, then `movedBases`); a moved base rebuilds the head tracker at the same revision — the same narrow exception the NOTE above `restageIfBasesMoved`'s call in `syncAttempts` already records, and it needs the same NOTE at the head site.

Staging the head's actions runs their guards against the committed state. A refusal there (`ActionRefusedError`) is the head segment's refusal: record it on the segment, bump the version, call `onSealedRefused`, and throw it out of `commitHead` — the drain classifies it as a guard refusal. Equivalent to the live replay refusing the same segment on a refresh, which may already have happened; `sealedRefusal(entryId)` is checked at the top for that.

Each attempt then does what `syncAttempts` does: a per-attempt tracker over the head tracker's transforms (copied) sharing the head tracker's pins, `Log.open` with `logAppendBlockIds(…, actionId, newRev)`, `addActions(head.actions, actionId, newRev, …, { timestamp: options.timestamp })`, `describeTouchedBlocks`, the oversize check, `stagedBaseRevs`, then `source.transact(...)`. The retry accounting is the same too: expiry, shortfall and validity rejection throw by name; a stale failure retains the attempt (`retainInFlightAttempt`), counts against the budget, backs off, refreshes with the stall check and `lastChance`, and loops. **Share it.** Extract the attempt machinery `syncAttempts` and `commitHead` have in common — the per-attempt tracker and log append given a target (actions, base transforms, pins), the failure accounting and stall check, the refresh-until-finished-or-refused loop — into named steps (a new `packages/db-core/src/collection/head-commit.ts` or a sibling module is fine) so the two paths cannot drift. Behaviour of `sync` must not change; the existing retry specs are the safety net. This is the restructuring backlog `debt-collection-write-retry-logic-outgrew-its-file` asks for, done now because a second caller exists; do only as much of it as the two callers need, and note in the handoff what remains of that ticket.

## The latch

`commitHead` takes the collection latch, and releases it around the one `await` on `source.transact` — the network — then retakes it before applying the outcome. Everything else (the precondition checks, the refresh, the head tracker, the log append, the fold, the replay, the inter-attempt refresh and backoff) runs under the latch. Mark the action in flight for the whole cycle (`beginInFlightAction(options.actionId)`), as `sync` does, so the inter-attempt refresh recognises the write's own entry.

While the latch is released, `act`, `sealPending`, `dropSealed` of another segment, `update`, and a snapshot or restore can all run. Two of them need the collection to know a head commit is in flight; keep `{ entryId, actionId }` in a field set just before the release and cleared right after the re-acquire:

- **A refresh (`updateInternal`) that walks and finds an entry under the in-flight action id** must not treat it as the write's own entry to finish (the attempt is not failed, nothing is retained, and `completeOwnEntry` would cancel the pending records of a write still in flight), and must not treat it as a stranger's (the replay would re-stage the head over committed state that already contains it, double-applying unguarded actions and refusing guarded ones). It cannot adopt a revision past that entry without judging it. So it **returns early, before `forgetAndAdopt`**, changing nothing (log `collection:refresh-deferred-to-head-commit`); the outcome is applied by `commitHead` when it retakes the latch, and the next refresh finds nothing new. The reader is served the state before this collection's own write, which it sees staged anyway. Put the check where `ownEntry` is decided, ahead of `completeOwnEntry`, and gate it on the in-flight field, not on `inFlightActionId` alone — `commitHead`'s own refresh runs with the field cleared and must take the own-entry path.
- **`dropSealed` of the in-flight head** throws (`reason: 'in-flight'`). Dropping another segment is allowed: it replays the live tracker, which the head tracker does not depend on.

A refresh that finds entries but NOT the in-flight one (a rival landed first; the attempt will be refused as stale) proceeds normally and may record the head as refused during its replay. `commitHead` therefore re-checks `sealedRefusal(entryId)` after every re-acquire and after its own inter-attempt refresh, and throws the recorded refusal instead of making another attempt.

## Applying the outcome

On success, after re-acquiring the latch, in this order: assert the head is still the first sealed segment (nothing else may remove it while in flight — the refresh defers and `dropSealed` refuses, so a failed assertion is a defect, throw); remove it and bump the version; fold the head tracker's transforms into the cache (`sourceCache.transformCache(headTracker.transforms, newRev)`); adopt the revision (`committed: [...committed, { actionId, rev: newRev }]`); THEN `replayActions()` — remaining sealed segments and the open segment over the committed state, which now holds the head. This is the fold-before-replay order the first ticket made `syncAttempts` take; a replay first would re-read at the superseded revision over a cache that has not seen the commit. Return the attempt's durability.

On a stale failure the own-entry logic needs one more arm. `consumeOwnEntry` today drops the leading actions of the open segment; when the in-flight write is a head commit the entry's actions are the head segment's, so consume = remove the head segment (assert `entry.actions.length` equals its action count, else throw as the open arm does) and bump the version. `completeOwnEntry`'s re-send uses the retained attempt verbatim and is unchanged. After a refresh that finished and consumed the entry, `commitHead` returns the refresh's durability as `sync` does.

## Edge cases & interactions

- **Byte-identical retries.** Add a `commitHead` case to `retry-resends-identical-transforms.spec.ts` over `RecordsAttemptsRefusesFirstCommit`, with the head segment inserting a fresh block: the two attempts' transforms are deep-equal, the action id is the supplied one, and the log entry's timestamp is the supplied one. Also the overflow variant if the shared machinery changed how `logAppendBlockIds` is reached; otherwise one case.
- **Staging into the open segment while the head commit is in flight.** Use `GatedCommitTransactor` (commit parks after landing): start `commitHead`, await `commitParked`, `act` an open action (it must not block — the latch is released), open the gate, await the commit; assert the committed log holds the head alone, the open action is still staged, and a read sees both. Test — this is the scenario the released latch exists for.
- **A sealed segment behind the head while in flight.** Same harness with E2 sealed before the commit: after success E2 is the head, its entries are visible, `sealedEntries()` is `[E2]`. Test.
- **A refresh that adopts a rival's revision while in flight.** With `TailLandsButReportsStale` or a parked commit: a reader's `update()` during the window either defers (the rival's entry is behind ours — the in-flight entry is in the walk) or proceeds (the rival landed first); in the second shape the attempt is refused stale, the refresh in `commitHead` adopts the rival, the head tracker is rebuilt at the new revision and the retry commits. Assert the final log order and that a reader sees both writes. Test the second shape; the first is covered by the next bullet.
- **A reader's refresh finding the in-flight entry.** Park the commit after landing (`GatedCommitTransactor`), run `update()` from the same handle: it returns without adopting (held revision unchanged, head still sealed, no refusal recorded), and after the gate opens and `commitHead` returns, the next `update()` finds nothing new. Test.
- **A head commit that fails after staging continued.** `TailLandsButReportsStale` with an open action staged during the window: the inter-attempt refresh finds the own entry, finishes it from the retained attempt, consumes the HEAD segment (not the open action), and `commitHead` returns the refresh's durability; the open action is still staged. Test — this is the contract on the new `consumeOwnEntry` arm.
- **Head refused on rebase before send.** Rival takes the head's key; `commitHead` throws the `TreeKeyTakenError`, the segment is marked refused, the callback fired once (whether the live replay or the head tracker's staging found it first), nothing was sent. Test.
- **Head refused by a rival that landed while in flight.** The attempt is refused stale; the refresh adopts the rival; the live replay refuses the head; `commitHead` throws the recorded refusal rather than retrying. Test.
- **`dropSealed` of the in-flight head** throws; of a later segment succeeds. Test the first; the second by inspection.
- **Snapshot and restore across a head commit** (the double-commit scenario of the doc's test plan, now with the commit): seal E, snapshot, stage an open action, `commitHead(E)` to completion, `restorePending`: the restore takes the replay arm (version bumped), E is not re-staged, the committed log holds E once, the open action is gone. Test.
- **Invented collection with sealed segments, head committed.** `createOrOpen` fresh, seal E1 and E2, `commitHead(E1)`: the committed collection holds the header, root and E1; E2 is re-staged over it and readable; the baseline is no longer consulted (context defined). Test.
- **In-flight id and own-entry completion when the retrying segment is the head and others are sealed behind it.** Covered by the "fails after staging continued" and "sealed behind the head" tests together; add the assertion that E2's actions were never consumed.
- **Expiry, shortfall, validity rejection** out of a head attempt throw by name and leave every segment staged. One test for one of them (shortfall, via a double returning `isShortfallFailure` shape); the others share the code path by construction once the machinery is shared — say so.
- **Abort and deadline.** `SyncOptions.signal` and `deadlineMs` apply unchanged through the shared loop. By inspection.
- **Two `commitHead` calls at once on one instance.** The second waits on the latch, then fails the precondition (the first's head is gone, or a head commit is in flight if the first released the latch). By inspection; one assertion in the gated test.
- **`sync` after sealed segments are all committed** behaves exactly as today: the version is irrelevant to `sync`, and `SealedSegmentsError` is thrown only while a segment is sealed. By the existing suites.
