# A lineage divergence drops the read cache

## Why this is its own ticket

`Collection.advanceContext` in `packages/db-core/src/collection/collection.ts` is the one place two copies of a collection's history meet: the handle's held context and the context a refresh just read. When the two name different actions at a shared revision they are different lineages, and the class doc records a parked decision: the seam deliberately changed no behaviour, and if the `collection:lineage-divergence` line was ever seen in the field, someone should decide then whether a divergence should also drop the read cache, and whether to keep re-reporting per refresh.

It has been seen. A downstream project (sereus) captured it on 2026-08-30 on a secondary-index collection written from two machines:

```
collection:lineage-divergence id=default/FormationUsage/index/FormationUsageByToken rev=1 held=63cJ50MoBCietBFJBvxWeQ read=vPYEKDif1s1ItefiE-5DQw
collection:lineage-divergence id=default/FormationUsage/index/FormationUsageByToken rev=2 held=W0vdcSKMif20MB6UYylCjQ read=NEJ50gSY7mr9KGV5QUKQDw
collection:context-not-lowered id=default/FormationUsage/index/FormationUsageByToken held=4 read=3
```

The partition-healing plan pass (2026-11-01) split the parked decision in two. The half this ticket owns is the one that stands alone: a divergence the refresh **adopts**, because the read's revision is at or above the held one. The other half, a handle that holds a higher revision on a lineage the network does not hold (the `context-not-lowered` line above, which refuses to lower and so seals the fork), is the reconciliation question and belongs to backlog `feat-replay-mode-collections-merge-divergent-histories`. This ticket changes nothing on that path.

## The decision

**On an adopted divergence, drop the whole read cache and replay every staged action. Report it once, at the refresh that discovered it.**

Why the whole cache, not the blocks the walked entries name: a refresh walks only the log entries above the held revision. The blocks the old lineage built at and below that revision were named by entries this handle will never walk, so `forgetAndAdopt`'s per-entry clear cannot reach them, the cache has no expiry, and an idle refresh stops after one request when the log has not moved. Nothing would ever clear them. The handle has just learned its cache describes a history the network does not hold, which is the "old content nothing will ever clear" trap that `docs/internals.md` §A block re-read after a refresh can be answered too old describes, reached by another door.

Why replay: the staged actions were computed against old-lineage content, and `mustReplay` fires today only on a conflict, a revision advance, or a moved base. A fork at the **same** revision (held 2 on lineage A, read 2 on lineage B; equal revisions adopt) advances nothing, so without a fourth reason the staged actions would be pended over blocks the handle no longer believes in.

Why once: adopting `next` overwrites the held lineage marker, and with the cache cleared the handle is consistent with the log, so the next refresh compares log to log and has nothing to report. Re-reporting would mean retaining the old marker for no consumer. The `context-not-lowered` line keeps re-reporting on every refresh as it does today; that is the signature of a sealed fork, and an operator meeting one should keep seeing it.

Floors raised under the old lineage are not knowledge about the new one. A floor says "this block is at least revision r"; on the new lineage the block may legitimately sit below r, and a standing floor would then make every read of it a below-floor answer for the life of the handle: never cached, re-asked of a second coordinator each time, logged `collection:block-below-floor`. So the floors are reset on an adopted divergence, before this refresh's walk raises the new lineage's floors for the entries it names.

## Mechanics

- **Detection becomes behaviour, so it runs unconditionally.** Today the comparison is gated on `log.enabled`. Keep `earliestFork` gated (it exists to print `forkRev=`) and detect with one comparison: `actionIdAt(next, current.rev)` against `actionIdAt(current, current.rev)`, a divergence when both are defined and differ. This is sufficient because action ids are unique per commit: two lineages that parted at or below the held revision name different actions at every revision both have reached from the fork onward. The one exception, the same action landing on both sides (a writer's torn retry), is the same lineage at that revision, which is the right answer. `actionIdAt` is linear in the uncheckpointed `committed` list and its `NOTE:` asks a non-diagnostic caller to search from the end; the held side's entry is normally the last one. Two scans per walking refresh, never per read. Leave a `NOTE:` tying the cost to backlog `debt-the-collection-log-never-writes-a-checkpoint`, since the list only truncates at checkpoints.
- **The verdict is computed before the walk's clears, and the whole step stays await-free.** `forgetAndAdopt` raises floors inside its per-entry loop before it calls `advanceContext`, so a reset after adoption would discard this walk's floors. Split the judgement from the adoption (a static helper returning whether the read is a different lineage and whether it will be adopted, then the adopt), and in `forgetAndAdopt`: on a forked-and-adopted verdict, `sourceCache.clear()` with no ids and reset the floors first, then the existing loop, then adopt, then `keepLogEnds` (the header and tail it read came from the new lineage and may be kept). No `await` between any of these: the invariant in `docs/internals.md` §The forgetting and the adopting are one synchronous step is what makes a concurrent unlatched read either land before and be forgotten with the rest, or land after and be judged under the adopted lineage.
- **`mustReplay` gets a fourth reason**: the refresh adopted a divergent lineage. Pass the verdict in beside `anyConflicts` and `priorContext`. A whole-cache clear also makes every pinned base read as moved, so the third reason would usually fire too; the explicit reason keeps the rule readable and covers a handle with pins on nothing.
- **The `attach` site is unchanged.** `attachToLog` compares the tail block's claimed latest action against a walk of that tail's own chain on a fresh handle. There is no cache to drop and no staged action; it keeps logging only.
- **Docs.** The class doc's parked `NOTE:` is replaced by the decision above. `docs/internals.md` §The revision context is monotonic says the line "only logs — adoption proceeds unchanged"; say what now happens on an adopted divergence and that the not-lowered case is unchanged and owned by backlog `feat-replay-mode-collections-merge-divergent-histories`. `docs/debugging.md` §Did the refresh itself fail to close the gap? describes reading the line; check its wording still holds once the line fires once per discovery with a cache drop behind it.

## Edge cases & interactions

- **Same-revision fork** (held 2, read 2, different actions): adopted, since equal revisions adopt. Must clear and replay. Verified by the test below.
- **Fork below the held revision, read above it** (held 2 on A, read 3 on B, parted at 1): adopted; clear, floors reset then re-raised by this walk's entries; replay. By inspection of the ordering in `forgetAndAdopt`.
- **Held above the read on a different lineage** (the sereus `held=4 read=3` line): `context-not-lowered` refuses, nothing is cleared, behaviour unchanged. The sealed fork is backlog `feat-replay-mode-collections-merge-divergent-histories`'s. By inspection: the forked verdict must not trigger a clear when adoption is refused.
- **Sealed segments** (offline queue): the replay re-stages every non-refused sealed segment and then the open one, exactly as a refresh that adopted a newer revision does; a segment the committed state now refuses is recorded and reported through `onSealedRefused`. No new path.
- **Head commit in flight**: a refresh that walks into this handle's own in-flight entry returns before `forgetAndAdopt` (`collection:refresh-deferred-to-head-commit`) and so never reaches the verdict. Unchanged.
- **Pinned read views**: a committed read's private `CacheSource` was seeded by cloning the shared cache when the view was built and is not touched by the clear; that is snapshot semantics working as intended. A view built after the clear seeds from an empty cache and reads at the adopted boundary.
- **Pinned bases of staged updates**: after the clear, `Tracker.revalidatePin` finds no cached revision for any pinned id and marks every base moved; the replay re-stages and each re-staged update pins the base it is computed against. `restageIfBasesMoved` before the next attempt then finds nothing moved. By inspection.
- **No comparable revision**: `actionIdAt` answers `undefined` on either side when the held revision's slot is a checkpoint or invalidation entry, or lies before the read side's most recent checkpoint. No divergence is detected and nothing changes; a fork invisible there stays invisible, which is today's behaviour and acceptable (no checkpoint is written today, see the debt ticket above).
- **An invented collection** holds no context and never reaches the comparison. Unchanged.
- **Logging off**: the clear and replay happen whether or not the `collection` debug namespace has a sink; only `forkRev=` on the line depends on `earliestFork`.

## Tests

One test, in the existing describe in `packages/db-core/test/collection.spec.ts`: arm the `rewrittenLineageTransactor` so a handle opens holding revision N under the rewritten lineage, read one data block through the handle so the cache holds it, extend the fixture so that while disarmed the transactor also serves **different content** for that block (the stored lineage's), refresh, and assert that a read of the block now returns the stored content. Expected before the change: the old content, served from the cache. The same test asserts the `collection:lineage-divergence` line fired exactly once across that refresh and the next. The replay reason needs no separate test: stage one action before the refresh and assert, in the same test, that after the refresh the staged block's base pin names the adopted lineage's revision. Do not add a test for the not-lowered path; nothing there changes.
