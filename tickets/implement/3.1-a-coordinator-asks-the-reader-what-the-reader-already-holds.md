# A coordinator asks the reader what the reader already holds

## What was observed

Same measurement as `a-read-of-several-blocks-consults-the-cohort-one-block-at-a-time` (sereus re-attach scenario, 900 ms one-way, two-machine group, optimystic build `ce5a0068`, trace `tickets/.logs/catch-up-stale-empty.log`). In the arm where the re-attaching machine B starts from an empty store, B sends its block reads to A as coordinator, and A's read-repair consults the rest of the block's cohort, which is B alone. One read of `default/strand/Header`:

- B's `get blockIds=1` goes out; A logs `read-repair-triggered` for the block.
- B's sync service logs `Received sync request for block default/strand/Header` at 16:10:33.763: A asking B what B holds.
- A logs `cluster-fetch:certified-selected … claimants: 1` and `cluster-fetch:local-current` at 16:10:34.677.
- B logs `get:done blockIds=1 ms=7285`.

So a read B routes to A costs 7.3 s: 3.64 s for B's request and A's reply (stream open plus request, two round trips) and 3.64 s for A's consult of B (the same two round trips again). The consult tells A only what B already knew when it sent the request. Every read B routed to A in that trace has this shape (`ms=7285`, `ms=7317`). A read B coordinates itself costs one consult, 3.64 s.

Why B routes to A at all in that arm is inferred, not traced (the key-network log namespace was not enabled): a node whose first connection is younger than `gracePeriodMs` (30 s) may not coordinate for itself while a connection is up (`shouldAllowSelfCoordination` → `grace-period-not-elapsed`, `packages/db-p2p/src/libp2p-key-network.ts`), so `findCoordinator` picks A and caches the pick. A freshly started or freshly joined machine is exactly the one catching up, so this is the common case, not a corner. Changing that guard is a separate, safety-motivated policy and is not proposed here.

## The rule to implement

The consult asks each other cohort member "what do you hold for this block?". When the asker is itself a cohort member, it can answer that question in the request. So:

- **Reader side.** When a read goes to a remote coordinator, the request carries, per requested block, what the reader holds locally: its committed `latest` `(rev, actionId)`, or an explicit "holds nothing". Read it from the local store only (no consult, no promotion side effects). Blocks the reader has no local answer for are omitted. A reader that sends to itself sends nothing extra.
- **The asker's identity is the connection's, never the payload's.** `RepoService` passes the authenticated `connection.remotePeer` to `CoordinatorRepo.get` as a server-side option. A payload field naming a peer id must not exist, so a request cannot speak for another member.
- **Coordinator side.** In `queryClusterForLatest`, for a cohort peer equal to the authenticated asker whose stated holding for this block is present, use that holding as the peer's answer instead of calling `clusterLatestCallback`: a stated `(rev, actionId)` counts as that peer's claim, "holds nothing" counts as the peer answering absent (the resolved-`undefined` case of the three-way contract). The asker then counts as *answered* for every downstream rule, including the unsettled-claim weakening rule in `recordAheadClaim`.
- **Only when it saves a consult without losing anything.** Substitute only when the stated holding is "nothing", or its revision is at or below the coordinator's own local revision (at equal revision, only with the same `actionId`). A stated holding *ahead* of the coordinator, or a same-revision different-action holding, is consulted exactly as today: the coordinator will need the asker's archive and commit proof to acquire or to judge it, and a stated revision carries neither.
- **No new trust.** A stated holding is uncertified, and it can only lower what the coordinator concludes about being behind: it never makes the coordinator adopt anything. A member that lies in its request could tell the same lie in a consult answer today.
- **Old peers.** A coordinator that predates the field ignores it and consults as before; a reader that predates it sends nothing and gets today's behaviour.

In a two-member cohort with the coordinator current, this removes the consult entirely: the read costs one request and reply, the same as a read the reader coordinates itself. The new-collection probe (`createOrOpen` against an absent header) through a remote coordinator also drops to one request when the reader states "holds nothing", and stays authoritative, because the whole cohort (the asker) has answered. In larger cohorts the consult still runs for the other members in parallel, so latency is unchanged and one stream per read is saved.

## Test

One reproduction test at the `CoordinatorRepo` layer: a two-member cohort (self plus peer P), a present local block, a `get` from asker P whose stated holding is at or below the local revision. Assert the read-repair pass completes with `clusterLatestCallback` never invoked for P and the answer unflagged; and that a stated holding ahead of the local revision still invokes it. If the reader-side attach is a small pure function over the local store, it needs no separate test beyond the mesh suite exercising it.
