# A full cohort member refuses a pend

## Why

A pend stages a write in each cohort member's storage. A member with no room left has no way to say so: `ClusterMember.validatePendOperations` judges the transaction's content, its revision and the reservations held against its blocks, and a member that finds nothing wrong approves. The space it does not have is checked nowhere. So a full member promises to apply, approves, and then either fails at apply — where its refusal is an unsigned `applyOutcomes` report, after consensus — or fills its disk.

The same place already has the machinery for this, built twice, which makes the shape of the answer clear rather than open. `docs/correctness.md` Theorem 1 Case 2 has the table: two refusals are *transient*, each gets its own non-counting vote kind, and each names only what its own source holds — `conflict` for a rival the member holds whole, `held` for a rival its storage knows only by action id. Both are counted apart from approvals and rejections at every threshold, member-side and coordinator-side, and the reason is recorded as a live defect rather than a tidiness question: `maxAllowedRejections` is zero for any cohort of three or fewer at the default threshold, so one member's transient "no" became a permanent `ValidatorRejectionError` for the whole transaction. "I am full" is a third transient refusal, and collapsing it into `reject` would reproduce that defect exactly.

## What to build

### The vote

A new `Signature` variant in `packages/db-core/src/cluster/structs.ts`: `{ type: 'out-of-space'; signature: string }`.

**Named `out-of-space`, not `full`,** because `full` already means something else one layer away — `WriteDurability`'s `'full'` is "every cohort member confirmed", the opposite end of the same subject.

**It carries no payload**, and that is a decision rather than an omission. A byte figure would be: unverifiable, since nothing the coordinator can check binds a member's claim about its own disk to anything; a publication of an operator's disk to every writer that pends; and unread, since the only thing a writer does with the refusal is stop expecting this member to take data now. So the vote's type is its whole message, as `approve`'s is.

That also keeps the signed preimage in the existing bare-tag form, `<hash>:out-of-space`, which `voteTagAndExtra` already produces for a variant with no extra field. Check the ambiguity argument the function's doc makes holds for the new tag — it does: a hash is base64url so the first `:` always ends it, and `out-of-space` differs from `approve`, `reject`, `conflict` and `held` at its first character. Extend that doc to name the bare-tag form as the shape `approve` and `out-of-space` share, rather than leaving the new tag to fall through a `default` branch documented as "any type off the wire this code does not know".

### The member

`PromiseVerdict` in `packages/db-p2p/src/cluster/cluster-repo.ts` gains a fourth kind, `out-of-space`; `voteFor` maps it to the new vote; `refusalLogTag` gains its line, so an operator reads four different events.

**The check goes last among the refusals.** A member that is full still reads and re-executes the transaction, because reading costs no storage, and a record that is genuinely invalid or genuinely expired must still get the permanent, structured answer that tells the writer not to retry it — a transient "I am full" in its place would send the writer round a loop against content no honest member will ever accept. A `held` refusal also wins, because it names an actionable rival that clears when that rival commits or cancels. So `out-of-space` is the answer only for a record this member would otherwise have **approved**, and that ordering is the ticket's one real correctness requirement at the member.

The gate reads `StoragePressure.stage()` — the previous ticket's synchronous last sample — at the `refusing` stage. Never a scan: a promise round is on the inbound path of a consensus round, and a directory walk inside it would cost the member its vote to a timeout.

**Member-side counting**: add `'out-of-space'` to the `retryableRefusals` filter in `getTransactionPhase`, beside `conflict` and `held`. Enough of them still make super-majority unreachable, which is the terminal-but-retryable outcome that clears the member's reservation so the record does not wedge the blocks against the retry meant to replace it — the same reasoning that put `held` there. It must **not** join `rejectedPromises`: nobody judged the write wrong. The phase it yields is still called `ConflictSuperseded`, which now covers three kinds of refusal rather than one; widen the comment at the enum to say the phase means "terminal for this record, retryable as a fresh one", and leave the name alone (renaming it reaches further than this ticket earns).

### The coordinator

In `packages/db-p2p/src/repo/cluster-coordinator.ts`, a branch for an out-of-space-answered shortfall, placed **after** the conflict and held branches — a named rival is strictly more actionable than "a member is full" — and **before** the generic shortfall, which must stay reserved for the genuinely silent cohort. It throws `CohortOutOfSpaceError` naming the refusing peers, the approvals it got and the super-majority it needed, and it gates its abandonment broadcast on the same `refusalsProveUnreachable` test the two existing branches use.

Only a pend can produce it — `out-of-space` votes come from `validatePendOperations`, which inspects pend operations only — so `CoordinatorRepo.commit` never meets one, exactly as `BlocksHeldError` documents for itself.

### The answer the writer gets

A new failure shape in `packages/db-core/src/network/struct.ts`, modelled field for field on `ShortfallFailure`, its nearest sibling: `OutOfSpaceFailure` with `conflict: false`, an `outOfSpace` payload naming the refusing peers, every other failure field typed `never`, added to the `WriteFailure` union; `isOutOfSpaceFailure` in `packages/db-core/src/network/stale-failure.ts`; and a `CohortOutOfSpaceError` built from it, wired where `PromiseShortfallError` is wired in `packages/db-core/src/transaction/coordinator.ts`.

`CoordinatorRepo.pend` **returns** it rather than letting the error escape, for the reason `ConflictRaceLostError`'s doc gives about thrown errors: db-core retries a throw verbatim as a transport fault, and a retry cannot help here — nothing about the revision is the problem, so a re-read and rebase sends the same bytes to the same full members. `conflict: false` and no `missing`/`pending` means `isConflictFailure` answers false, so the retry loop does not absorb it, and the writer gets the typed terminal answer the parent ticket asks for instead of a stale-revision loop.

**A cohort of one casts no vote**, so the solo arm needs its own gate: when the resolved cohort is this node alone and its pressure stage is `refusing`, `CoordinatorRepo.pend` answers the same `OutOfSpaceFailure` directly. This mirrors how the validity path already reaches a cohort of one — through the coordinating node's own storage-tier check rather than a vote (`docs/correctness.md`'s note on `ValidityRefusal.peerId`). Without it, a full single-member deployment accepts writes from client nodes until its disk fails; the host-gate sibling ticket covers only writes originating on the node itself.

### Two things this deliberately does not do

**A member that approved a pend honours the commit, whatever its space has done since.** The refusal is at the gate or nowhere: an approve is a promise to apply, and a member that broke it after consensus would strand a decided transaction that every other member is applying. The headroom between `refuseAbove` and the allotment is precisely what makes that promise keepable — it is sized for pends already approved.

**A full cohort is not re-placed.** The parent ticket's expected behaviour says a writer "gets its write placed on another member, or a typed answer saying the cohort is out of space", and for blocks the second is the only one available: a block's cohort *is* its replica set, derived from the block's own key through `findCluster`, so there is no other member to ask. Re-placement is a blob question (the blob cohort has a choice of holders, which backlog `feat-cohort-selection-owner-aware-placement` is about), not a block one. When every member of a block's cohort is full, the honest answer is the typed failure with every peer named, and what fixes it is an operator adding space or the cohort's nodes shedding to peers that have it. Write that down in `docs/correctness.md` rather than leaving a reader to infer that re-placement was forgotten.

## Edge cases & interactions

- **A cohort of three at the default threshold.** `superMajority` is 3 and `maxAllowedRejections` is 0, so one full member blocks every write to that slice. That is the defect `held` exists to avoid reproducing — and it is avoided here in the sense that matters (the writer gets a typed, non-permanent, non-looping answer rather than a validity verdict), not in the sense that the write succeeds. It cannot succeed: two members cannot meet a bar of three. The sibling steps are what resolve it — the full member sheds, and the cohort reforms around peers with room.
- **An older member meeting the new vote.** A build that does not know `out-of-space` verifies the signature correctly (the preimage is the bare tag, which its `voteTagAndExtra` default already produces) and then counts it toward nothing: not an approval, not a rejection, not a retryable refusal. The record then neither decides nor dies on that member until its promise timeout. Acceptable under the repository's current rule that backwards compatibility is not yet a constraint — but say it in the ticket's handoff so a reviewer is not left to discover it, and do not add a compatibility shim for it.
- **Equivocation.** `equivocationKey` keys on the vote type (plus a reject's validity marker), so a member that voted out-of-space and later approves is caught as a type flip exactly like any other, and the first-seen vote is kept. No change needed — verify by reading the function.
- **A late out-of-space vote.** `docs/correctness.md`'s "a vote that arrives after the round was decided is not counted" covers the new kind with no change: the promise map is frozen once approvals reach super-majority, and a late refusal of any kind is logged and dropped. Add the kind to that paragraph's list.
- **The vote and the slot hold.** A member holding a slot for an aged writer answers `held`; a full member answers `out-of-space`. The ordering above means a slot hold wins, which is right: the hold exists to let a starving writer through, and a full member that refuses it for space has not changed its mind about the hold.
- **Reputation and penalties.** An out-of-space vote is an honest, signed answer and must not be penalized anywhere a `conflict` or `held` vote is not. Check the coordinator's penalty classification reads vote types rather than "not an approve".
- **The stage is up to a sample old.** A member can approve a pend in the window after it crossed the limit, which is the same bounded staleness every gate in this chain accepts, and which the headroom covers.
- **An unmeasurable member never refuses.** `stage()` answers `unknown`, the gate does nothing, and a deployment with no allotment on a memory, SQLite or LevelDB store votes exactly as it does today.

## Tests

One spec: **a single full member in a cohort of three does not turn the write into a validator rejection.** Drive a pend at a cohort where one member's pressure stage is `refusing` and the other two approve; assert the member signs `out-of-space`, that the member-side phase is the terminal-but-retryable one rather than `Rejected`, and that the coordinator's answer is the out-of-space failure rather than a `ValidatorRejectionError` or a conflict-shaped `StaleFailure`. That is the whole of the branching this ticket adds, and it is the exact defect class the `held` vote was created for — which is the strongest evidence that it is the one test worth having here.

Nothing else: the vote's preimage is covered by the existing `clusterVoteSigningPayload` spec's shape if the new tag is added to it (one case, not a new spec), and the failure shape, the classifier and the error class are types plus one field.
