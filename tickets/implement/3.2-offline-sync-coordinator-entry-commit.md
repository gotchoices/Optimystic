Second of four tickets for stage 3 of [docs/offline-sync.md §Implementation stages](../../docs/offline-sync.md#implementation-stages). `TransactionCoordinator` gains `commitEntry`: the per-entry slice commit of [§One entry, one network transaction](../../docs/offline-sync.md#one-entry-one-network-transaction), built over the `HeadCommitHandle` the previous ticket extracted. The queue (`offline-sync-queue-core`) is its only caller; an entry that wrote one collection uses `Collection.commitHead` instead.

## What it does

```typescript
export type EntryCommit = {
	entryId: EntryId;
	actionId: ActionId;
	/** The entry's local commit time: every participant's log entry carries it (`WriteIdentity`). */
	timestamp: number;
	/** The collections the entry wrote, each of which must hold `entryId` as its HEAD sealed segment. */
	collectionIds: readonly CollectionId[];
	/** Attempts an earlier `commitEntry` under this action id recorded through `recordAttempt`
	 * and did not resolve, per collection (see `HeadCommitOptions.resume`). */
	resume?: ReadonlyMap<CollectionId, InFlightAttempt>;
	/** Write-ahead hook, awaited with every participant's attempt before any latch is released
	 * (see `HeadCommitOptions.recordAttempt`). */
	recordAttempt?: (attempts: ReadonlyMap<CollectionId, InFlightAttempt>) => Promise<void>;
};

commitEntry(entry: EntryCommit, options?: SyncOptions): Promise<TransactionDurability | undefined>
```

It answers what `commit` answers: who holds each collection it wrote, across every attempt, or `undefined` when the opening refresh found every participant already saved under `actionId` and sent nothing. It throws what `commit` throws, plus the recorded refusal of any participant's head (an `ActionRefusedError`) and `SealedSegmentsError` from `openHeadCommit`'s preconditions.

**It requires `pendValidation: 'none'`** and throws before touching anything otherwise: a queued entry has no statements for a member to re-execute, and the pend carries bare transforms exactly as the plugin's `commitBatchLegacy` sends them. The queue builds one coordinator per submission over exactly the entry's collections, as `commitBatchLegacy` does.

## The cycle

The loop is `commitAttempts` + `commitOnceLatched` re-expressed over handles, with these differences, each deliberate:

1. **Open a handle per participant** (`Collection.openHeadCommit(entryId, { actionId, timestamp }, entry.resume?.get(id))`) in **sorted collection-id order** — the acquisition order `commitOnce` uses, so a concurrent `commit` over overlapping collections cannot deadlock. The handles are held for the whole call and closed in a `finally`, in reverse order. A participant missing from the coordinator's map is a defect (throw before opening any).
2. **Opening refresh**, like `commitHead`'s: refresh every participant through its handle with a `RefreshReport`; a participant whose refresh finished its own entry has had its head consumed by the handle (`consumeHead`) and is recorded in the saved set with its durability, then closed and dropped from the participant set. A `completion-refused` `TornActionError` is retried as a refresh round against the same budget, with `lastChance` on the round that would spend it, exactly as `commitAttempts` does today. Every participant is visited even after one throws (`visitEveryThenThrow`).
3. **Attempt**: `handle.stage()` on each remaining participant (its head tracker, its log append at its own next revision, under `actionId` and the entry's timestamp — `logAppendBlockIds` applies because the mark is set), giving per-participant transforms, log tail and revision. Then `entry.recordAttempt` with every participant's `retainedAttempt(staged)`, awaited; a throw aborts before any send (handles closed, nothing changed). Then `release()` every handle, and run the existing phases: `declareTouchedBlocks`, `gatherPhase`, `pendPhase`, `commitPhase`, `cancelPhase`. Then `retake()` every handle in sorted order, inside a `finally`.
4. **Outcome**, under the retaken latches:
   - Success: `fold(staged)` on every participant in one await-free pass, then `replay()` on each. Return the saved set merged with the attempt's durability per collection (`writtenDurability`).
   - A partial landing that is retried forward (`staleLoss` with a non-empty committed set): `fold` + `replay` the committed participants and record them as saved; `refused(staged)` on the rest; continue the loop with only the remaining participants, as `commitOnceLatched` does through `CoordinatorStaleLossError`. The committed participants' handles are closed.
   - A partial landing on a hard failure: same local treatment, then throw `CoordinatorPartialCommitError` naming committed and failed collections, with the failed ones still sealed and their attempts retained.
   - A clean stale loss: `refused(staged)` on every participant; the refresh round through the handles; next attempt.
   - A validity rejection, expiry, shortfall or too-large: `refused(staged)` on every participant (harmless retention), throw by name.
   - Once anything is saved, every later failure is rethrown through `reportSaved` as a `CoordinatorPartialCommitError`, as `commit` does.
5. **The head's recorded refusal** is re-checked where `commitHead` re-checks it: `stage()` before every attempt and `refused()` after every refused send, both of which throw it. A refusal escaping this way is thrown out of `commitEntry` with every other participant's segment intact (nothing was sent, or the send was refused).

What the slice does **not** do, and why: no pre-commit snapshots or `restorePending` (the live tracker is never touched by an attempt; a failed attempt leaves it as it was by construction); no `getPendingActions`, `clearPendingActions` or `tracker.reset` (the head segment is dropped and folded through the handle); no `stampData` (there is no stamp — the one-open-stamp rule does not apply to a slice, and a slice may run while a stamp is open on another coordinator over the same collections, which is the plugin's open SQL transaction; the collection latch is what serialises them). The refresh between attempts refreshes the entry's participants only, never the coordinator's whole map (the `NOTE:` on `refreshBetweenAttempts` names that cost).

## Identity instead of a Transaction

The phases read four things off their `Transaction`: `id` (the action id), `priority`, `reads` (recorded on the log entry) and `stamp.timestamp`; the expiry check (`isTransactionExpired`) runs outside them in `commitOnce` and `execute`. Introduce a small `WriteContext = { actionId, timestamp, reads, priority }` in `packages/db-core/src/transaction/entry-commit.ts` and make `applyActionsToCollection`, `coordinateTransaction`, `pendPhase`, `pendRequestFor`, `pendTogether`, `pendCollection` and whatever else reads those fields take it; `commit` and `execute` derive one from their `Transaction` (priority re-read per attempt, since `commitAttempts` ages it). `commitEntry` builds its own: `reads: []`, priority from its own loss count, clamped by `clampPriority`. Do not fabricate a `TransactionStamp` for the slice: a stamp carries a peer id, a schema hash and an expiry that would all be lies.

## Edge cases & interactions

- **A refusal at pend of one participant leaves nothing durable** (the pend-all-then-commit ordering): a rival commits a row to collection B before the drain; `commitEntry` over A and B is refused at B's pend, the refresh adopts the rival, B's replay refuses the head, and the refusal is thrown: A's log holds nothing under the action id, both segments stay sealed, A's head tracker was never sent. **Test.**
- **Both participants commit as one action**: each log holds one entry under `actionId` with the entry's timestamp; a segment sealed behind the head in one collection is re-staged over the committed state; the open segment survives. **Test** (assert on the entries read through a fresh handle over the unwrapped transactor, as `committedKeys` does in `collection-segments.spec.ts`).
- **The latches are released during the round trip**: with the commit parked (`GatedCommitTransactor` over both collections), `act` on A resolves while the commit is parked and the action is in A's open segment after the fold. **Test.**
- **Partial landing on a hard failure, then finished by a re-drive**: a double that commits A and throws on B's commit (the one-round path falls back and B is refused; or `RecordsAttemptsRefusesFirstCommit` shaped over B only); `commitEntry` throws `CoordinatorPartialCommitError` with A committed and B failed; A's head is consumed, B's head still sealed and first; a second `commitEntry` over `[B]` alone under the same action id lands B, and each log holds the entry once. **Test.**
- **A recorded attempt resumes a torn participant**: `TailLandsButReportsStale` over A with `maxAttempts: 1`; the first `commitEntry` throws; `recordAttempt` captured A's attempt; a second `commitEntry` with `resume` finishes A on its opening refresh and commits B, answering a durability with both collections. **Test** — the coordinator-path twin of the previous ticket's case.
- **`reportSaved` after a refresh saved a participant**: the opening refresh of a re-drive saves A, then B's pend is refused by a validity rejection — thrown as `CoordinatorPartialCommitError` with A committed. By inspection: it reuses `reportSaved` unchanged.
- **A head refused while the round trip is in flight** (a reader's refresh adopted a rival and refused B's head): the send is refused stale, `refused()` throws the recorded refusal, A's attempt is retained and A's segment intact. By inspection of the re-check order; the one-collection case pins the same ordering.
- **The sorted open order against a concurrent `commit`** on the same collections: no test; the deadlock-freedom argument is the one `commitOnce` documents, and the open order is asserted at the seam (a check that `collectionIds` are visited sorted, or a comment citing `commitOnce`).
- **`commitEntry` while a `commit` of the same coordinator is open**: not a supported combination (the queue builds a coordinator per submission). State it on the method; no guard beyond the latch.
- **Backlog `debt-multi-collection-retry-cannot-see-the-taken-revision`** applies to the slice too: its refresh rounds pass no `tailFloor`. Leave a one-line `NOTE:` at the slice's refresh citing the ticket, and append the slice as a second site on that ticket.
