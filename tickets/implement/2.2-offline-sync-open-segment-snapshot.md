Second of three tickets for stage 2 of [docs/offline-sync.md §Implementation stages](../../docs/offline-sync.md#implementation-stages). The design is [§Rollback and savepoints only touch the open segment](../../docs/offline-sync.md#rollback-and-savepoints-only-touch-the-open-segment). Builds on the segment list and the segment-aware replay from `offline-sync-segment-bookkeeping`.

## The problem a snapshot has now

`Collection.snapshotPending` captures the whole staged state (every tracker transform, every pending action) and `restorePending` puts it back verbatim. With sealed segments, the sealed list can change while a snapshot is outstanding: the drain (third ticket) commits the head segment and removes it, or a refusal removes another, or a new segment is sealed. A verbatim restore would then put a drained segment back into the staged list, and the next head commit would commit it a second time. The doc's test plan names that scenario ("an entry queued, then an SQL transaction opened and rolled back while the drain commits the entry: the entry is committed exactly once").

## The snapshot

`CollectionSnapshot` keeps `transforms` (the WHOLE tracker, sealed effects included — `Tree.readView` builds a committed read view from it, and that view must include every queued entry the SQL layer was told had committed), `pins`, and `context`, and changes two things:

- `pending` holds the OPEN segment's actions only.
- a new `sealedVersion: number` records `SegmentList.version` at capture.

A snapshot built by hand (tests do this) may omit `sealedVersion`; treat an absent value as "unchanged".

## The restore

`restorePending(snapshot)` becomes `async` and returns `Promise<void>`, because one of its arms has to replay, and replay runs the (async) action handlers. It is still latch-free by contract: every caller already holds the collection latch or runs on the single call path that excludes a concurrent stage (the coordinator's `rollback` and the bridge's savepoint rollback document that assumption today), and nothing here changes it.

Decide the arm from three synchronous facts — `sealedChanged` (the snapshot's version differs from the list's), `boundaryMoved` (today's test: the held revision is above the captured one, or the capture had none and one is held now), and whether any segment is sealed now:

| sealed changed | boundary moved | sealed now | arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| no | no | any | **verbatim**, exactly today: reset the tracker to a copy of the snapshot's transforms, restore its pins, set the open segment to the snapshot's actions. This keeps an invented collection's header, the case `restorePending` already documents. |
| no | yes | none | today's two arms, unchanged: an empty captured open segment resets the tracker empty; a non-empty one restores verbatim (the NOTE about a mid-transaction savepoint across a moved boundary stays as it is — conditional, not yet observed). |
| no | yes | some | **replay**: a reset would drop the sealed segments' effects, and verbatim would restore them at the old boundary. |
| yes | any | any | **replay**. |

The replay arm: set the open segment to the snapshot's actions, then `await this.replayActions()` — sealed segments in order (refused ones skipped, a newly refused one recorded and reported through `onSealedRefused` as in any replay), then the restored open actions. The snapshot's pins are NOT restored on this arm: a replay resets the tracker (which clears the pin store) and every re-staged update re-pins its base from the read cache as it is now, which is the base the re-staged operations were actually computed against. The verbatim arm restores the captured pins as today, for the reason that arm's doc already gives. Say both halves in the method comment — the staged-base rule in [docs/internals.md §Staged Edits Keep Their Base](../../docs/internals.md#staged-edits-keep-their-base) is what each arm has to satisfy.

A refusal of the OPEN segment during the replay arm propagates out of `restorePending`, as a refresh's replay would surface it. The caller rolling back is told its transaction's own actions no longer apply at the boundary the collection is now at — rare (it needs a sealed change AND a rival between capture and restore), and the honest answer. Document it; do not swallow it.

## Callers

Every caller awaits the promise:

- `TransactionCoordinator`: the three sites in `commitOnceLatched` (the catch, the partial-commit failed arm, the empty-committed restore loop), `rollback`, and the `execute` failed arm. The `execute` site carries a NOTE saying the fold is kept await-free because `restorePending` is synchronous; the participants' latches are held across that span, so awaiting there admits no interleaved stage — rewrite the NOTE to say that, rather than deleting it.
- `Tree.restore` becomes `async` and returns the promise.
- The Quereus bridge: `rollbackToSavepoint` and the per-collection loop in `rollbackTransaction` await each restore. Quereus types the connection hooks as `MaybePromise<void>` (`rollbackToSavepoint` in `packages/quereus-plugin-optimystic/node_modules/@quereus/quereus/dist/src/vtab/connection.d.ts`), so `OptimysticVirtualTableConnection.rollbackToSavepoint` and `TransactionBridge.rollbackToSavepoint` become `async` and return the promise. Confirm, by reading the Quereus call site, that the engine awaits a returned promise before running the next statement; record what you found in the handoff. This is the one plugin change in stage 2, forced by the type change; nothing about the bridge's behaviour changes while no segment is sealed, because the verbatim arm is then always taken.
- `Collection.unstage` keeps its own verbatim-or-replay split and is unaffected.

## Edge cases & interactions

- **The double-commit scenario, at the db-core level.** Seal segment E, take a snapshot (the bridge's `markDirty` moment), stage an open action, then remove E while the snapshot is outstanding (`dropSealed(E)` in this ticket; the third ticket adds the `commitHead` variant), then `restorePending`. Assert: E is gone from `sealedEntries()`, the open action is gone from the tracker, the tracker equals a fresh replay of the remaining state, and `sealedVersion` on a new snapshot differs. Test — this is the contract on the branching above.
- **Savepoint rollback while a refused entry is removed.** Seal E1 and E2 (E2 updates a row E1 inserted, under an `unchanged` guard), snapshot, stage an open action, `dropSealed(E1)` — the replay refuses E2 — then restore: E2 stays refused (not retried by the restore's replay), the open action is gone, the callback fired once. Test.
- **No sealed segment, every existing path.** The verbatim arm and today's moved-boundary arms are byte-for-byte today's code; `collection.spec.ts` ("external commit visibility after conflict replay", "a restored snapshot brings back the bases its operations were computed on"), `coordinator-rollback-pending.spec.ts`, `coordinator-own-action-replay.spec.ts`, `read-view-pinned.spec.ts` and the plugin's savepoint and rollback specs must pass unchanged apart from the added `await`s.
- **Pins through the replay arm.** After a replay-arm restore of a snapshot taken over a committed base, `tracker.stagedBaseRevs` names the cache's current revision for a re-staged update, and `movedBases()` is empty. One assertion in the double-commit test.
- **An invented collection.** Verbatim arm keeps the header as today; replay arm resets to the invented baseline (first ticket) so the header survives too. One case: invented, seal E, snapshot, stage, `dropSealed(E)`, restore — the tree is still readable. Test.
- **`readView` from a snapshot taken over sealed segments** includes the sealed effects (the committed view the SQL layer expects). By inspection: `transforms` is unchanged in meaning; one assertion in the first test that a `readView` of the snapshot sees E's entry after E was dropped live.
- **Snapshot taken while a head commit is in flight** (third ticket): the version is bumped when the head is removed, so a restore after that takes the replay arm. Name it here so the third ticket's test covers it; nothing to build here.
- **Two snapshots at different depths** (nested savepoints): each carries its own version; restoring the outer after the inner does the right thing per the table. By inspection.
- **A refusal of the open segment inside the replay arm** propagates. Test with a rival taking the open action's key between capture and restore, with one segment sealed.
