# One declared link round trip, every dial deadline derived from it

## Why

Opening a relayed connection costs about four link round trips (eight one-way delays). Sereus measured 7.3 s at 900 ms one-way and 12.1 s at 1500 ms one-way. Opening a protocol stream costs one more round trip, even on a connection that is already open (`packages/db-p2p/test/stream-open-costs-a-round-trip.spec.ts`; `@libp2p/multistream-select@7` ignores `negotiateFully: false`). db-p2p's RPC clients dial under a hard-coded 3000 ms (`DEFAULT_DIAL_TIMEOUT_MS`, the "pushDialTimeoutMs ?? 3000" defaults, the Quereus plugin's `dialTimeoutMs: 3_000`). So:

- a relayed connection cannot open above about 375 ms one-way;
- above a 3 s round trip, even an open connection fails every RPC: cluster consensus rounds, repo reads and writes, sync, dispute and pushes.

Sereus supports relayed links up to a 3 s round trip (maintainer decision). `NodeOptions.connectionManager` raises libp2p's own `dialTimeout` and `inboundUpgradeTimeout`, but those govern only dials without a caller signal, and every RPC here passes one. No `NodeOptions` field reaches the RPC deadlines.

`read-consult-budget-bounds-the-whole-request` (prereq) makes the read-path consult honour `cohortQueryTimeoutMs`. This ticket covers everything else.

## Decision

The source ticket left two options open: a node-level link-latency declaration, or a per-deadline override. This ticket takes **one node-level declaration**:

- It matches how sereus states its requirement ("links up to a 3 s round trip"), and sereus prefers it.
- The deadlines can no longer drift apart; today three separate 3000s and two 10000s agree only by comment ("Matches `spread-on-churn.ts`").

Explicit per-deadline settings that already exist keep winning over the derived value: `connectionManager.*`, `underReplicationDrain.push*`, `spreadOnChurn.push*`, `clusterPolicy.cohortQueryTimeoutMs`, and a caller's own `dialTimeoutMs`. So nothing that works today changes.

Stream negotiation on a reused connection gets no separate bound. The derived dial deadline includes one round trip for it, and negotiation never costs more than a fresh dial. Rejected alternatives:

- Not forwarding the dial signal into `newStream` on the reuse path would need two signals through `IPeerNetwork.connect`: the caller's cancellation still has to reach `newStream`, and only the dial deadline would be dropped.
- A separate negotiation deadline adds a second number that must track the first.

## Specification

`NodeOptions.linkRoundTripMs?: number`: the slowest round trip, in milliseconds, between any two nodes that will talk to each other, relayed hops included. Absent means today's behaviour exactly: every derived value floors at the current constant.

A pure resolver in `rpc-deadline.ts`, e.g. `resolveLinkDeadlines(linkRoundTripMs?: number): LinkDeadlines`, with `r = linkRoundTripMs`:

| deadline | derivation | reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RPC dial (`dialTimeoutMs`) | `max(3000, 6 × r)` | relayed connection open ≈ 4 r, stream negotiation 1 r, one r of margin |
| RPC response (`responseTimeoutMs`) | `max(10_000, 3 × r)` | request plus reply is 1 r; the rest covers payload transfer and peer-side work |
| libp2p `connectionManager.dialTimeout` and `inboundUpgradeTimeout` | `max(10_000, 5 × r)` | connection open only, ≈ 4 r plus margin; both ends, since every node is someone's listener |
| `clusterPolicy.cohortQueryTimeoutMs` when undeclared | `max(1000, 3 × r)` | a consult on a reused connection is 2 r (negotiation plus request); a fresh connection does not fit, which is safe (the read is flagged silent, not misreported) |

Validation copies `resolveCohortQueryTimeoutMs`. A value that is not finite and above zero, or large enough that `6 × r` overflows the 32-bit delay `setTimeout` accepts, **throws at node construction**. Falling back to the defaults would silently keep the LAN deadlines on the one deployment that declared the field to escape them.

Threading:

- `ProtocolClient` subclasses (`ClusterClient`, `SyncClient`, `DisputeClient`, and `RepoClient`'s redirect hop) take their fallback deadlines from the client instance, not from module constants. Pass the resolved values into their constructors or `create` factories. `withRpcDeadlineDefaults` merges against those instead of the constants; the constants remain as the undeclared floors. `DisputeClient`'s hard-coded `dialTimeoutMs: DEFAULT_DIAL_TIMEOUT_MS` uses the instance value too.
- `createLibp2pNodeBase` resolves once and hands the values to: `createClusterClient`, `createDisputeClient`, every `SyncClient` it builds (including `RestorationCoordinator`'s, through its init), the drain's `pushBlockToPeers` defaults, `SpreadOnChurnMonitor`'s `push*` defaults, `BlockTransferCoordinator`'s `transferTimeoutMs` (as `max(30_000, dial)`), the libp2p `connectionManager` block, and an undeclared `clusterPolicy.cohortQueryTimeoutMs` (filled in *before* `resolveClusterPolicy`, so `CoordinatorRepo`, `ClusterMember` and `reconcilePassTimeoutMs` all see one number).
- Expose the resolved `LinkDeadlines` on `OptimysticNodeAttachments`, so a host holding a node can reach them without the options it was built from.
- The Quereus plugin's `collection-factory.ts` builds its `NetworkTransactor` with `dialTimeoutMs: 3_000`. Take it from the node's attachment instead, and pass `linkRoundTripMs` through from `libp2pOptions` where it builds the node.
- `NetworkTransactor`'s own `DEFAULT_DIAL_TIMEOUT_MS` in db-core stays; db-core has no node options. Its callers pass the value.

Tripwire, not in scope: the plugin's `NetworkTransactor` `timeoutMs: 30_000` overall transaction budget. At a 3 s round trip a pend-plus-commit with a redirect is about 5–6 r (15–18 s) on a warm connection, and a cold relayed connection adds 4 r. It fits, but narrowly. Leave a `NOTE:` at that site: if slow-link writes show `RepoClient timeout`, derive the budget from the same declaration.

## Tests

One unit spec for the resolver, which is the specification above and has real branching:

- undeclared → exactly today's constants;
- a small `r` → floors hold;
- `r = 3000` → the scaled values;
- invalid or overflowing input → throws.

No wiring tests. The premise spec already pins why the dial deadline must include a round trip.
