# Blob announcements are answered

Third of three parts of the blob copying work. The prereqs built the node's reader of committed state (`BlobReferenceCheck`), the client's `announce` and `held`, and the `responsible` hold with its scope. This part built the receiving half: a node decides for itself what to do with an announcement, takes the hold, pulls the blob, and tells the announcer it has it. It deliberately does **not** track who still owes a copy, announce on its own, or report durability — that is `feat-blob-owed-copies-are-drained` (`tickets/plan/2.63-feat-blob-owed-copies-are-drained.md`), which replaces the node's `held:received` log line with the owed entry's update.

## What was built

**`AnnounceLimiter`** (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/announce-limiter.ts`). Two bounds, one instance per node: `admit(peer)` counts one announcement in that peer's fixed one-minute window and refuses past `BLOB_ANNOUNCES_PER_PEER_PER_MINUTE` (600); `readSlot(read)` runs at most `BLOB_REFERENCE_READS_IN_FLIGHT` (4) reads of committed state at once across every peer together, queueing the rest first-come-first-served and never refusing. At most `BLOB_ANNOUNCE_PEERS_TRACKED` (4096) windows are remembered; a new peer past that evicts the window that started longest ago. All three constants are in `packages/db-p2p/src/protocol-limits.ts` with the honest-announcer arithmetic in their comments.

**`BlobAnnounceReceiver`** (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/announce-receiver.ts`).

- `decide(announcer, ref, scope)` answers in the order §Who may fetch and who may store sets out: `on-demand` → `declined`; a peer the membership predicate refuses → `unreferenced`; a cohort (this node's own `findCluster` view) without self, or a lookup that throws → `not-responsible`; a peer over its allowance → `unreferenced`, with no read; then `BlobReferenceCheck.inScope` under a read slot, raced against `answerWithinMs` (half the node's `responseTimeoutMs`, measured from the start of `decide` so waiting for a slot counts); then `holdResponsible`, flushed before the answer, and `whole` or `fetching`.
- The pull: one session per reference (`inFlight: Map<BlobRef, Set<string>>`), background priority, the announcer as a known holder, no `waitMs`. A second announcer joins and is sent `held` with the first. On resolve, `client.held` to each awaiting announcer the gate still admits, once, not retried. On reject, the hold is kept and the next announcement starts fresh.
- `resume()` restarts the pull of every `responsibleRefs()` blob that is not `here`; the node calls it off the start path.
- `keepAfterRead(ref)` is reader repair: in the background, cohort → not already responsible → `BlobReferenceCheck.anywhere` under a read slot → `holdResponsible`. Never throws, deduped per reference, a no-op on an `on-demand` node.
- `stop()` makes a pull that ends afterwards send no `held`.

**`NodeBlobService`** gained `afterFetchedWhole?`, called when a session it started resolves and the blob is then `here`, and `fetch` now returns at once for a blob already whole here (after `signal?.throwIfAborted()`), so no `fetch` of a whole blob reaches committed state.

**Node wiring** (`packages/db-p2p/src/libp2p-node-base.ts`): `NodeOptions.blobs.hold` (`'eager'` default), late-bound `onAnnounce` / `onHeld` on the blob protocol service entry, the limiter and receiver built on both roles after `wireBlobFetcher`, the receiver's stop wrapper installed ahead of the fetcher's, `resume()` kicked off, and `BlobAnnounceReceiver` / `AnnounceLimiter` exported from both entries.

## Use cases to exercise

- **The round trip.** Two serving nodes, `clusterSize: 2`. A puts a blob, commits its reference in a registered scope, announces to B: `fetching`, B's `responsibleScope(ref)` is already the scope when the answer lands, B's `stat` reaches `here`, a second announce answers `whole`. (`packages/db-p2p/test/blob-announce.spec.ts`.)
- **Reader repair.** A referenced blob nobody announced: B reads it through `b.blobs.read` and afterwards holds it `responsible` under the scope it was found in. (Same spec.)
- **The early answers.** `on-demand`, a cohort without self, and an unregistered scope each answer without pulling and leave no `responsible` hold. (`packages/db-p2p/test/blob-announce-receiver.spec.ts`.)
- **The allowance.** 600 admitted from one peer, the 601st refused, admitted again a minute later, a second peer unaffected. (Same spec.)
- **Worth exercising by hand or by review** (see the gaps below): a node started with `blobs: { hold: 'on-demand' }` declining every announcement and running no repair; two announcers of one reference both receiving `held`; a restart between `fetching` and the pull finishing; `DEBUG=optimystic:db-p2p:blob:*` showing the `announce:*` lines in the documented order.

## Known gaps and deviations, honestly

- **Most of the ticket's edge cases are inspection-only, not tested.** Untested by anything added here: `on-demand` end to end through a real node, two announcers on one pull both receiving `held`, a `held` from a non-member being dropped, `resume` after a restart, `stop()` mid-pull, a slow reference read being answered `unreferenced`, and `held:send-failed`. The ticket parked the restart and the `held` sink in `feat-blob-owed-copies-are-drained`, which is where `held` first has an effect worth asserting; the rest are branches a reviewer should read rather than assume.
- **Three log lines were added beyond the ticket's list**, each because the alternative was eating an exception: `announce:read-failed` (on `:error`; the reference read threw rather than answering `uncertain`, which `BlobReferenceCheck` is written not to do), `read:keep-failed` (on `:error`; reader repair threw, so the blob keeps only its `cached` hold), and `fetched:stat-failed` on `blob:service` (the stat deciding whether a finished fetch left the blob whole threw, so repair was not offered the reference). All three are documented in `docs/debugging.md`. Cut them if a reviewer would rather the failure propagate.
- **A local `stat` that throws inside `decide` is read as "not here" rather than rejecting** (`announce:stat-failed`), so the answer is `fetching` and the pull reports the same corruption as `fetch:gave-up`. That keeps the interface's promise that `decide` rejects only on a store fault writing the hold, but it does mean a node with one corrupt local tree answers `fetching` for a blob it will never finish. The announcer's drain gives up under its own rule.
- **`resume()` is skipped entirely on an `on-demand` node.** The ticket does not say what should happen to holds a node took while it was eager and then was reconfigured; pulling them seemed like the one thing an on-demand node must not do. Named in a comment at the guard.
- **`held` arriving before the node finishes starting is logged `held:dropped-non-member`.** The membership check the `onHeld` closure applies is late-bound (`blobPeerAdmitted`, which refuses until the gate exists), because the gate is built after `createLibp2p` and a protocol handler is live before then. Reusing the non-member line rather than inventing a fourth one is a judgement call; the alternative was reading an uninitialised `const` under temporal dead zone.
- **`'<unidentified>'` shares one allowance bucket.** `BlobProtocolService` passes that literal when a connection names no peer and no inbound gate is configured, so every such announcer counts against one window, and `sendHeld` would fail to parse it as a peer id (caught, logged `held:send-failed`). Conservative in both directions, but worth a reviewer's eye.
- **An `AnnounceLimiter` eviction can let a flooded-out peer start its count early.** `NOTE:` left at the eviction site saying so, and that the read limit is what actually bounds the work.
- **The two-node spec commits for real and runs in well under a second locally**, which is fast enough to be suspicious — it is worth confirming the assertions can fail (they do: a `not-responsible` answer or a missing hold fails the first case).
