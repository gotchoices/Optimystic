# A blob being read is not swept

`PieceSweeper` deletes every stored piece that no held blob's tree reaches. Its roots used to be exactly the blobs with a record, so a blob whose last hold had gone had no root and its pieces were deleted — correct when nothing names the blob, wrong when a read of it is running. Nothing takes the last hold off a blob on its own today, so the hole was not reachable in practice; `feat-blob-cached-copies-stay-within-a-budget` makes it reachable by removing the `cached` hold from least-recently-read blobs and then sweeping.

What landed is a count of reads and fetches in flight per reference, kept by `LocalBlobService`, which the sweeper takes as extra roots. The budget ticket will read the same count (`inUse`) to skip a blob it must not evict.

## What changed

**`LocalBlobService` (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/local-blob-service.ts`)**

- A private `uses: Map<BlobRef, number>` and three public members over it: `inUse(ref)`, `inUseRefs()` and `beginUse(ref): () => void`. `beginUse` raises the count and returns a release that lowers it and deletes the entry at zero; the release captures a `released` flag so a second call is a no-op and cannot lower another holder's count. `beginUse` is synchronous and takes no gate, which is what lets it be the first statement of a generator body with no `await` before it.
- `readThrough` now takes the use as the first statement of its body and releases it in a `finally`, delegating the read itself to a new private `readChunks` (the old body, unchanged). One site covers `LocalBlobService.read` and both branches of `NodeBlobService.read`.
- `LocalBlobService.fetch` holds a use around its body, even though it only stats.
- The `NOTE:` above `holdCached` that asked a future ticket for this mechanism is replaced by a pointer to it.
- The sweeper is constructed with `inUseRefs: () => this.inUseRefs()`.

**`PieceSweeper` (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/piece-sweeper.ts`)**

- `PieceSweeperDeps.inUseRefs: () => Iterable<BlobRef>` — required, not optional, so the one construction site is a compile error without it.
- `walkRecord`'s tree walk is split out as `walkTree`, and a new `walkInUse` walks every in-use ref with no record required. It is called inside the exclusive gate in `decideAndDelete`, after the re-walk of `changed` and before `inProgress`'s ids are added — the latest point at which the sweep reads anything, so every use taken before the deletes begin is seen.
- The class doc's list of things that can make a piece live goes from four to five.

**`NodeBlobService` (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/node-blob-service.ts`)**

- `fetch` holds a use around its whole body (so a blob being brought here is in use from before its first piece, not from the session's start).
- `startSession` takes a use when the session starts and releases it when `session.done` settles, either way. This is the window the old `holdCached` `NOTE:` named: a read that consumed its whole range returns, releasing its own use, while its session is still doing its confirming walk and marking the record whole.

`BlobAnnounceReceiver` needs no use: it takes and flushes the `responsible` hold before it starts its pull, so the blob has a hold of its own throughout.

| Test | What it pins |
|---|---|
| `a sweep keeps the pieces of a blob being read after its last hold went` | The one race the next ticket's eviction depends on: `put` 300 KiB (two data pieces and a list root), `unpin` so the record is gone and the pieces remain, take the read's first chunk, then `sweep()` — `deleted: 0, kept: 3`; the rest of the read yields the original bytes; a second `sweep()` after the read finishes deletes all three. |

**Verified it reproduces:** with `inUseRefs` stubbed to `() => []`, this test fails with `deleted: 3` where `0` was expected. The stub was reverted before the handoff.

## Known gaps and what to look at

- **A use taken during the delete phase is not protected.** `beginUse` takes no gate, so a read beginning after the sweep read `inUseRefs()` inside its exclusive block but before the deletes finish is unprotected. This is the pre-existing exposure of reading a blob with no hold at all, recorded at the `NOTE:` above `NodeBlobService.storedPiece`, which was deliberately left as it is. Making `beginUse` take the gate shared would make it `async`, which would push it after an `await` and lose the "before any `await`" property the generator site depends on. The trade is deliberate and the window is bounded by one delete phase.
- **An abandoned iterator never releases its use.** A consumer that stops calling `next()` without calling `return` never runs the `finally`, so the blob is protected for the life of the process. Parked as a tripwire `NOTE:` at `beginUse` rather than defended against.
- **An in-use ref whose tree is malformed aborts the whole sweep** (`tree-unreadable`), exactly as a held blob's does, so nothing is deleted that pass. That is the keep-everything direction this subsystem already chose (the accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` above `walkRecord`), but it does widen the set of refs that can stop a sweep to include blobs with no record.
- **A ref both in use and holding a record is walked twice.** Idempotent, and the cost is one extra tree walk of a blob somebody is reading right now.
- **`inUse` has no caller yet.** It exists for `feat-blob-cached-copies-stay-within-a-budget`; only `inUseRefs` is wired (into the sweeper) today, so `inUse`'s correctness is pinned by nothing but reading it.

## Review findings

- Tripwire parked at `LocalBlobService.beginUse` (`NOTE:`): a use ends only when its holder releases it, so an async iterator abandoned without `return` keeps its blob out of every sweep for the life of the process; harmless while consumers use `for await` or close the iterator, and the remedy if a node is ever seen keeping blobs nothing reads is a time limit on a use.
