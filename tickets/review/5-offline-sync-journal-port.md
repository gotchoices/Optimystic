Stage 5 of [docs/offline-sync.md §Implementation stages](../../docs/offline-sync.md#implementation-stages), part one of three: the db-core half of persistence. It adds no storage. Part two (`offline-sync-journal-store`) implements the port over a key-value store in the plugin; part three (`offline-sync-journal-restore`) restores journaled entries after a restart through the `restore` added here. Both downstream tickets' expectations of this API were checked against what landed (the `stage` contract in the restore ticket names `holdsCommitted`, `restoreSealed` with `{ before }` or `'last'`, and `queue.restore(init, stage)`, all as built).

## What landed

**The port** (`IOfflineJournal` in `packages/db-core/src/offline/journal.ts`, exported from the offline barrel): `queued(record)`, `driving(entryId, state)`, `settled(status)`, with the two record types `JournalEntryRecord` and `JournalDriveState` as the ticket defined them, plus a named alias `JournalSettledStatus` for the two terminal statuses. It speaks only entry ids, collection ids, actions and `InFlightAttempt`s.

**The queue** (`OfflineQueue` in `packages/db-core/src/offline/queue.ts`):
- `OfflineQueueOptions.journal` replaces `recordAttempt`. A queue built without a journal skips every write and behaves as before.
- `enqueue` is async and writes before it admits: seal, build the entry record from each sealed collection's `sealedActions`, await `journal.queued`, then register, emit `queued`, publish and start or wake the drain. On a throw every sealed collection is unsealed in reverse order (`Collection.unseal`) and the journal's error is rethrown; nothing is registered or emitted. One path, no synchronous twin. `queued` still arrives before `enqueue` resolves.
- `journal.driving` is written at three sites: `recordAttempts` before each send (a throw aborts the cycle exactly as the old hook's did, through the unchanged `RecordAttemptFailed` wrapper, and sets no doubt), `partiallyCommitted` after the entry's committed and retained maps are updated and before `partially-committed` is emitted, and after an identity renewal in the `torn-not-saved` arm of `applyFailure`. The latter two log `offline:journal-write-failed` and go on. The state is built by `driveState(entry, attempts?)` in `packages/db-core/src/offline/entry.ts`, which copies the maps and lays the attempts about to go over the retained ones.
- `journal.settled` is awaited before `committed` is emitted (`committed` is async now; `submit`, `partiallyCommitted` and the restore await it) and before `failed` is emitted in `remove`. A rejection is logged and swallowed there.
- `restore(init, stage)` with `RestoreInit` and `RestoredSegment` as specified: the drain hold, insertion by `seq` with `nextSeq` raised past it, identity from the drive record, the four `stage` outcomes, rollback on a throwing `stage` (the entry leaves the queue, every segment already staged is dropped, the error rethrown, nothing emitted), refused after `dispose` and after a defect.
- The hold: one `DrainHold` the loop meets at the top of every iteration (`parkForHold`, step zero, ahead of the pending-refusal step), with a `parked` signal the loop raises there and in the loop's `finally` (so a loop that exits while a restore waits does not strand it). A loop that never started, has stopped, or is parked on an empty queue counts as parked at once. Restores serialise on their own chain. After the loop parks the restore also waits out the removal chain before `stage` runs.

**The collection** (`packages/db-core/src/collection/collection.ts` and `segments.ts`): `sealedActions`, `unseal`, `holdsCommitted` (with the checkpoint `NOTE:`), `restoreSealed`, and `SegmentList.insert` plus `SegmentList.unseal`. `replayActions` takes an optional entry id whose refusal is recorded but not reported, which is how `restoreSealed` returns the restored segment's own refusal instead of reporting it; `recordSealedRefusal` gained a `report` flag and its log line a `reported=` field.

**Callers outside db-core**, which the ticket's file list did not name but which the async `enqueue` and the removed option forced: `OfflineMode.enqueue` and `TransactionBridge.sealIntoQueue` are async (the bridge's `commitTransaction` awaits the seal, so a journal failure lands in its existing `afterFailedCommit` and rolls the transaction back as any failed commit); `packages/db-p2p/test/offline-queue-mesh.spec.ts` moved from `recordAttempt` to a journal double.

**Docs**: `docs/offline-sync.md` §What is journaled (the two records in the queue's terms, the plugin adding table names), §Write ordering (rules 1 to 4 reworded, rule 4 as "before the next send" with the window and what covers it), §Identity across attempts (names `IOfflineJournal.driving`), §Restore after a restart (the `holdsCommitted` rule and the insertion-by-sequence rule in prose), §Following transactions ("synchronously" loosened). `docs/internals.md` §The offline queue: the port, `restore`, the hold, and `recordAttempts` as the write-ahead wrapper over `driving`. `docs/debugging.md`: the `offline` namespace row. `yarn lint:docs` clean.

## Decisions the reviewer should check

- **`renewIdentity` does not write the drive record itself.** The ticket named it as one of the three sites "after the new id is minted". Writing inside it would make a partial commit whose remainder failed `torn-not-saved` write twice with identical content, so `renewIdentity` stays a synchronous mint-and-clear and its two callers write once: `applyFailure`'s `torn-not-saved` arm right after it, and `partiallyCommitted` once the renewal, if any, and the committed set are both on the entry. Same disk state, one write fewer.
- **The sequence number is taken only once the journal has the record.** `enqueue` builds the entry with `seq = nextSeq` and increments after `journal.queued` resolves, so a refused enqueue leaves no gap in the order a prefix listing of the journal returns. Pinned by the "takes the same seq" assertion.
- **One log line for every journal failure**: `offline:journal-write-failed` with `step=queued|send|partial-commit|renewal|settled`. The old `offline:record-attempt-failed` is gone; debugging.md updated. While editing that row I also corrected `offline:partial-commit-deferred`, which never matched the code's `offline:partially-committed`.
- **`discard` of the entry being restored throws `UnknownEntryError` through an explicit guard** (`restoring`). The ticket's rationale ("since it is not yet listed") does not hold as written: the entry has to be in `list` and `byId` before `stage` so `stage` can read its position from `entries()` and so refusals its insertion reports about other entries are attributed. The behaviour is the ticket's; the guard is explicit.
- **`queued` is emitted for every restore that does not settle at once**, a refused restored segment included (then `failed` from the loop's first step), matching an enqueue followed by a refusal. The ticket's "otherwise `queued` is emitted" was read as "unless settled".
- **`dispose` during a restore**: a restore that has not started `stage` when the queue is disposed rejects with `QueueDisposedError` and releases the hold; one that has finishes its registration. Stated in the method's doc comment; by inspection.
- **`already-committed` keeps a class the drive record knew.** A collection the drive record lists with a real durability is not overwritten with `undefined` when `stage` also answers `already-committed` for it.
- **`attempts` is reported as 1 for an entry restored with a drive record**, with the code comment the ticket asked for; 0 without.
- **Position flags on insertion are set silently**, as `claimRemoval` already does when the head changes: the old head's `waitingFor` becomes `earlier-entry` with no `queued` emission; `entries()` and `status()` show it live.
- **`takeHold` wakes the loop** so a loop sleeping on the re-check timer reaches the hold promptly; the wake also resets the backoff step, as every wake does. A restore therefore re-drives a transiently failed head at once after it releases rather than after the remaining backoff. Minor, noted in the hold's comment.

## Tripwires recorded

- `Collection.holdsCommitted` `NOTE:` — `actionIdAt` reaches back only to the last checkpoint; none is written today (backlog `debt-the-collection-log-never-writes-a-checkpoint`); read the log entry instead if that changes.
- `OfflineQueue.restoring` `NOTE:` — a `discard` of another entry chained while `stage` runs is not held back; a refusal of the restoring entry's already-inserted segment that such a drop's replay reports is attributed by `causeOf` to the discarded entry. Fine while restores run at start-up; make `discard` wait out the hold if it ever misleads.

## Known gaps and contracts to keep in view

- `enqueue`'s latch-free contract gained a clause: no second enqueue of the same collections may run until the first resolves, because the unseal on failure requires each segment to still be the newest sealed one. The plugin's single-writer bridge guarantees this; a `Tree`/`Diary` application has to bracket it. Stated in the method's doc.
- `causeOf` during a restore with no concurrent removal answers `undefined` for refusals the insertion's replay reports about other entries, as the ticket accepted; stated at `causeOf`.
- The restored entry's `expiresAt` is taken from the record as given, not re-derived from `expireAfterMs`.
- The "`stage` answers `already-committed` for one of two collections" case is covered by a test over a real partial commit rather than by inspection; see below.
- Not run: the full db-p2p suite (only a test file changed there; the mesh spec and the package typecheck were run), `yarn check:rn`, and the env-gated integration specs.

## Tests added

`packages/db-core/test/offline-queue.spec.ts` — the harness's `recordAttempt` hook became a `JournalDouble` (records every entry record, drive state and settled status; each step can be made to throw), and `queueEntry` awaits the async `enqueue`. The first existing case also asserts the write-then-admit order (nothing admitted before the journal has the record, `queued` emitted before `enqueue` resolves, the record naming the sealed collection with its actions verbatim, one drive record ahead of each send, each terminal status handed to the journal as the very object later emitted). The "write-ahead hook that throws" case now uses a journal whose `driving` throws. New describe `OfflineQueue with a journal`:
- **journal refuses the entry record** — the enqueue rejects with the journal's error, nothing is reported or queued, both collections are unsealed with their actions back in the open segment, the caller's `restorePending` of the pre-transaction snapshots ends with the actions gone and nothing unsynced, and the next enqueue takes seq 1.
- **`settled` rejects** — `committed` still emitted, entry gone, `offline:journal-write-failed step=settled` logged, `whenDrained` resolves.
- **restore while a submission is in flight** — with the head's commit parked and a second entry queued behind it, a restore at seq 1.5 waits (stage has not run while parked; `discard` of the restoring entry throws `UnknownEntryError`), then runs with the entry listed as head and the other as `earlier-entry`, inserts its segment first in the collection, the parked entry commits once, the restored entry drains next, then the other; one drive record per send, no entry record written by the restore.
- **every collection already committed, then a refused restored segment** — a second queue over fresh handles restores an entry the first queue committed, `stage` answering through `holdsCommitted` on the recorded attempt: `committed` emitted at once with `undefined` classes and no `overall`, no `queued`, the journal told, nothing sent, nothing sealed; then a restore whose segment the committed state refuses fails with `constraint`, no `causedBy`, `queued` then `failed`, nothing sent.
- **a partly committed entry restored** — a first queue partially commits over two trees (the drive record rewritten with the committed collection and the pending attempt); a second queue restores it with that record: queued in doubt with its committed half, `attempts` 1, only the pending collection sealed, not discardable; the next submission drives only the pending collection under the recorded action id, and the final durability names both collections with each log holding the entry once.

`packages/db-core/test/collection-segments.spec.ts`:
- **SegmentList `unseal` and `insert`** — the newest-only and empty-open-segment rules, the actions returned to the open segment, insertion before a named segment or last, the already-sealed and unknown-`before` refusals, and the version bumps.
- **`restoreSealed` returns its own refusal** — recorded on the segment, listed, not reported, the read unaffected.
- **`restoreSealed` ahead of a later segment the replay newly refuses** — the later segment refused and reported; the restored one staged and visible; the segment behind it re-staged.
- **`restoreSealed` refusals** — over a non-empty open segment, before an unknown segment, under an id already sealed, and while a head commit is in flight (`SealedSegmentsError` `in-flight`); each changes nothing; allowed once the commit is done.
- **`holdsCommitted`** — true at the held action and revision, false past it, false under another action, still true after adopting a rival's later revision, false on an invented collection.
