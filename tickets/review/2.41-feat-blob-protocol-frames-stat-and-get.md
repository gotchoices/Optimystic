# Review: blob protocol server and client (`stat` and `get`)

The blob protocol (`${prefix}/db-p2p/blob/1.0.0`) now has a server on every node and a client a caller can construct. The wire types, codec, frame helpers, `ProgressDeadline`, limits and `openStreamWithin` came from the prereq (`blob-protocol-wire-and-frames`). Nothing here fetches, verifies or stores a piece: the client hands each piece's bytes to its caller; the server serves only what its own piece store holds. The fetcher (`feat-blob-fetcher-verifies-and-resumes`) is the first consumer of the client and constructs it; the node does not.

## What was built

- **`BlobProtocolService`** (`blob/blob-protocol-service.ts`, factory `blobProtocolService(init)(components)`, the `BlockTransferService` shape). Per inbound stream: the authorization gate before anything is read; a `ProgressDeadline` of `progressTimeoutMs`; one `next()` on `readBlobFrames`, then the reader is released (`releaseBlobFrames`), so a second frame is never read and further input sits in libp2p's capped read buffer; decode; answer; `closeBlobStream`. `stat` maps locality `here`/`partial`/other to `whole`/`partial`/`none`. `get` takes a slot from `InboundGetAllowance` (2 per peer, 16 per node, released in the handler's `finally` on every path), sends the `held` header, then reads each held piece just before sending it. A piece `has` reported and `get` then cannot find aborts the stream with `BlobProtocolError`. `announce` → `onAnnounce` or `declined`; `held` → `onHeld`, answered `{kind:'held'}`. Any throw → `serve:stalled` (deadline fired) or `serve:aborted`, stream aborted.
- **`BlobProtocolClient`** (`blob/blob-protocol-client.ts`): `stat`, `get`, `wireBytes`. Refuses locally (RangeError) an empty list, a list over its cap, or a `get` with duplicate ids. Opens through `openStreamWithin` with `dialTimeoutMs`, sends the header, closes its write side, reads the answer from one iterator, `refused` → `BlobPeerRefusedError`, then `checkAnswerFits`. `get` resolves on the header; `pieces` reads exactly `held.length` frames then requires end of stream (`truncated` / `extra frame` as `BlobProtocolError`); leaving the loop early aborts the stream. The caller's signal aborts the stream with `signal.reason` and that reason is what the call throws; the deadline aborts with `BlobTransferStalledError`. `wireBytes` counts each sent frame with its prefix and every received chunk as it arrives (the pass-through choice, so no `uint8-varint` dependency).
- **Two helpers added to `blob-frames.ts`**, used by both ends: `abortStreamOnStall(stream, deadline)` (the reader does not watch the deadline itself, so something must abort the stream when it fires) and `releaseBlobFrames(frames, peer)`.
- **`closeBlobStream` now rethrows libp2p's `close` event as its error.** libp2p 3's `close()` rejects with the stream's `close` *Event* (not an Error) when the stream ends while it waits; seen in a throwaway check as `serve:aborted err=[object Event]`, and on the client it would have been thrown to the caller as a non-Error. Duck-typed, since Hermes has no `Event` global.
- **Node wiring** (`libp2p-node-base.ts`): `inboundAuthorizationOf(options)` builds the authorization slice once; it is passed into `storageProtocolServices` (new parameter) and spread into the new `blob` service, which is registered on both roles outside the serving-only spread. `logIgnoredServingOptions` no longer lists the two authorization options. Comment at `preparedStreamProtocols` on why blob is left out. The `servesBlocks` and `authorizeInboundStream` doc comments on `NodeOptions` updated to match.
- Exports added to `index.ts` and `rn.ts`. Module doc of `inbound-authorization.ts` lists the protocols instead of "four".
- **Docs**: `docs/blobs.md` status line and §Moving pieces between machines (built vs to be built, frame sequence, deadlines and the drain rule, what the receiver buffers and why not `pause`, no prepared stream); `docs/internals.md` §Inbound Stream Authorization (five protocols, client node gates only the blob protocol, why blob is not in the prepared-stream pool) and §Client nodes (table row; ignored-options list); `docs/debugging.md` row for `blob:protocol`.

## Tests

- `test/blob-protocol-stalls.spec.ts` (new), two real nodes over loopback TCP, `responseTimeoutMs: 500`:
  - *Header lies*: A's handler is replaced (`force: true`) by one that answers `{kind:'get', held:[true]}` and sends nothing more; B's `get` resolves and iterating `pieces` rejects with `BlobTransferStalledError` in under 3 s (measured ~600 ms).
  - *Receiver stops reading*: B opens a raw stream, pauses, writes a `get` for three 1 MiB pieces held in A's `MemoryPieceStore` under their real raw-leaf ids, closes its write side, never reads; the stream ends with a remote reset in under 3 s (measured ~570 ms). Mutation-checked: with the server's deadline set to 60 s it fails.
- `test/inbound-stream-authorization-node-wiring.spec.ts`: `buildBlobProtocol(PREFIX)` added to `GATED_PROTOCOLS`; "four" wording replaced by the list.
- `test/node-service-set.spec.ts`: the lock spec's literals gain `blob` on every shape, client included (a deliberate change to the service map).
- No happy-path test, per the plan (the fetcher's specs exercise it end to end). A throwaway spec, deleted before handoff, confirmed on real nodes: `stat` of a held and an unknown blob (`whole`, `none`); a `get` of a 3 MiB blob's 13 pieces plus one not held (bytes equal to the store's, `notHeld` correct, `wireBytes.received` above the data size); early `break` (client `received=1`, stream aborted); caller abort (the caller's own reason thrown); `refused: busy` with two paused streams holding the per-peer slots, and the slots released afterwards; a duplicate-id `get` refused locally.

## Departures from the plan, and known gaps

- **Three pieces, not two, in the receiver-stops-reading spec.** yamux doubles a stream's receive window when data arrives within a few round trips, and the protocol negotiation does that on loopback before the requester pauses: in the throwaway check a paused requester's stream was served 716 KB in full. Three 1 MiB pieces exceed any window the negotiation can grow, and stay under the requester's own 4 MiB read-buffer cap (past which it would reset the stream itself and the spec's `local === false` check would catch it).
- **What that spec can and cannot tell apart.** It pins that the responder gives up within one deadline. It cannot distinguish "waited for drain under the deadline" from "queued everything and then stalled in `close`", because `close` also runs under the deadline. The difference is memory, which no test here measures.
- **Malformed covers more than decode errors.** A header frame over its cap and a stream that ends before any request (both `BlobProtocolError` from the reader) are also answered `refused: malformed`, not just `decodeBlobRequest` failures.
- **Client `stat` requires end of stream after its header**, the same rule as `get` (the plan stated it for `get` only); a server that sends anything after a `stat` answer is a protocol error.
- **A `get` with nothing held finishes inside `get`** (requires end of stream and releases the deadline and listener), since a caller has no reason to iterate an empty `pieces`.
- **`pieces` never iterated at all** (with something held): the stream is aborted when the deadline fires; until then the caller's abort listener stays attached. Documented on `BlobGetReply.pieces`.
- **`stat` refs are answered one at a time**, not in parallel: a partial blob's stat walks its tree, and 64 concurrent walks would be work a peer chose. A local `stat` that throws (a corrupt tree) is answered `none` and logged on `:error` as `serve:stat-failed`.
- **The server's deadline also covers its local reads** between frames (`has`, `get`, `stat`), so a store that hangs aborts the stream after one timeout. Measured nothing on slow stores.
- **Allowance is taken after the request is read.** Streams that are open but have not sent a request are bounded only by libp2p's per-connection stream limit and the progress deadline.
- **`BlobProtocolError.peer` for the swept-piece case** is the requester's id, though the broken promise is this node's; the detail says which piece left the store.
- `docs/debugging.md` said `db-p2p` emits 59 sub-namespaces; the section had 64 rows before this change (counted with `awk` over the section, `grep -c '^| \`'`). Now 65.
