# Review: blob bytes are reported by hold, and the cache gets a share

## What landed

Four pieces, all inside `@optimystic/db-p2p`.

**`LocalBlobService.bytesByHold()`** (`src/blob/local-blob-service.ts`) — a new door, `BlobByteRecords`, beside the existing `BlobCacheRecords` / `BlobResponsibleRecords` / `BlobReclaimRecords`, with a `HoldBytes` (`{ bytes, unsized }`) per hold kind. One pass over `readableRecords`, each blob attributed to the ONE hold that keeps it by precedence `pin > owed > responsible > cached` (`HOLD_PRECEDENCE` / `strongestHold`, beside `cacheOnly`). A record carrying no `size` is counted in `unsized` rather than as zero bytes. The precedence, the over-count, and the unreadable-record rule are all stated on the method. The `readableRecords` `NOTE:` now names four callers instead of three.

**`StorageAccount.breakdown()` and `StorageBreakdown`** (`src/storage/storage-account.ts`) — the whole `StorageCapacity`, plus `pieceBytes`, `blocksAndBookkeepingBytes` and `blobBytesByHold`. The blob record source arrives as a thunk, `StorageAllotmentOptions.blobRecords`, so no setter and no mutation. `capacity()` was split into `capacity()` + a pure `capacityOf(scan)` so `breakdown()` builds both off one scan. The blocks-and-bookkeeping tripwire `NOTE:` sits on its field.

**`cacheBudgetBytes` and `CACHE_ALLOTMENT_SHARE`** (same module) — one pure function, `min(declared cap, total × ⅛, total − (used − cached))`, floored at 0, with an unknown total answering the declared cap unchanged. The two reasons for an eighth (256 MiB is an eighth of 2 GiB; 12.5 % cannot reach the 85 % `moveOut` threshold) are on the constant.

**A budget asked for per run** (`src/blob/cache-budget.ts`) — `BlobCacheBudgetDeps.budgetBytes` is now a function asked once per `runOnce`. `sizedCacheBytes(blobs)` is exported and computed once by `runOnce`, which then asks for the budget with that figure and hands both to `chooseEvictions`. `libp2p-node-base.ts` wires both thunks on the one common path (serving and client alike): the account gets `blobRecords: () => blobs`, and the budget gets `async cachedBytes => cacheBudgetBytes({ declaredBytes, capacity: await storageAccount.capacity(), cachedBytes })`.

Docs: `docs/blobs.md` §Holds' `cached` row and §Lifetime's budget bullet, `NodeOptions.blobs.cacheBudgetBytes`'s comment and `DEFAULT_BLOB_CACHE_BUDGET_BYTES`'s (both now say "cap"), and `packages/db-p2p/docs/storage.md` §9 (three new subsections: what the bytes are, the attribution's over-count, what `breakdown()` costs, and the cache as a tenant of the allotment).

## Where the ticket's shape was changed, and why

Read these first — they are the parts a reviewer is most likely to disagree with.

- **`budgetBytes` takes `cachedBytes`.** The ticket declared `budgetBytes: () => Promise<number>` but also said to "ask for the budget with that figure", and `cacheBudgetBytes` needs `cachedBytes` for its third term. A no-argument thunk cannot supply it without measuring the cache a second time, which the ticket explicitly rules out. So the signature is `(cachedBytes: number) => Promise<number>`.
- **`CacheEvictionChoice.cachedBytes` is gone.** With the sum lifted into `sizedCacheBytes`, `runOnce` owns the figure and returning it from `chooseEvictions` would just pass a caller's own value back. `CacheEnforcement.cachedBytes` is unchanged and still reported.
- **`cacheBudgetBytes` returns a whole number of bytes.** The share term is fractional for most allotments (⅛ of 1 GiB is exact, ⅛ of a `statfs` figure is not); `Math.floor` over the minimum keeps the budget a byte count. Not in the ticket.
- **`pieceBytes` can be read by `breakdown()` itself.** The memoized scan asks the piece store for its figure exactly when the block store does NOT already measure the pieces; in the other case `breakdown()` asks the piece store directly. Making the scan always ask would have added a `stat`-per-piece walk (`FilePieceStore.bytesUsed`) to every `capacity()` — a call ring selection makes several times per operation — which is why `FilePieceStore`'s own `NOTE:` says a node sharing the base path never calls it. The consequence is honest but worth checking: in that case the piece figure may be newer than the use it is subtracted from by up to the memo's 60 s TTL. Stated on the method. A piece-store rejection is caught and logged and leaves `pieceBytes` absent, the rule `freeBytes` already follows, which is also why the field is optional.
- **`StorageScan` gained `blockBytes`.** Needed so `blocksAndBookkeepingBytes` can distinguish "the blocks came to 0" from "nothing measured the blocks" — the latter leaves the field absent, per the ticket's "absent when either is unknown".
- **`StorageAllotmentOptions.blobRecords` sits in the host-facing options type**, as the ticket said ("in its options"), beside the `now` and `usedBytesCacheTtlMs` seams. The node spreads `{ ...options.storageAllotment, blobRecords: () => blobs }`, so wiring always wins over anything a host passed. Worth a reviewer's opinion on whether a second constructor parameter would read better.

## Known gaps

- **`breakdown()` has no caller.** The ticket named an operator's dashboard and `logRingInfo` in reference-peer as its consumers, but listed neither in `files:` nor in the TODO, so nothing calls it yet. It is tested only through its parts (see below).
- **The attribution does not reconcile with the piece figure, by design.** Shared pieces count in both blobs and a partly held blob counts at full size. Both are stated on `bytesByHold` and in storage.md §9. Anyone building the dashboard must not subtract one from the other.
- **In `libp2p-node-base.ts` the blob service happens to be built *before* the account**, so `() => blobs` could have been `blobs`. The thunk is still what landed — the ticket asked for it, and it keeps the wiring correct if that order ever changes. The comment at the site says so.

## Tests

Three added, as specified; nothing else.

| Test | File | What it verifies |
|---|---|---|
| `cacheBudgetBytes` → "takes the smallest of the declared cap, the share of the allotment and what the allotment leaves" | `test/storage-account.spec.ts` | Five table rows over the pure function: the declared cap wins on a 64 GiB allotment, the share wins on 1 GiB, what the allotment leaves wins when 1000 MiB of 1 GiB is already spent, a node over its allotment floors to 0, and an unknown total falls back to the declared cap. This is the whole of the new policy. |
| `cacheBudgetBytes` → "leaves the cache its share when every measured byte IS the cache" | `test/storage-account.spec.ts` | The leftover term subtracts the cache's own bytes: a node whose entire 1 GiB use is cache gets the full 128 MiB share, not 0. The sign error this catches would quietly disable the cache on every node. |
| `LocalBlobService.bytesByHold` → "counts a blob with two holds once, under the stronger" | `test/blob-cache-budget.spec.ts` | Over a real `LocalBlobService`: a `responsible` + `cached` blob appears only under `responsible`, with one cache-only and one pinned blob beside it so a double count would show as a doubled figure, and `owed` reporting `{ bytes: 0, unsized: 0 }`. |

Deliberately untested, per the ticket: the breakdown's assembly (arithmetic over figures `capacity()` already pins), the thunk plumbing (`tsc`), and the doc wording.

## What to try by hand

- A node on memory storage (no allotment, no free-space report) must keep the flat 256 MiB budget exactly — that is the "unknown total" arm, and it is why every existing memory-storage spec was unaffected.
- A node with `storageAllotment.bytes` set small (say 512 MiB) and a cache full of fetched blobs: the budget should land at 64 MiB, and lowering the allotment should take effect at the next read that fetches rather than at a restart.
- `bytesByHold()` on a node that has put, pinned, fetched and been made responsible for blobs: the four figures should sum to no more than the node's blob records' sizes, and the `cached` figure should match what an enforcement reports as `cachedBytes` when every cache-only blob is sized.

## Review hints

- The deviations above, in order — especially the `budgetBytes` signature and the `pieceBytes` read.
- Whether `Math.max(0, capacity.usedBytes - pieceBytes)`'s floor hides anything worth seeing (it fires only when a backend reports a total smaller than its own pieces').
- Whether `sizedCacheBytes`'s `blob.bytes ?? 0` is the same sum the old `sized.reduce(…)` made — it is, but it now folds the unsized blobs with a `?? 0` rather than filtering them out first, and `isSized` is still what the eviction walk uses.
