## What changed

Quereus 5.4 sends `request.limit` / `request.offset` for `order by … limit n` (constant: `tryLimitedSortAbsorb`; `limit ?`: one request per bound, `tryRuntimeLimitChoice` / `largestOrderedBound`; all through `probeAccessPlan`, which re-asks without the limit when the chosen plan leaves a filter unclaimed). Nothing of the limit reaches `query()`. The plugin's planner now uses it:

- **One licence rule** — `rowsReadUnderLimit(request, rows, deliversOrdering, handledFilters)`: `min(rows, limit + offset)` when a limit was sent, the candidate delivers the required ordering (or none is required), and it claims every filter; `rows` otherwise. Mirrors `rowsToProduce` in `@quereus/quereus-store/src/common/store-module-access-plan.ts`, except it does not clamp to 1 (so `limit 0` costs one descent). It sizes the per-row cost term only; every candidate's `rows` stays the cardinality.
- **Walk with no filter** — `unfilteredWalkCost`: `seekCost + ROW_FETCH_COST(5) · min(wanted, 256)`, plus `tableScanCost + sortCost(tableRowCount)` when `wanted > 256`. `tableScanCost` now rides in `PlanPricing`. Consequences: a page of ≤ 256 rows is walked; an unlimited ordered read of a table over 256 rows (an un-analyzed table is assumed to hold 1000) is a scan and sort; a `limit ?` gets a `LimitChoiceNode` bound of at most 256.
- **Walk under residual filters** — unchanged price, `seekCost + tableRowCount`, ignoring any limit. Recorded as an accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` on `orderedWalkCandidate` with the revisit condition (backlog `feat-an-ordered-walk-tests-its-own-residual-filters`).
- **Equality and range seeks** — their row term goes through `rowsReadUnderLimit`. A range with a second same-side bound or another column's filter stays unlicensed.
- **`WALK_ROWS_BEFORE_SCAN`** stays for every walk and range; its doc no longer claims the limit never reaches the module, and its `NOTE:` carries the new revisit condition (lift the switch and drop the scan term if Quereus ever forwards a licensed `limit + offset` to `query()`). The walk does no explicit truncation, and the doc comment on `orderedWalkCandidate` says why (`inEngineOrder` reorders runs).
- Every `feat-sort-absorb-blind-to-limit-general` citation is gone (code, the spec comment, docs). `yarn lint:docs` is clean.

## Known gaps — reviewer please weigh

1. **A spelled-out `nulls first` page regressed to scan and sort.** `sortKeysAsTableOrdering` in `@quereus/quereus/src/planner/rules/retrieve/rule-grow-retrieve.ts` refuses to absorb a Sort with any explicit `nulls`, so `order by t nulls first limit 20` reaches the module only through the physical pass, which carries the ordering but no limit. With a filter it carries no ordering at all, and that part was already so. Before this ticket the unlimited price still chose the walk; now, on a table over 256 rows, the engine sorts it. Verified with a probe of the requests the engine sends. Recorded as a `NOTE:` on `orderedWalkCandidate` and in *Pricing*. The fix belongs in Quereus (treat explicit `nulls first` as the default it is), which is outside this repo, so I did not file anything. Decide whether it merits a `blocked/` ticket.
2. **Small un-analyzed tables now sort.** An un-analyzed table is priced at 1000 rows, so an unlimited no-filter ordered read of, say, 42 rows is a scan and sort where it used to walk. That is correct under the agreed rule, and both are cheap at that size. I did not measure it.
3. **Seeks are still priced at 1 a row** although they fetch like a walk (about 5). Out of scope by decision; stated in *Pricing* as a known approximation.
4. **Candidates outside the helper.** The full scan, the partial-PK scan and the PK multi-seek do not go through `rowsReadUnderLimit`. By inspection this does not matter: the scan claims no filter and delivers no ordering whenever another candidate competes, and the multi-seek's cost has no per-row term. Check that you agree.
5. `index-ordered-walk.spec.ts` now depends on `analyze` working on an Optimystic table (it does, and `alter-table-arms.spec.ts` already relies on it).
