Second of three tickets for stage 2 of [docs/offline-sync.md §Implementation stages](../../docs/offline-sync.md#implementation-stages); the design is [§Rollback and savepoints only touch the open segment](../../docs/offline-sync.md#rollback-and-savepoints-only-touch-the-open-segment). Implemented as the ticket specified, with one structural choice in the coordinator and one open question for the reviewer, both below.

## What changed

**The snapshot.** `CollectionSnapshot` keeps `transforms` (the whole tracker, sealed effects included, which `Tree.readView` builds its committed view from), `pins` and `context`; `pending` is now documented as the open segment only (it always was, since `act` only ever appended there), and a new optional `sealedVersion` records `SegmentList.version` at capture. `snapshotPending` fills it. A hand-built snapshot without it reads as "unchanged".

**The restore.** `Collection.restorePending` is `async` and takes one of the arms in the ticket's table, from `sealedChanged` (captured version differs from the list's), `boundaryMoved` (unchanged test) and `hasSealed()`:

- sealed changed, or boundary moved while anything is sealed: **replay** — set the open segment to the snapshot's actions, `await replayActions()`. Pins are not restored; the replay's reset clears them and every re-staged update re-pins from the cache as it is now.
- boundary moved with nothing sealed: today's two arms byte for byte (empty captured open segment resets the tracker empty; non-empty restores verbatim, with its NOTE kept).
- otherwise: **verbatim**, exactly today's code, pins included.

The method comment carries the arm table, the per-arm pins statement against [docs/internals.md §Staged Edits Keep Their Base](../../docs/internals.md#staged-edits-keep-their-base), the latch-free contract, and that an open-segment refusal inside the replay arm propagates (it is `replayActions`' existing behaviour for the open segment; nothing swallows it).

**Callers, all awaiting.** `Tree.restore` is `async`. In `TransactionCoordinator` all five sites await: the `commitOnceLatched` catch, its partial-commit arm, its empty-committed loop, `rollback`, and the `execute` failed arm. The `execute` NOTE now says the committed fold stays await-free and the failed restore is awaited under the held latches, as the ticket asked. In the Quereus bridge `DirtyTree.restore` returns `Promise<void>`; `restoreUncommittedTrees`, the legacy sweep's partial-restore loop, `rollbackTransaction` and `rollbackToSavepoint` await each restore, and `rollbackToSavepoint` plus `OptimysticVirtualTableConnection.rollbackToSavepoint` are `async`. `createSavepoint` and `releaseSavepoint` stay synchronous.

**Quereus awaits the hook (confirmed by reading the engine).** `VirtualTableConnection.rollbackToSavepoint` is typed `MaybePromise<void>` (`packages/quereus-plugin-optimystic/node_modules/@quereus/quereus/dist/src/vtab/connection.d.ts`). The one engine site that calls it, `_rollbackToSavepointBroadcast` in `dist/src/core/database.js`, does `for (const connection of this.getAllConnections()) { await connection.rollbackToSavepoint(depth); }`, so connections are restored one after another, never concurrently. Its callers all await it: the `ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT` statement emitter (`dist/src/runtime/emit/transaction.js`), `_rollbackAndReleaseSavepointBroadcast` (which the DML executor awaits at both of its statement- and row-savepoint failure sites, `dist/src/runtime/emit/dml-executor.js`), and the external-change path in `dist/src/core/database-external-changes.js`. So no statement runs until every connection's restore has resolved. One nuance for the reviewer: `_rollbackAndReleaseSavepointBroadcast` wraps the rollback broadcast in a swallowing try/catch, so a refusal thrown out of the replay arm during a statement-level savepoint rollback is swallowed by the engine there and the statement's own error is what the caller sees; the user-level `ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT` statement propagates it.

**One structural choice: two passes in the partial-commit arms.** `commitOnceLatched`'s partial-commit arm and `execute`'s failed arm used to interleave, per participant, "fold if committed, else restore". Both now fold every committed participant first in one await-free pass and restore the failed ones after, awaited. The success fold's event-loop atomicity across collections is a documented promise (docs/transactions.md "Session-mode publish is event-loop-atomic", and the `readCommittedSnapshot` audit in the module), and an await between two committed folds would have broken it for the partial case. Each participant's handling is independent of the others', so the order change has no other effect; the reviewer should confirm that reading.

**Docs.** `docs/internals.md` §Staged actions are segments has a fifth rule with the arm table in prose, the pins statement per arm, the propagating refusal, the awaiting callers and the two-pass note; the fourth rule's tail no longer calls the version-aware restore a future ticket. The *Staged Edits Keep Their Base* bullet that said the snapshot "carries and restores" pins now says "on the verbatim arm" and names what the replay arm does instead; the guards section's rollback paragraph (the other `restorePending` mention) now says the empty reset applies with nothing sealed and that a sealed segment replays instead. `docs/offline-sync.md` §Rollback and savepoints gained two sentences, because the implementation refines the section: a moved boundary over a sealed segment replays too, and the restore is asynchronous with a propagating open refusal. `yarn lint:docs` passes (48 documents, all resolve).

## Tests

All in `packages/db-core/test/collection-segments.spec.ts`, under "a snapshot covers the open segment only", over `Tree` and `TestTransactor` with real rival commits:

- **restoring across a dropped segment replays what remains** — the double-commit contract. Seed, seal E1 (insert) and E2 (update of the committed row), snapshot (the `markDirty` moment: `pending` empty, `sealedVersion` 2), stage an open action, `dropSealed(E1)`, restore. Asserts E1 is gone from `sealedEntries()` and from reads, the open action is gone, E2 is re-staged, a new snapshot's `sealedVersion` differs, the tracker's transforms deep-equal a fresh handle that stages E2 alone (valid because E2 is update-only, so no random block ids enter), `stagedBaseRevs` names the committed revision for every updated block with `movedBases()` empty (the pins-through-the-replay-arm assertion), and `tree.readView(snapshot).get(1)` still sees E1's entry after E1 was dropped live.
- **restoring while a refused segment was removed keeps the later segment refused** — E1 inserts under `absent`, E2 updates that row under `unchanged`, snapshot, stage, `dropSealed(E1)` refuses E2 (callback fires once), restore. Asserts E2 is still refused by the same error object, the callback fired exactly once (the restore's replay skipped it rather than retrying), the open action is gone and the committed row reads through.
- **restoring an invented collection across a dropped segment replays from its baseline** — invented tree, seal E1, snapshot, stage, drop, restore. Asserts still invented, nothing sealed, both rows gone, the header still staged, and a later stage reads back (the tree is still readable and writable).
- **a refusal of the restored open segment inside the replay arm propagates** — seal E1, outer snapshot, stage a guarded insert, inner snapshot carrying it, restore the outer (verbatim arm), seal E2, rival takes the guarded key, refresh adopts it cleanly, restore the inner. Asserts `TreeKeyTakenError` escapes `restorePending`, the sealed callback never fired, both sealed segments are listed and re-staged ahead of the refusal, and the rival's row reads.

Existing specs changed only by `await`s: `collection.spec.ts` (two `restorePending` calls), `session-mode-commit.spec.ts` (two `Tree.restore` calls), `adapter-integration.spec.ts` (eight direct `rollbackToSavepoint` calls, the bare-bridge no-throw test now awaits the async rollback so a rejection still fails it, and four inline `DirtyTree` doubles' `restore` made `async` for the `Promise<void>` contract). No test was skipped or loosened.

Runs: db-core suite 1946 passing; Quereus plugin suite 1064 passing, 14 pending (the pre-existing env-gated ones), 0 failing; `yarn typecheck` clean; `yarn lint:docs` clean. No pre-existing failure was met.

## Gaps and questions for the reviewer

- **A throw out of the replay arm stops the caller's loop.** `rollback`, `rollbackTransaction` and `rollbackToSavepoint` each await restores in a loop and let the first throw escape, leaving the later collections of that loop un-restored (and, for the bridge, its per-transaction state not torn down). Reachability is narrow: in the coordinator's commit spans the replay arm cannot fire (under the latches nothing seals or drops a segment, and a failed participant's boundary never moves); for a whole-transaction rollback the restored open segment is empty, so the replay can only record sealed refusals, never throw; only a mid-transaction savepoint capture (`rollbackToSavepoint`) carrying earlier statements' actions can meet it, and needs a sealed change or a sealed segment across a moved boundary plus a rival. I left the loops as first-throw-propagates rather than adopting the refresh's visit-every-collection-then-throw pattern, since the state after an open refusal is already "abort the transaction" (the `updateInternal` note on a throw out of `replayActions`), and the third ticket's queue cascade may want a say. Worth a decision.
- **The pins assertion is weaker than the ticket's wording.** "Names the cache's current revision" is asserted as "equals `committedRevision()`", which coincide in that scenario because every block is at the seed commit's revision; the cache's per-block revision is private to the collection. If the reviewer wants the exact form, expose a read-only probe.
- **Nested savepoints at different depths** are covered by inspection only (each snapshot carries its own version; the fourth test uses an outer/inner pair but only to set up the open-refusal shape).
- **A snapshot taken while a head commit is in flight** is the third ticket's test, as the ticket says; `dropSealed` stands in for the head commit here and both bump the version.
- `docs/transactions.md`'s "Session-mode publish is event-loop-atomic" sentence describes the success fold, which is untouched, so it was not edited; the partial arms' two-pass note lives in `docs/internals.md`.
