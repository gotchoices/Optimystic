# Reactivity: tiers below the root use the stable collection anchor

The third paragraph of the decision accepted on 2026-10-02 (recorded in `reactivity-notifications-need-the-tail-cohort-to-be-the-topic-cohort`), still to be designed into implement tickets once that ticket lands:

> **Tiers below the root do not rotate.** Tiers `d ≥ 1` are addressed by a stable collection anchor, `collectionTopicId = H(collectionId ‖ "reactivity")`: `coord_d(P) = H(d ‖ prefix(H(P), d·log₂F) ‖ collectionTopicId)`. Only the root moves when the tail rotates. A rotation therefore re-links at most `F` tier-1 child cohorts (and the root's own direct subscribers) to the new root, not every subscriber in the tree. A notification still names its `tailId`, and its signature still verifies against the root group of that tail.

Accepted costs to restate in `docs/reactivity.md` § Anchor: tier-1 cohorts sit at fixed positions for the collection's life (load spread over `F` cohorts and bounded by the cohort-topic intake caps — a weaker form of § Anchor's "no permanent target" argument; the root, which carries every notification, still rotates).

## Why it matters

Every tier's address includes the topic id, and the topic id `H(tailId ‖ "reactivity")` changes every 32 commits, so each rotation moves the whole tree and every subscriber re-registers. At 10 commits/s and 10,000 subscribers that is about 3,000 registrations per second against a new root that admits `cap_promote_fast` = 32 per window, and on a live node the successor tail cannot be announced in advance (block ids are random; deterministic ids are backlog `6.5-block-id-derivation`), so subscribers learn of each rotation only from the watch service's 30 s tail check. On a busy collection, network watch degrades to 30 s polling.

## The design question this plan must settle first: the topic's identity

Addressing tiers `d ≥ 1` by `collectionTopicId` is not enough on its own: cohort-topic registration records, the node's subscriber registry, forwarder push state, the rotation drain gate and the recover redirect are all keyed by the topic id. If the identity stays `H(tailId ‖ "reactivity")`, a tier-1 cohort's records still change identity at every rotation and every subscriber still re-registers (to the same cohort, but N registrations all the same), which defeats the stated purpose.

**Recommended:** the reactivity topic id becomes `collectionTopicId` at every tier, and the root is located purely by the root key (the tail's routing key, which the root-placement work already carries on every frame). A rotation is then "same topic, the root moved", which is what the accepted text describes. `H(tailId ‖ "reactivity")` is retired. Verify this reading against the accepted text before planning further; it changes no wire field beyond what the root-placement work added.

Consequences to plan (each currently assumes a new topic per tail):

- **Subscriber side.** `ReactivityCollectionWatch.moveTo` builds a new manager per tail topic and compares topic keys; `ReactivitySubscriptionManager` derives `topicId` from `tailIdAtAttach`; `ReactivitySubscriberRegistry` is keyed by topic. With a stable topic, a tail change re-registers only a subscription attached at the root (`treeTier 0` on its handle); one attached at `d ≥ 1` stays put.
- **Forwarder side.** `ReactivityForwarderHost`'s push state, `markRotated` drain gate and `rotationRedirectFor` are keyed by topic. A node in both the old and the new root group must keep serving; a node only in the old one drains. Key the drain on (topic, tail) or on "this node no longer applies the collection's tail".
- **Recover.** `staleTopic = reactivityTopicId(latestKnownTailId)` in `packages/db-p2p/src/reactivity/recover-transport.ts` and the redirect logic move to (topic, tail).
- **db-core rotation** (`packages/db-core/src/reactivity/rotation.ts`) derives `newTopicId` from the new tail; it becomes "new root key".
- **Re-linking tier-1 children** to the new root needs multi-tier fan-out, which does not exist yet (`feat-reactivity-notifications-reach-child-cohorts`). Plan the identity and addressing now; leave re-link to that ticket, and say so.

## Folded in: tell the root's direct subscribers about a move

Previously backlog `feat-reactivity-rotation-reaches-current-subscribers` (retired into this ticket). After this change only the root's direct subscribers have to move, but they still hear nothing from the new root until the watch tick notices the new tail (up to about 30 s, once per 32 commits). The rollover commit rewrites the old tail block's `nextId`, so the old tail's storage group sees the rotation (`ReactivityOriginationManager` detects the `tailId` change and calls `markRotated`); a node in that group could forward the first new-tail notification, unmodified, to its direct subscribers during the drain window (`T_drain`, 60 s), and a subscriber seeing a different `tailId` already detects the move (`detectRotation` → `RotationNotice`). Decide whether this is still worth doing once only root subscribers move: its saving is one notification's delay per 32 commits, its cost is a second fan-out set during the drain window, and it only reaches subscribers held by a node that applied the rollover.
