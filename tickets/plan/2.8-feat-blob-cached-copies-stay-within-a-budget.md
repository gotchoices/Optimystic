# Cached blob copies stay within a budget

docs/blobs.md §Holds lists four holds that keep a blob on a machine. One of them, `cached`, is taken by `feat-blob-pieces-move-between-machines` when a read fetches pieces from another machine, and nothing releases it, so a machine that reads many files grows its storage without bound. docs/blobs.md §Lifetime says: "Cached blobs are evicted under a budget, least recently read first. This is local and needs no agreement." This ticket builds that: a byte budget for blobs held only for reads, enforced after every read that fetched and once at start, which removes the `cached` hold from the least recently read blobs until the total is within the budget and then runs the piece sweep. A blob with a read in progress is never evicted, and the sweep never deletes the pieces of a blob being read.

It is independent of the reference pass (`feat-unreferenced-blobs-are-reclaimed`), which releases `responsible` and `owed` holds; that ticket reuses the log namespace and the in-progress-read protection added here.

## Design

### What counts against the budget

A **cache-only blob** is one whose record holds `cached` and no other hold (no pin, no `owed`, no `responsible`). Only cache-only blobs count and only they are evicted: a blob that also has another hold stays whatever its cache status, so counting it would make the budget evict other blobs to pay for one it cannot remove. A blob's bytes are the record's `size` (known once the root piece is here); a record with no size counts 0. Pieces shared between blobs are counted once per blob, which over-counts and evicts sooner; that errs toward less storage, which is the purpose of the budget.

Rejected: measuring with the piece store's `bytesUsed` or walking each blob's tree for its present pieces. The first counts every blob, held or not; the second costs one piece-store read per piece per enforcement. The record size is already there.

### Module `packages/db-p2p/src/blob/cache-budget.ts`

```ts
/** 256 MiB: a phone's worth. A host with room sets more through `NodeOptions.blobs.cacheBudgetBytes`. */
export const DEFAULT_BLOB_CACHE_BUDGET_BYTES = 256 * 1024 * 1024;

/** One cache-only blob as the budget sees it. */
export interface CachedBlob {
	readonly ref: BlobRef;
	readonly bytes: number;		// record size, 0 when unknown
	readonly lastReadAt: number;	// the record's last-read time, 0 when never read
}

/** Which of `blobs` to evict to bring their total within `budgetBytes`, least recently read first
 * (ties by `ref` ascending), skipping any for which `inUse` is true. Pure. */
export function chooseEvictions(blobs: readonly CachedBlob[], budgetBytes: number, inUse: (ref: BlobRef) => boolean): {
	evict: BlobRef[];
	cachedBytes: number;			// total before eviction
	skippedInUse: number;
};

export interface BlobCacheBudgetDeps {
	records: BlobRecordStore;
	budgetBytes: number;
	/** `LocalBlobService.inUse`. */
	inUse: (ref: BlobRef) => boolean;
	/** `LocalBlobService.sweep`. */
	sweep: () => Promise<void>;
	log: Logger;
}

export interface CacheEnforcement {
	cachedBytes: number;
	evicted: BlobRef[];
	skippedInUse: number;
}

export class BlobCacheBudget {
	constructor(deps: BlobCacheBudgetDeps);
	/** Enumerate cache-only records, choose, remove each chosen blob's `cached` hold, then sweep once
	 * if anything was evicted. Single-flight: a call while one runs returns a promise for one more run
	 * after it (several such calls share that one run). */
	enforce(): Promise<CacheEnforcement>;
	/** `void enforce()` with a logged catch, for call sites that must not wait. */
	request(): void;
}
```

`chooseEvictions` walks the sorted list, and for each blob while `cachedBytes - evictedBytes > budgetBytes`: skip it if `inUse(ref)` (count it), otherwise add it to `evict`. A budget it cannot meet because the rest are in use is left over budget until the next enforcement.

Removing the hold goes through the record store's own hold-removal operation, with a condition evaluated on the record as it is when written: the record still holds `cached` and nothing else. A blob that gained a pin, `owed` or `responsible` hold between the enumeration and the write keeps every hold. When the removal leaves a record with no holds, the record store deletes the record, as `feat-a-node-stores-blob-pieces` defines, and the pieces are left for the sweep. `inUse` is checked again immediately before each removal, so a read that started after `chooseEvictions` ran keeps its blob.

### Reads in progress (`LocalBlobService`)

```ts
/** Whether a `read` or `fetch` of `ref` is running on this node. */
inUse(ref: BlobRef): boolean;
/** Count a use of `ref` until the returned function is called; call it exactly once. */
beginUse(ref: BlobRef): () => void;
```

A `Map<BlobRef, number>` of uses. Every public `read` and `fetch` of `LocalBlobService` and of `NodeBlobService` holds a use for its whole duration. An async generator's body starts on its first `next()`, so `read` takes the use as the first statement of its body, before any `await`, and releases it in a `finally` around the rest; `finally` runs on completion, on a throw, and when a `for await` consumer breaks (which calls `return`). `fetch` does the same around its body. `NodeBlobService`'s network read and fetch take the use themselves before calling down, so a blob being fetched from another machine is in use from its first piece.

**The sweep protects blobs in use.** The sweep from `feat-a-node-stores-blob-pieces` keeps every piece in the tree of every held blob, plus the pieces of `put`s in progress. Add the refs for which `inUse` is true to the roots it walks. This closes the one race the budget creates: the budget removes the last hold of a blob, a read of it starts, and the sweep then runs; the sweep walks the in-use blob's tree from its reference through the pieces present and deletes none of them.

### Triggers

- `NodeBlobService`: after a `read` or `fetch` that took a `cached` hold has released its use, call `budget.request()`. A read that fetched nothing adds nothing to the cache and does not trigger.
- Node start: once, after the blob service is built, `budget.request()`, so a budget lowered across a restart is applied.

No timer: the cache grows only through reads that fetch.

### Node option

`NodeOptions.blobs.cacheBudgetBytes?: number`, added to the `blobs` option object the earlier tickets introduced; default `DEFAULT_BLOB_CACHE_BUDGET_BYTES`. `0` keeps no cache-only blob beyond those being read. A value that is not a finite integer of at least 0 makes `createLibp2pNodeBase` throw a `RangeError` naming the option, before the node starts. `createLibp2pNodeBase` builds one `BlobCacheBudget` beside the blob service and hands `NodeBlobService` its `request`.

### Logging

`createLogger('blob:reclaim', node.peerId.toString())`, namespace `optimystic:db-p2p:blob:reclaim` (shared with the reference pass of `feat-unreferenced-blobs-are-reclaimed`):

- `cache:enforced cachedBytes=%d budget=%d evicted=%d skippedInUse=%d` — once per enforcement that found the cache over budget.
- `cache:evicted ref=%s bytes=%d lastReadAt=%d` — once per hold removed.
- `cache:enforce-failed - %e` — `request`'s catch.

## Edge cases & interactions

- **Read in progress.** Never chosen, and re-checked immediately before the hold is removed. Test.
- **Read starting after the hold is removed.** The sweep walks the in-use blob's tree and deletes none of its pieces; the read finishes. Inspection of the sweep roots.
- **Abandoned iterator.** A consumer that stops calling `next()` without calling `return` never runs the `finally`, so the blob stays in use until the process ends. Record this as a `NOTE:` at `beginUse`: harmless while consumers use `for await` or call `return`; if a cache that never shrinks is seen, add a time limit on uses.
- **Blob with another hold.** Neither counted nor evicted; its `cached` hold stays, and counts again once the other hold is released. Test (inside the eviction test).
- **Hold gained between enumeration and removal.** The conditional removal keeps every hold. Inspection.
- **Partial cache-only blob.** Counted at its record size and evictable like any other; a later read fetches again. Inspection.
- **Equal last-read times.** Ordered by reference, so the choice is deterministic. Inspection of `chooseEvictions`.
- **Budget 0.** Every cache-only blob not in use is evicted after each fetching read. Inspection.
- **Concurrent enforcements.** Single-flight; a request during a run gets one more run. Inspection.
- **Crash during enforcement.** A removed hold whose record write did not reach disk is removed again next time; a deleted record whose pieces were not swept leaves orphans the next sweep removes. Nothing is lost that a hold still names. Inspection.
- **Large record counts.** Each enforcement enumerates every record: `NOTE:` at `enforce` — if record counts reach the tens of thousands and enforcement shows in profiles, keep a running total of cache-only bytes in the record store.

## Tests

`packages/db-p2p/test/blob-cache-budget.spec.ts`, over a real `LocalBlobService` with a `MemoryPieceStore` and a real blob record store on a `MemoryKVStore`. A cache-only blob is made by `put` (pinned `default`), adding a `cached` hold and a last-read time through the record store, then `unpin(ref, 'default')`.

- **Least recently read cache-only blobs are evicted until within budget.** Four cache-only blobs of 300 KiB with last-read times 1, 2, 3, 4, and one blob with `cached` and a pin read at 0; budget 700 KiB. `enforce()` evicts the blobs read at 1 and 2 only; their records are gone and their pieces are not in the piece store after the sweep; the other three blobs still read back whole with `from: 'here'`. Pins the ordering, the cache-only rule and the hand-off to the sweep.
- **A blob being read is not evicted.** Two cache-only blobs over a budget that holds one; open a `read` of the least recently read one and take its first chunk; `enforce()` evicts the other and reports `skippedInUse: 1`; finish the read, `enforce()` again evicts the first.
