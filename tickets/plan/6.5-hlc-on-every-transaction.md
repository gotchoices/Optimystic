# A hybrid logical clock on every transaction

This is Stage 1 of the migration path in [docs/crdt-sync.md](../../docs/crdt-sync.md) §Migration Path, turned into a design pass. It is the first foundation of the partition-healing program the plan pass of 2026-11-01 decomposed the old `6.5-partition-healing` ticket into; the other foundation is plan `a-transaction-carries-its-lane`, and the stage that consumes both is backlog `feat-replay-mode-collections-merge-divergent-histories`. The doc's own rule applies: nothing past this stage starts until this one has shipped and been measured.

## Why now, rather than on the trigger the doc names

`docs/crdt-sync.md` §Migration Path says to start when a hot collection shows contention sharding cannot resolve. That trigger has not fired. A different one has: small groups (a phone and its backup, two phones over a relay) producing **forked collections**, two copies under one collection id naming different actions at the same revisions, with no way to merge them. The maintainer's decision of 2026-09-15 names this layer as the fix for that, ahead of any rule at the solo-write path. The doc's trigger paragraph is amended by this pass to say so. The field evidence itself is distilled in the replay-mode backlog ticket; this ticket needs only its conclusion, that a deterministic order across machines is the first thing a merge needs.

## What exists

- A `TransactionStamp` carries `peerId` and `timestamp` (wall clock, milliseconds), fixed at BEGIN, hashed into the stamp id. Only coordinator-path writes have a stamp.
- Every log entry carries a `timestamp`, which since the "fixed function of the write and the revision" rule (`docs/internals.md` §Key Invariants) is **per write**: minted once beside the action id, re-sent verbatim by every retry of that write (`WriteIdentity`), supplied by the offline queue for a head commit. A bare `Collection.sync` write has no stamp and no peer id on its entry.
- Nothing orders entries across machines today; the log-tail cohort sequences them, and the revision number is the order.

## What this stage adds

An HLC, the Kulkarni-Demirbas construction in the doc: `{ wallMs, logical, nodeId }`, where `wallMs` is the max of the local clock and the highest HLC the node has observed, `logical` breaks ties within one millisecond, and `nodeId` is the issuing peer id. Causality falls out: a node cannot issue an HLC below any it has observed, so a change built on something it read sorts after it. Stage 1 **records** the HLC on every transaction and every log entry and changes no ordering, no admission, no visibility.

## Questions this pass must settle

1. **Where the HLC lives.** Recommendation: on the log entry, as the thing that replaces `LogEntry.timestamp`'s role as the per-write identity (the entry is the unit a merge orders, and bare syncs have no stamp), and on the stamp for coordinator-path writes so the transaction id covers it. The two must agree for one write. Decide whether `timestamp` stays as a plain field beside `hlc` for readers that want wall time, or whether `hlc.wallMs` is that field.
2. **Who supplies `nodeId` for a bare sync.** The collection has no peer id. The transactor does (`NetworkTransactor` knows self; `TestTransactor` can carry a fixed one). Recommendation: the transactor exposes an `HlcClock` and the collection asks it for the write's HLC when it mints the write identity, so the clock and the identity are minted together and re-sent together.
3. **How the last-observed HLC is tracked.** One clock per node, in db-core (pure, injectable), advanced by: every log entry a refresh walks, every entry a chain read returns, every consensus record a `ClusterMember` receives (db-p2p feeds it), and every HLC this node issues. Decide the feed points and make the clock the only minting site.
4. **Restart.** A node whose wall clock is behind at restart would issue an HLC below ones it issued before, unless the last-observed value survives the restart. Recommendation: persist the clock's high-water mark in the node's store on every advance that moves `wallMs`, or lazily with a bump on restart; weigh the write cost against the store's write group.
5. **Clock skew.** The doc's open question: should members refuse an HLC whose `wallMs` is far from their own clock? Recommendation for Stage 1: record and log the gap (`hlc:skew`) on every record a member receives, refuse nothing; the refusal is Stage 2's decision, and the measured gaps are what it decides from. This is the "measured" half of "shipped and measured".
6. **Wire and fixtures.** The log entry is persisted in blocks and read by the upgrade-check suite against fixtures older releases wrote. An entry without the field must read as `{ wallMs: timestamp, logical: 0, nodeId: unknown }` or the change documents an upgrade step and retires the fixture per `packages/upgrade-check/readme.md`. Decide which, and whether `nodeId` may be absent on legacy entries.
7. **Offline queue semantics.** A queued entry's HLC is minted at enqueue, when the user made the change, not at drain. Under a later HLC-ordered merge that puts the change where the user made it, which is the local-first semantics `docs/offline-sync.md` §Relation to the other designs maps onto crdt-sync's local / propagated / stable states. Confirm the queue's `HeadCommitOptions.timestamp` becomes the HLC and that a re-drive under the same action id re-sends the same HLC (the identity rule).
8. **The transaction id.** Adding `logical` to the stamp changes stamp ids for new transactions only; nothing persisted keys on an old stamp id across versions. Confirm and note.

## Edge cases & interactions to carry into the implement tickets

- Two writes on one node inside one millisecond: `logical` increments; distinct HLCs. Pinned by a unit test of the clock (pure).
- A record arrives with `wallMs` far ahead of local time: the clock jumps forward (standard HLC) and this node's own writes then carry a future `wallMs` until wall time catches up. Logged in Stage 1; bounded in Stage 2.
- A retry of a torn write re-sends the HLC it minted; a rebuilt attempt at a new revision keeps the same HLC too, since the HLC is the write's identity, not the attempt's. By inspection of `WriteIdentity`.
- `TransactionCoordinator.execute` re-runs the engine and re-stages; that is a different write and gets a fresh HLC. By inspection.
- A validator re-executing statements (`pendValidation`) must not see the HLC as part of what it re-derives; it is supplied, not computed. By inspection of `operations-hash.ts`.
- The checkpoint entry and the invalidation entry take revisions of their own; decide whether they carry an HLC (recommendation: yes, every entry does).

## Tests that pay for themselves

The clock itself: one table-driven test over the construction (local ahead, observed ahead, same millisecond, restart with persisted mark). One test that an entry written through `Collection.sync` and one through the coordinator carry an HLC that re-sends unchanged across a retry, which extends `packages/db-core/test/retry-resends-identical-transforms.spec.ts` rather than adding a spec. Nothing else: recording a field is wiring.
