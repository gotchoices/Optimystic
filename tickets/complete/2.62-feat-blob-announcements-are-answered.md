# Blob announcements are answered

Third of three parts of the blob copying work. The prereqs built the node's reader of committed state (`BlobReferenceCheck`), the client's `announce` and `held`, and the `responsible` hold with its scope. This part built the receiving half: a node decides for itself what to do with an announcement, takes the hold, pulls the blob, and tells the announcer it has it. It deliberately does **not** track who still owes a copy, announce on its own, or report durability — that is `feat-blob-owed-copies-are-drained` (`tickets/plan/2.63-feat-blob-owed-copies-are-drained.md`), which replaces the node's `held:received` log line with the owed entry's update.

## What was built

**`AnnounceLimiter`** (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/announce-limiter.ts`). Two bounds, one instance per node: `admit(peer)` counts one announcement in that peer's fixed one-minute window and refuses past `BLOB_ANNOUNCES_PER_PEER_PER_MINUTE` (600); `readSlot(read, abandonIf?)` runs at most `BLOB_REFERENCE_READS_IN_FLIGHT` (4) reads of committed state at once across every peer together, queueing the rest first-come-first-served, and — when the caller passes the deadline its answer has — drops a read still in that queue when the deadline passes, unrun. At most `BLOB_ANNOUNCE_PEERS_TRACKED` (4096) windows are remembered; a new peer past that evicts the window that started longest ago. All three constants are in `packages/db-p2p/src/protocol-limits.ts` with the honest-announcer arithmetic in their comments.

**`BlobAnnounceReceiver`** (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/announce-receiver.ts`).

- `decide(announcer, ref, scope)` answers in the order §Who may fetch and who may store sets out: `on-demand` → `declined`; a peer the membership predicate refuses → `unreferenced`; a cohort (this node's own `findCluster` view) without self, or a lookup that throws → `not-responsible`; a peer over its allowance → `unreferenced`, with no read; then `BlobReferenceCheck.inScope` under a read slot, raced against `answerWithinMs` (half the node's `responseTimeoutMs`, measured from the start of `decide` so waiting for a slot counts); then `holdResponsible`, flushed before the answer, and `whole` or `fetching`.
- The pull: one session per reference (`inFlight: Map<BlobRef, Set<string>>`), background priority, the announcer as a known holder, no `waitMs`. A second announcer joins and is sent `held` with the first. On resolve, `client.held` to each awaiting announcer the gate still admits, once, not retried. On reject, the hold is kept and the next announcement starts fresh.
- `resume()` restarts the pull of every `responsibleRefs()` blob that is not `here`; the node calls it off the start path.
- `keepAfterRead(ref)` is reader repair: in the background, cohort → not already responsible → `BlobReferenceCheck.anywhere` under a read slot → `holdResponsible`. Never throws, deduped per reference, a no-op on an `on-demand` node.
- `stop()` makes a pull that ends afterwards send no `held`.

**`NodeBlobService`** gained `afterFetchedWhole?`, called when a session it started resolves and the blob is then `here`, and `fetch` now returns at once for a blob already whole here (after `signal?.throwIfAborted()`), so no `fetch` of a whole blob reaches committed state.

**Node wiring** (`packages/db-p2p/src/libp2p-node-base.ts`): `NodeOptions.blobs.hold` (`'eager'` default), late-bound `onAnnounce` / `onHeld` on the blob protocol service entry, the limiter and receiver built on both roles after `wireBlobFetcher`, the receiver's stop wrapper installed ahead of the fetcher's, `resume()` kicked off, and `BlobAnnounceReceiver` / `AnnounceLimiter` exported from both entries.

### Found and fixed in this pass

**The read queue outlived the answers it existed to serve** (`AnnounceLimiter.readSlot`, `BlobAnnounceReceiver.referenced`). `BLOB_REFERENCE_READS_IN_FLIGHT` bounded how many reference reads ran *at once* but nothing bounded the queue in front of them, and a read whose announcement had already been answered — because the half-response-timeout budget expired while it was still waiting its turn — stayed queued and eventually ran a full read of committed state for an answer that had been sent. So whenever announcements arrive faster than reads finish, the queue grows without bound and the node spends its whole read allowance on decisions nobody is waiting for; every fresh announcement then also misses its budget, which keeps the state going. That needs no attacker: an announcer at the honest rate plus committed-state reads slowed by a partition reaches it, and docs/blobs.md claimed the four slots were "what bounds the work", which they were not.

The fix keeps the design's distinction and makes it true. `readSlot` takes an optional `abandonIf: AbortSignal` — the caller saying its answer has a deadline — and a read still **queued** when that signal aborts is taken out of the queue unrun and answered `READ_ABANDONED`; a read that has **started** is never abandoned and keeps its slot, which is what still makes a slow read count against the node's read work. `referenced` arms the signal from the same timer that gives up on the answer. `announce:read-slow` gained a `started` field so an operator can tell a slow read from a queue backlog. docs/blobs.md (§Moving pieces between machines, §Who may fetch and who may store) and docs/debugging.md say so.

While there, `referenced`'s `RAN_LATE` sentinel symbol became a small `RanLate` result (`{ ranLate: true, started }`), which carries the fact the log needs instead of passing a mutable out-parameter down into the callee.

### Tripwires parked (not tickets)

- **`resume()` starts every interrupted pull at once.** `NOTE:` at the loop in `announce-receiver.ts`: the fetcher's background `get` slots bound the streams but not the number of live sessions, so a node that crashed with thousands of pulls open walks thousands of local trees at once on its way up. Conditional on a crash under heavy announcement load; the remedy named in the note is to start them in batches of the fetcher's slot count.

### Considered and not filed

- **`libp2p-node-base.ts` is 3162 lines** (`wc -l`), about 69 of them this ticket's, and the receiver is wired through two mutable `let`s (`blobReceiver`, `blobPeerAdmitted`) bound several hundred lines after the service entry that closes over them. Both the size and the hand-rolled late binding are already owned by backlog `debt-node-factory-wiring-steps-own-their-teardown`, which carries the measurements; nothing new to add to it, so no second ticket and no arm.
- **`holdResponsible` is written and flushed on every re-announcement** of a reference the node already holds, together with a fresh committed-state read, once per pass of the announcer's drain. That is the design — the hold's `at` means "last verified" — and the accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` at `local-blob-service.ts` already records the write-every-call cost. Left alone; its revisit condition has not tripped.
- **`announce:stat-failed` reads a throwing local stat as "not here"**, so a node with one corrupt local tree answers `fetching` for a blob it will never finish. The handoff flagged it. It is the right call against the alternative (resetting the stream over a fault the announcer cannot act on), the pull reports the same corruption as `fetch:gave-up`, and the announcer's drain gives up under its own rule. No change.
- **The defensive `.catch` that logs `announce:read-failed`.** `BlobReferenceCheck.inScope` catches both of its reads and cannot throw, so the branch is unreachable through the node's own wiring — but `references` is injected as an interface, and converting an unexpected throw into `uncertain` rather than a reset stream is the cheap, honest choice. Kept.
- **A `held` arriving before the node finishes starting is logged `held:dropped-non-member`**, because the membership closure refuses until the gate exists. Harmless today (nothing acts on `held`) and self-correcting once the owed drain lands, since the owed entry is re-announced. Noted here for that ticket rather than changed.

### Tests

The implementer's four tests were read as code under review. All four pin something the specification names, so none was cut: the wire round trip with the hold durable *before* the answer; reader repair end to end; the three answers reached before any pull, each with no hold written; and the per-peer window with its boundary and per-peer isolation.
