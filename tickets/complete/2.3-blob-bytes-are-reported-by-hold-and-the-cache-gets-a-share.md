# Blob bytes are reported by hold, and the cache gets a share

## What landed

Implemented by `ticket(implement): blob-bytes-are-reported-by-hold-and-the-cache-gets-a-share`, reviewed and amended in this pass. Four pieces, all inside `@optimystic/db-p2p` apart from two comment corrections in the filesystem and browser storage packages.

**`LocalBlobService.bytesByHold()`** — a new door, `BlobByteRecords`, beside `BlobCacheRecords` / `BlobResponsibleRecords` / `BlobReclaimRecords`, answering a `HoldBytes` (`{ bytes, unsized }`) per hold kind. One pass over `readableRecords`, each blob attributed to the ONE hold that keeps it by precedence `pin > owed > responsible > cached`. A record carrying no `size` is counted in `unsized` rather than as zero bytes. The precedence, the over-count and the unreadable-record rule are stated on the method.

**`StorageAccount.breakdown()` and `StorageBreakdown`** — the whole `StorageCapacity`, plus `pieceBytes`, `blocksAndBookkeepingBytes` and `blobBytesByHold`. The blob record source arrives as a thunk (`StorageAllotmentOptions.blobRecords`), so no setter and no mutation. `capacity()` was split into `capacity()` + a pure `capacityOf(scan)` so `breakdown()` builds both off one scan. The blocks-and-bookkeeping tripwire `NOTE:` sits on its field.

**`cacheBudgetBytes` and `CACHE_ALLOTMENT_SHARE`** — one pure function, `min(declared cap, total × ⅛, total − (used − cached))`, floored at 0, with an unknown total answering the declared cap unchanged. Both reasons for an eighth (256 MiB is an eighth of 2 GiB; 12.5 % cannot reach the 85 % `moveOut` threshold) are on the constant.

**A budget asked for per run** — `BlobCacheBudgetDeps.budgetBytes` is a function asked once per `runOnce`, given that run's `sizedCacheBytes(blobs)`. `libp2p-node-base.ts` wires both thunks on the one common path, serving and client alike.

Docs: `docs/blobs.md` §Holds and §Lifetime, `docs/debugging.md`'s `blob:reclaim` row, `NodeOptions.blobs.cacheBudgetBytes` and `DEFAULT_BLOB_CACHE_BUDGET_BYTES` (both now say "cap"), and `packages/db-p2p/docs/storage.md` §9.

## Review findings

The implement-stage diff was read before its handoff. Checked, in this order: the three new figures' arithmetic against every combination of "measured / not measured" each backend can produce; the budget rule against all five of its arms and against what the eviction walk then does with it; the attribution's precedence against a record carrying several holds and against a record carrying none; the error path of every read any of the three new entry points makes; what the change means for each shipping storage backend in turn (filesystem, SQLite, LevelDB, browser, memory); and every document that mentions the cache budget or the account, not only the ones the ticket named.

### Fixed in this pass

- **A storage fault removed the cache's bound instead of tightening it** (`libp2p-node-base.ts`). The budget thunk awaited `storageAccount.capacity()` and let it reject. `capacity()` rejects whenever the block store's own use read rejects — that read is deliberately not caught, because ring selection must not act on a figure nobody measured. A rejection there rejected the whole enforcement, `BlobCacheBudget.request` logged it and moved on, and so nothing evicted for as long as the fault lasted: the blobs held only for reads grew without any bound at all. Before this ticket the budget was a constant and could not fail, so this was a new regression on an error path, not a pre-existing one. Fixed by giving the wiring a named helper, `enforcementBudgetBytes`, that catches the failed read, logs `cache:budget-unmeasured`, and answers the declared cap — which is the answer `cacheBudgetBytes` already gives a node that cannot measure its storage, so a read that failed and a backend that reports nothing now behave alike. Ring selection keeps the rejection. The contract is now stated on `BlobCacheBudgetDeps.budgetBytes` ("it must ANSWER rather than reject"), on `enforce()`'s list of what rejects it, in `docs/debugging.md` and in storage.md §9.
- **Two documents claimed consumers that do not exist.** `StorageAccount.breakdown`'s own comment and storage.md §9 both said the method "is read by an operator's dashboard and by `logRingInfo` in reference-peer". Neither reads it; nothing does. Both now say so, and the method's comment adds that its assembly is therefore unproven against a real backend and that the first caller should check the figures against the store they describe. This repo's documents are careful to mark what is **Built:**, and these two were asserting the opposite of the truth.
- **`docs/debugging.md` still described the old budget.** Its `blob:reclaim` row said the budget was "sized by `NodeOptions.blobs`'s `cacheBudgetBytes`, 256 MiB by default" — the flat figure this ticket replaced. It now carries the three-way minimum, that the budget is asked for again at every enforcement, and the new `cache:budget-unmeasured` line. The package's log-namespace mirror test does not guard message names, only namespaces, so nothing would have caught this.
- **Two piece stores' `NOTE:`s now asserted something false.** `FilePieceStore.bytesUsed` said "a node whose block store shares the base path never calls it", and `IndexedDBPieceStore.bytesUsed` said it "runs only off the documented configuration … and never calls it". `breakdown()` calls exactly that method in exactly that configuration — it is how the piece figure is separated from a block store that measured it whole — so on the filesystem backend the report stats every piece, and on the browser backend it reads every stored byte. Both `NOTE:`s now name `breakdown()` as the caller, say what the read costs on that backend, and say what to build if the report is ever wanted routinely. The cost itself is accepted: without it `blocksAndBookkeepingBytes` would be unknown on every real deployment, which would gut the figure, and nothing on a hot path calls the report.
- **A new hold kind would have been silently dropped from the report.** `HOLD_PRECEDENCE` was an ordered `readonly BlobHoldKind[]`, whose element type constrains only what is written, not what is left out — so a fifth hold kind added to `BlobHoldKind` would have compiled, made `strongestHold` answer `undefined`, and left those blobs out of the report entirely. Replaced with `HOLD_RANK`, a `Readonly<Record<BlobHoldKind, number>>` whose missing key the compiler names. This is the remedy the `NOTE:` beside `HOLD_KINDS` in `blob-record.ts` already prescribes for the same hazard in two sibling lists; the new list is now the one that follows it. `strongestHold`'s `undefined` now means only "a record holding nothing", which the decoder refuses, and the comment at that branch says so.

### Found and deliberately left

- **`breakdown()` has no caller, and that is more than a loose end.** The ticket's own `## Why` is that an operator sees one number and cannot act on it; after this ticket the operator still sees one number, because the only surface a human watches (`logRingInfo` in reference-peer) still logs `capacity()`. Filed as `backlog/feat-an-operator-can-see-what-fills-a-node`, because wiring it is not the one-line change it looks like: the reference peer's status lines go through a `debug` logger whose argument is evaluated whether or not the namespace is enabled, so a naive swap makes every node pay the per-piece walk on every start-up with logging off. The ticket names that gate, the catch the existing call site already has, and the open question of a pollable surface. Filed rather than fixed here because it is outside this ticket's `files:` and because the gating decision is design, not cleanup.
- **`breakdown()` rejects rather than answering a partial report** when the block store's use read or the record pass fails, while the free-space read and the piece read it takes itself are caught and leave their figure unknown. Left as it is and documented on the method: `absent` is already spoken for (it means "not looked", not "looked and failed"), the shape matches `capacity()`, and with no caller there is nobody whose needs would settle the question. The method now tells a future caller to catch and say which half is missing rather than widen those fields.
- **The piece figure a breakdown reads itself may be newer than the use it is subtracted from,** by up to the memo's 60 s TTL, and the difference is floored at 0. Already stated on the method by the implementer; checked and correct, and a third scan would not make a report that already over-counts the blobs honest.
- **`StorageAllotmentOptions.blobRecords` sits in the host-facing options type** and the node's spread silently overrides anything a host passed there. It follows the `now` and `usedBytesCacheTtlMs` precedent in the same type and is documented as wiring; not worth a second constructor parameter.
- **The thunk is defensive at today's only call site** — the blob service happens to be built before the account, so the service itself would do. Left: it costs nothing, the comment at the site says why, and the account's contract is the thunk either way.
- **The over-count in `cachedBytes` does not loosen the budget**, which is worth recording because it looks as though it should. Over-counting the cache raises the budget's third term by the same amount it raises the figure compared against it, so when that term binds the eviction trigger reduces to `usedBytes > totalBytes` and the walk sheds exactly the overage. Checked algebraically, not measured.
- **`libp2p-node-base.ts` is 3642 lines** and this ticket added about thirty. No size ticket was filed: the file's growth is not this ticket's, and `backlog/debt-node-factory-wiring-steps-own-their-teardown` already claims the wiring in it.

### Not found

- **No correctness defect in the three new figures' arithmetic.** Every branch was walked by hand against each backend's measuring behaviour: the shared-location case (block store measures the pieces, so the piece figure is subtracted from a total that includes it) and the separate case (the piece figure is added to the block figure and subtracting it leaves the blocks) both come out right, the `blockBytes`/`pieceBytes` "unknown versus 0" distinction is carried correctly through to `blocksAndBookkeepingBytes`, and `sizedCacheBytes`'s `?? 0` fold is the same sum the removed `sized.reduce(…)` made.
- **No regression for a node that cannot measure its storage.** A memory storage reports no free space and configures no allotment, so the total is unknown and the budget is the declared cap unchanged — which is why every existing memory-storage spec was unaffected, and the full suites confirm it.
- **No test cut.** The three tests added are the budget policy over its five arms, the sign of the leftover term's subtraction, and the precedence over a real `LocalBlobService`. None restates the implementation, none verifies a mock, and each covers real branching; they stay as written.
- **No test added.** The one defect found is a two-line catch in node wiring whose siblings (`resolveBlobCacheBudgetBytes`, `assertStorageAllotmentBytes`) are untested by convention and whose helper is not exported; pinning it would mean exporting wiring to test it, which the project's own testing bar rules out. The type-safety fix is enforced by the compiler.
- **No conditional concern left unparked.** The two tripwires this change created — the per-piece read the report now pays on two backends, and the report's all-or-nothing error shape — are `NOTE:`-and-comment entries at their sites, listed above. No other condition was found that is fine now and only becomes work later.
