# Blob fetcher: verify, resume, schedule — complete

`BlobFetcher` (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/blob-fetcher.ts`) is the one code path that receives blob pieces. Each `start` runs a `FetchSession` (`blob-fetch-session.ts`): an unheld walk that ends at once when nothing is missing; a `cached` hold and a second walk whose absent entries are the frontier; rounds over `blobHolderCandidates`, one batch of up to 16 pieces at a time, the stream slot acquired before the pieces are chosen; every piece checked with `checkPiece` against the store's own digest (`BlobFetchStore.digest`) before `storePiece`; a peer serving wrong bytes penalized once (`PenaltyReason.InvalidRestoration`) and excluded for the session; failure classified by `unavailableOutcome`; `waitMs` backoff; `markWhole` for a whole-blob session. `PieceTraffic` (`blob-piece-traffic.ts`) keeps the pieces in flight across a fetcher's sessions, so concurrent sessions request each piece once, and the `stored(id)` waiters. The node builds one client and one fetcher on both roles (`wireBlobFetcher` in `libp2p-node-base.ts`), stopped before the blob service; nothing calls it yet — `feat-blob-pieces-move-between-machines` builds `node.blobs.read`/`fetch`/`stat` on it and `feat-a-referenced-blob-is-copied-to-its-cohort` uses it at background priority.

Departures from the plan, all accepted in review: `digest` lives on `BlobFetchStore` rather than as a separate dep (a mismatch is unrepresentable); a presence check of the batch after the slot is granted; the in-flight map keyed by piece id alone; `done` marked handled internally; extra `fetch:failed` line and `reason` on `fetch:corrupt`.

## Review findings

Read the implement diff (`ticket(implement): feat-blob-fetcher-verifies-and-resumes`) first, then the handoff; compared against the implement-stage plan.

**Correctness — one defect found and fixed.**
- *Two sessions fetching one blob at once failed with an internal assertion.* `FetchSession.batchFor` dropped pieces another session had in flight, then awaited the presence check (`presentAmong`, the plan departure above) before `fetchBatch` claimed the batch. A concurrent session could claim the same piece inside that await, and `PieceTraffic.claim`'s assertion then threw "pieces already in flight were claimed again", rejecting the session. Reproduced with real nodes (two sessions on node B, each asking a different holder first) — it failed every run. Fixed in `FetchSession.askPeer`: the batch is filtered for in-flight pieces again with no await before `fetchBatch` claims it (comment at the site; `batchFor`'s doc updated). The handoff listed concurrent sessions as "inspection plus the claim assertion"; the assertion was what fired.
- Checked by reading and found sound: abort and stop paths (the session controller forwarding both signals, listeners detached on settle, a batch already sent using the fetcher's stop signal, `stop()` awaiting each session's unwinding, start after stop); the frontier and re-walk only after a stored list piece; the confirming walk before a round ends; the classification inputs (`Round.attempts` plus the session's corrupt peers); `stored(id)` waiter settling on success, failure and outside-range ids; the corrupt-piece `break` aborting the reply stream (`receivePieces`' `finally` aborts an early exit); a truncated reply is the client's `BlobProtocolError`, so a peer cannot leave pieces unanswered without failing and cause a re-ask loop; no session waits on another while holding a claim, so the elsewhere-wait cannot deadlock; the stop-wrapper order in `libp2p-node-base.ts`.
- Known gap left as documented by the implementer: a peer penalized for one piece is recorded `no-answer` for the rest of its batch in that round, which can turn another piece's `not-held` into `peers-unreachable` only when that peer was the round's last hope; inside the plan's stated tolerance.

**Tests.** Added *requests each piece once when two sessions fetch one blob at once* to `packages/db-p2p/test/blob-fetcher.spec.ts`: the reproduction of the defect above, and the pin for the "one request per piece across sessions" contract the handoff left to inspection (each data piece served once across both holders, every piece stored once, content reads back exactly).
