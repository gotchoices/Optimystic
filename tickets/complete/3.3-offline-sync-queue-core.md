Third of four tickets for stage 3 of [docs/offline-sync.md §Implementation stages](../../docs/offline-sync.md#implementation-stages): the queue itself, usable by a `Tree`/`Diary` application with no SQL. Partial commits (`partially-committed`) are the next ticket.

## What was built (`ticket(implement): offline-sync-queue-core`)

- `OfflineQueue` and `OfflineQueueOptions` in `packages/db-core/src/offline/queue.ts`: `enqueue` (seals each collection's open segment under one entry id, emits `queued` synchronously, starts the drain lazily), `onSealedRefused` (records and wakes only; never calls a latched method), `entries`, `status`, `discard`, `onStatus`, `onQueueState`, `queueState`, `wake`, `whenDrained`, `dispose`. One drain loop per queue, serialised: pending refusals, expiry, empty-queue wait, head refusal check, reachability gate, submit (`Collection.commitHead` for one collection, `TransactionCoordinator.commitEntry` for several), outcome by `classifyCommitFailure` class. The gate and the transient failures share one re-check timer. Every removal, the loop's and `discard`'s, runs on one promise chain.
- `packages/db-core/src/offline/status.ts`: the status shapes, `OfflineQueueState`, `QueuedEntry`, and the errors `EntryNotDiscardableError`, `UnknownEntryError`, `QueueDisposedError`, `EntryDiscardedError`, `EntryExpiredError`.
- `packages/db-core/src/offline/reachability.ts`: `ICohortReachability` and `alwaysReachable`.
- `Collection.knownLogTailId()`; `refusalReason` extracted from `classifyCommitFailure`'s `guard-refused` arm and shared with the queue; `RefusesPendsAsShortfall.refusing` on the test double.
- Docs: an "offline queue" subsection in `docs/internals.md`, an `offline` namespace row in `docs/debugging.md`.

Two rules are the queue's own. **One action id for the entry's whole life**, re-supplied on every re-drive with the attempts retained under it as `resume`; a `torn-not-saved` outcome alone mints a fresh one. **In doubt is sticky**: set once an attempt is about to be sent, cleared only by a terminal outcome or `torn-not-saved`; an entry in doubt is neither discardable nor expirable.

## Review findings

Read the implement diff first, then the handoff. Every file the change touched was read, and the design sections it serves (§Draining, §Classifying an attempt's outcome, §Identity across attempts, §Removing an entry, §Following transactions) were compared against the code. Both drivers the queue calls into were read for the two contracts the queue leans on: that a write-ahead hook's throw aborts the cycle with nothing sent and propagates unchanged (`driveHeadCommit` in `packages/db-core/src/collection/head-commit.ts`, `commitEntryOnce` in `packages/db-core/src/transaction/coordinator.ts`), and that an abort reaches the queue as the signal's own reason (`makeAbortError`, used by `AttemptCycle.beforeAttempt`, `RetryBudget.assertOpen` and `abortableDelay`), so the `QueueDisposedError` arm of `applyFailure` is live.

### Fixed in this pass (minor)

- **`whenDrained` could resolve while a discard's removal was still running.** Its synchronous shortcut answered "drained" when the list was empty and the loop parked, but a `discard` of the last entry chains its segment drop off the loop's path, so the caller got the promise back before the `failed` status was emitted and the segment gone. The queue now counts chained removals (`pendingRemovals`) and the shortcut requires none outstanding; the loop's own path already waited on the chain. Pinned by the new spec case.
- **In doubt and the retained attempt were set before the write-ahead hook was awaited.** A hook that threw (the journal's seam in stage 5) left the entry in doubt and kept a never-sent attempt as the `resume` for the next drive, though both drivers promise nothing is sent after such a throw. Worse in a multi-attempt cycle: a never-sent attempt 2 overwrote the retained, possibly-landed attempt 1. `OfflineQueue.recordAttempts` now runs the hook first and marks doubt and retention only once it has accepted the attempt; the design's rule ("an entry that has been SENT is in doubt") is what the code now says. The `NOTE:` moved with it; `docs/internals.md` updated. Pinned by the new spec case.
- **The disposal abort was recorded as the entry's `lastError`** while the comment beside it said it was not a failure of the write. It no longer is.
- **One submission could start after `dispose`**: a dispose landing while the gate was being asked found nothing in flight to abort, and the loop then submitted the head and ran its whole retry budget before the loop ended. The loop now re-checks `disposed` after the gate, and after the pending-refusal step; `expireDue` stops when disposal lands mid-step, since disposal promises to leave every entry as it is.
- **Dead branch**: `nextPendingRefusal` skipped the in-flight entry, but the step only ever runs between submissions. Removed; the comment now says why the submitting entry's refusal is never met there.
- **File size.** `queue.ts` was 906 lines. The parking and re-check timer state (the wake waiter, the pending wake, the backoff step) is its own small unit with its own invariants, now `DrainWaker` in `packages/db-core/src/offline/wake.ts`; the entry record and its status projections are `packages/db-core/src/offline/entry.ts`. `queue.ts` is 799 lines; the two new files are internal to the directory and not exported. One behavioural simplification rode along: a wake resets the re-check step whenever it arrives, where before a wake consumed by the empty-queue park did not. The step is irrelevant while the queue is empty, so nothing observable changes.
- **Docs.** `docs/offline-sync.md` §Removing an entry step 3 said `causedBy` is "the entry whose removal triggered the replay"; the code names the nearest earlier failing entry (`OfflineQueue.causeOf`), and its own three-deep test expects E3 ← E2. The sentence now states the implemented rule and why. The doc's "a refused pend also mints a fresh id" sentence is left to the next ticket, as the implement ticket assigned it.

### Judgment calls weighed and kept

- **`causedBy` names the nearest earlier failing entry.** Accepted: one replay refuses every dependent segment at once, so the literal rule would flatten a dependency chain to E3 ← E1, E2 ← E1; the nearest rule gives the chain an application can follow. Its cost, which the doc edit above records implicitly: two independent entries refused by one rival commit in one reader's replay are reported as E2 ← E1, where neither depended on the other. Neither rule can tell the two apart from a replay's output; the chain form was judged the more useful default.
- **A `partial` outcome is re-driven under the same id for the collections still holding the segment, and not counted as unclassified.** Correct as far as this ticket goes; the next ticket owns reporting the entry as `partially-committed`.
- **A wake arriving mid-submission is kept and skips the next backoff.** Accepted: losing a connection event is worse than one early re-drive. Documented on `DrainWaker`.
- **The head's promotion to `waitingFor: undefined` is not re-emitted as a `queued` event.** Accepted; `status()` and `entries()` reflect it live, and the next emission is normally `submitting`.
- **After a defect the drain stops and `enqueue` refuses.** Accepted as the conservative choice (segments stay sealed; nothing is lost), with one tripwire below.

### Tripwires recorded (conditional, not tickets)

- `OfflineQueue.stopOnDefect`: a removal that throws is treated as a queue defect, but `dropSealed`'s replay reads blocks through the collection's source and can throw for a cache miss while the cohort is unreachable. Fine while every replay runs over blocks the staging read into the cache; the `NOTE:` names the remedy (retry the removal on the re-check timer) if `offline:drain-stopped` is ever seen with such an error.
- `OfflineQueue.expireDue` (pre-existing `NOTE:`): expiry is applied up to one re-check delay late.

### Tickets filed

None. No finding served a current-release anchor beyond what was fixed inline, and no class-level invariant was left unguarded by the fixes above.

### Tests

The implementer's ten cases were read as code under review; each pins a design contract rather than the implementation (lifecycle, multi-collection slice, gate, three-deep cascade, reader-found refusal, the transient classes, crash-free in-doubt resolution, discard racing the drain, a local commit mid-drain, disposal mid-flight) and none verifies a mock, so none was cut. One case was added for the two defects found: a write-ahead hook that throws leaves the entry queued, not in doubt, with the hook's error as `lastError` and nothing sent; `discard` then succeeds and `whenDrained` resolves only after the discard's `failed` status.

## Known gaps carried forward

- `docs/offline-sync.md` still says a refused pend mints a fresh id; the next ticket (partial commits, marking the stage) corrects it, as assigned.
- No stage-6 reachability or wake wiring in db-p2p; the gate defaults to always reachable.
- `QueuedEntry` exposes no action id; the spec reads it through the write-ahead hook. The journal will need it on the status shape.
- One retained attempt per collection, mirroring the collection's own `retainInFlightAttempt`: a cycle whose earlier attempt landed on a coordinator lagging its cohort, and whose later attempt was then recorded, resumes from the later one. A limitation of the collection's retention, not the queue's.
