# A blob's owed copies are drained

`node.blobs.announce(ref, scope)` makes a node owe the blob to its cohort, and `BlobCopyDrain` announces it to each owed member until every member that accepts blobs holds it, reporting durability as that changes and releasing the writer's `owed` hold at `full`. A committed reference therefore survives the writer going offline once the cohort holds it.

## What landed

- **`BlobCopyDrain`** (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/blob-copy-drain.ts`) holds one `DrainScheduler<OwedBlobEntry, BlobCopyTally>` and supplies two seams: the owed-blob ledger as the entry source, and one announcement round per entry. Public surface: `owe`, `noteHeld`, `settle`, `checkNow`, `isDeclining`, `noteDeclined`, `getDiagnostics`, `start`/`stop`.
- **The cohort view.** `findCluster(routingKeyForBlob(ref))` plus `absentCohortMembers` (an offline member still owes a copy), then an acceptance lookup over the other members. Any of the three failing leaves the view *unresolved* rather than short of members, because a view short of members could read `full` and release the writer's hold while a member that never got the blob is simply unnamed.
- **One entry's step.** Settle; stop at `unrouted`, at a vanished entry, or at `full`; `solo` when nobody else accepts; `waiting` when no owed member is reachable; otherwise announce to each reachable owed member in turn and act on the answer (`whole` → holder, `fetching` → wait for its `held`, `declined` → remember the peer, `not-responsible`/`unreferenced`/a rejection → count against the give-up rule).
- **`settleRef` is the one durability transition,** serialized per reference by the drain's own `KeyedSerialQueue`, so a pass and an arriving `held` cannot both decide the class changed and emit the same value twice. At `full` it writes the class on the record, deletes the entry, and only then releases the `owed` hold.
- **Wiring** (`createLibp2pNodeBase`): the drain is built on **both roles** — a client is often the machine a blob was put on — which is why `PartitionDetector` moved up out of `wireServingRole` and is threaded in through `ServingRoleContext`. `NodeOptions.blobs.copy` configures it; `enabled: false` builds none and then `announce` rejects rather than recording an entry nothing would send. `node.blobCopyDrain` and `node.owedBlobLedger` are exposed for tests and diagnostics, and the `onHeld` closure feeds `noteHeld`.

The code arrived in two commits: a salvage of an interrupted run (`f3a9c3af`, the drain and all the wiring) and then `ticket(implement): feat-blob-owed-copies-are-drained`, which verified that code against the ticket and added the two specs and the doc edits. The review read both as one diff.

## Review findings

Read the salvaged source and `ticket(implement): feat-blob-owed-copies-are-drained` as one diff, before the handoff summary — which mattered here, since the ~430 lines of `blob-copy-drain.ts` had never been read adversarially by anyone.

**Correctness — one finding, filed; no defect fixed inline.**

- **A cohort member read as `refuses` may only be unidentified, and is then dropped from the durability denominator.** `BlobCopyDrain.accepts` leaves a `refuses` member out of `view.accepting` — out of the numerator *and* the count it is measured against. `refuses` comes from the member's peer-store protocol list, and a member that has not finished libp2p's `identify` can hold a *partial* list (the protocol of a stream it opened to us, merged by libp2p), which reads `refuses`. A pass landing inside that window can therefore read `full`, release the writer's `owed` hold and delete its copy while that member holds nothing — one copy short of what `full` promises, and nothing re-announces to it, since its cohort membership never changed. Not a loss: `full` is unreachable without some *other* member holding the blob. A NOTE on `blobAcceptanceOf` had already recorded the misread with an explicit condition — "harmless while a refusal only skips one attempt" — and this drain is what trips it, so the decline was not still standing. Filed as `tickets/backlog/debt-a-peer-read-as-refusing-may-only-be-unidentified.md` at the representation rung: `protocolSupport` should tell "identified and lacks the protocol" from "record incomplete" at the source, which also retires `membershipOf` reading such a peer `foreign`, and which `feat-the-blob-cohort-reaction` will lean on next (it makes the same verdict decide when a departing node may drop a `responsible` hold). Both sites now carry a NOTE pointing at it.
- Everything else read clean. Checked by reading and by running: the `full` path's write-delete-release order and what a crash between the steps leaves; `settleRef` returning `undefined` for a vanished entry on every caller; the pass's second settle against the *same* view it read, so its own announcements are counted over one cohort; an `unknown` acceptance counted as owed (and never announced to, since the scheduler's eligibility reads the same protocol list — consistent by construction); `hold: 'on-demand'` excluding this node from `accepting` while `holdsWhole` still adds it to the holder set, which cannot reach `full` without another member; a `held` for a blob no longer owed; `not-responsible` counted as a retry and never remembered as a refusal; the give-up bound leaving the entry and the hold in place (so a peer abandoned mid-copy stalls the copy, never the durability); and the drain running on a client whose unfed `PartitionDetector` reports no partition.
- **Judged intended, not defects.** A transient cohort-lookup failure writes and emits `unrouted` over a known `majority` — `unrouted` is a reportable class meaning "could not be resolved", the same reading the commit path gives it, and the next pass re-emits the real class. And `stat` on a blob that reached `full` can answer `unknown` with no durability, because releasing the last hold deletes the record: documented in `docs/blobs.md`, and marginal in practice since `put` leaves a `pin`.

**Minor — fixed inline: one docs gap.** `docs/blobs.md` §Placement, copying and durability names `NodeOptions.blobs`'s `hold` and `cacheBudgetBytes` in prose but had nothing on the new `copy` sub-option. Added one clause there: `copy` sets the drain's schedule in the same fields as `NodeOptions.underReplicationDrain`, and `copy: { enabled: false }` builds no drain and makes `announce` refuse. Nothing else needed fixing — the two naming corrections the implement run made (`LocalBlobService.announce`'s stale message, the `blob:protocol` row's `held:*` lines) were already right.

**Tripwires — two parked, neither a defect today.**

- At the `solo` arm of `drainEntry`: a `solo` entry can never clear by itself, because "no other member accepts blobs" is a steady state in the phone-only group the docs describe, so the 30-second re-check keeps re-arming and each tick rescans the whole ledger and reassembles one cohort per entry. All local work (`findCluster` dials nothing) and the block drain behaves the same way, but its entries do clear. The NOTE says to give the `solo` verdict a longer re-check of its own if that cost ever shows.
- At `accepts`, and as an amendment to the NOTE on `blobAcceptanceOf`: pointers to the backlog ticket above, so the next reader of either site meets the finding rather than re-deriving it.
- The two NOTEs the implement run added (the unbounded `declining` set, `settleRef`'s record-less re-emit) were re-read against the code and left as they stand.

**Tests — all five kept, none cut, none added.**

- Kept: the three `blob-copy-drain.spec.ts` behaviours (the `local`→`majority`→`full` event sequence with the entry and hold released; the two exclusion arms, `declined` and `refuses`; `not-responsible` as a retry rather than a refusal) and the two `blob-copy.spec.ts` round trips over real nodes (`unreferenced` then `fetching` to `full`; the owed entry and `owed` hold outliving the writer's process, finishing the copy with only the pieces the receiver lacked). Each pins a named piece of §Placement, copying and durability on branching logic; none restates the implementation or asserts against a mock the repo owns. The not-responsible test's abandonment and reconnect assertions do partly restate `DrainScheduler`'s own spec, but its core claim is this drain's; trimming them would be churn.
- The two-node specs run in ~500 ms each, which looked too fast to be real — checked, and they are: in-memory stores over loopback TCP, with the 2 MB transfer and the piece-service counts asserted on the real stores. The "restart" shares the store objects across the two node instances, which is how this repo models a restart; what it pins is that the resumed drain read the entry off the store rather than out of memory.
- Added none. The review found no defect to reproduce, and the one contract left unverified is not worth a test: `copy.enabled: false` → `announce` rejects could only be pinned cheaply at the `NodeBlobService` layer, where the branch is an explicit `if (undefined) throw` that cannot drift — the half that can drift is the node factory omitting `oweCopies`, and reaching that needs a booted node for a config path with no caller. `announce`'s hold-before-entry ordering is a crash-ordering invariant one line long and untestable without fault injection. Both are recorded here instead.
