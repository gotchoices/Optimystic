# Cached blob copies stay within a budget (complete)

Every fetch takes the `cached` hold on the blob it is fetching, and nothing released it, so a machine that read many files grew its storage without bound. `docs/blobs.md` §Lifetime says what should happen: cached blobs are evicted under a byte budget, least recently read first, never while a read of them is in progress. That is what landed, in `ticket(implement): feat-blob-cached-copies-stay-within-a-budget`, plus the review's fixes below.

## What is built

**`BlobCacheBudget`** (`packages/db-p2p/src/blob/cache-budget.ts`) holds the blobs whose record carries `cached` and nothing else within `DEFAULT_BLOB_CACHE_BUDGET_BYTES` (256 MiB) or whatever `NodeOptions.blobs.cacheBudgetBytes` says. `chooseEvictions` is the pure policy: least recently read first, ties by reference so the choice is deterministic, skipping any blob a read or fetch is running on. `enforce` enumerates, chooses, asks `inUse` again immediately before each hold removal, and awaits one piece sweep when anything was evicted. It is single-flight with one queued follow-on — a run in flight read the records before the read that triggered the new call, so joining it would under-enforce. `stop()` refuses further runs and awaits the one in flight.

**The door into the records.** `LocalBlobService` gained `BlobCacheRecords` (`cachedBlobs`, `releaseCached`, beside the existing `inUse` and `sweep`) — a door rather than a second `BlobRecordStore` over the same key-value store, so every write still goes through the one per-ref queue, the sweep gate and `PieceSweeper.noteRecordChange`. `releaseCached`'s change function is itself the condition: a blob that gained a pin, an `owed` or a `responsible` hold since the enumeration keeps every hold and the call reports `false`.

**Triggers.** `NodeBlobService` calls an optional `requestCacheEnforcement` at the end of a read that started a fetch session and again when that session settles — either alone leaves a gap, and the single-flight collapses the pair into one run plus one follow-on. The node builds the budget over its `LocalBlobService`, validates the option (`RangeError` before `createLibp2p`, so a misconfigured host never gets a started node to roll back), installs its stop wrapper after the blob service's so it runs before it, and enforces once at start so a budget lowered across a restart applies to what the last run cached. Nothing runs on a timer: the cache grows only through reads that fetch.

**Docs.** `docs/blobs.md` status line, §Holds `cached` row and §Lifetime bullet; a `blob:reclaim` row in `docs/debugging.md` with its three `cache:` lines.

## Tests

`packages/db-p2p/test/blob-cache-budget.spec.ts`, three specs over a real `LocalBlobService` on a `MemoryPieceStore` and a `MemoryKVStore` whose scheduled sweeps never fire:

- **Ordering, the cache-only rule and the hand-off to the sweep**, which cannot be checked apart: four cache-only 300 KiB blobs plus one that is cached *and* pinned, budget 700 KiB — the two least recently read go, their records and every one of their pieces with them, and the rest (the pinned one included, though it is the least recently read of all) still read back whole.
- **A blob just brought by a fetch is dated by its `cached` hold**, not by the epoch — the review's regression test, below.
- **A blob being read is skipped, and evicted once the read ends**: budget 0, one chunk of a read taken, `skippedInUse: 1`, the record and root piece intact; drain the read and the next enforcement takes it.

## Review findings

**Read the implement diff first, then the handoff.** Checked by reading and running: the eviction order, the cache-only rule, the skip of a blob in use and the re-check before each write, the single-flight-with-follow-on scheduling, `stop()` against a run in flight, the record store's delete-on-empty-holds contract that `releaseCached`'s `true` rests on, the node's option validation and stop-wrapper ordering, the two trigger call sites, both documentation files, and the implementer's tests.

**Fixed in this pass**

- **A blob a fetch had just brought was the first thing evicted.** `cachedBlobs` read `record.lastReadAt ?? 0`, but `touchLastRead` runs at the *start* of a read and never creates a record, so the read that fetches a blob for the first time finds no record to write a read time to — the `cached` hold the fetch then takes carries the only time the record has. Every freshly fetched blob therefore sorted as the least recently read of all, and a cache at capacity evicted exactly what it had just paid the network for, re-fetching it on the next read. `lastReadOf` in `local-blob-service.ts` now falls back to the `cached` hold's `at`. One regression test (`dates a blob whose only read is the fetch that brought it by its cached hold`) fails without the fix and passes with it; `docs/blobs.md` §Lifetime states the rule and why.
- **`entry-parity.spec.ts` failed.** The implement pass added `./blob/cache-budget.js` to `src/index.ts` but not to `src/rn.ts`, so React Native and browser consumers could not import it — the full suite the implement stage does not run is what catches this. Added to `rn.ts`; `yarn check:rn` bundles and compiles.
- **A third copy of the same record scan.** `cachedBlobs` repeated the `listRefs` + `readableRecord` loop that `responsibleRefs` and `listPins` already each carried. Extracted to one private `readableRecords()` generator all three now filter, with the "one `get` per record" tripwire stated once there; each caller keeps its own remedy note.
- **A stale citation**: the tripwire comment lives at `BlobCacheBudget.runOnce`, not `enforce`.

**Filed**

- `tickets/fix/2.81-the-blob-cache-budget-counts-a-partly-held-blob-as-nothing.md` — a cache-only blob with no `size` on its record contributes **zero** bytes, and since the budget's whole decision is "is the total over the limit", a cache made of such blobs is never found over the limit and nothing is ever evicted. Verified over a real service: a 300 KiB blob with all its pieces present and no recorded size reports `cachedBytes: 0` and survives an enforcement at a budget of **0**. Two paths leave such a record, both read from the code rather than run: a fetch for a ranged read never writes `size` at all (`BlobFetchSession.finish` writes it only for a request with no range), and a fetch that is cancelled or loses its peers leaves the hold and its stored pieces with no size. Not fixed here because the remedy is a choice between accounting mechanisms and amends the design of record's "at its record's `size`" clause; the ticket carries what the cheap routes are.

**Parked as a tripwire, not filed**

- Only a read or fetch that pulled something triggers an enforcement, so a blob that becomes cache-only when some *other* hold is released — an `unpin` now, the `responsible` and `owed` releases `feat-unreferenced-blobs-are-reclaimed` will add — can leave the cache over budget until the next read that fetches or the next restart. `NOTE:` above `BlobCacheBudget` in `packages/db-p2p/src/blob/cache-budget.ts`; the remedy if it is ever seen is to call `request()` from those releases too. The implement pass's own `NOTE:` at `runOnce` (every enforcement reads every blob record) stands, now beside the shared-scan note in `local-blob-service.ts`.

**Checked and deliberately left alone**

- **The node wiring is pinned by nothing but reading it** — the `RangeError`, the one-shot enforcement at start, the stop-wrapper order, and the two `requestCacheEnforcement` call sites. The handoff flagged this and asked for a reviewer's judgement: agreed with the implementer. A test that made a fetch happen needs a two-node fixture, and what it would pin is wiring the types already hold together. The ordering claim was confirmed by reading `libp2p-node-base.ts`: the budget's wrapper is installed after the blob service's, so it runs before it and no enforcement writes a record or sweeps after the service has stopped.
- **`chooseEvictions` has no direct unit test** and its ties-by-reference branch is unexercised. Both are reached through `enforce`; a separate test of the pure function would restate it, and determinism on equal read times is not a behaviour a user can observe.
- **The re-check before the write is not atomic with it**, as the handoff says. What covers the window is the sweep taking in-use refs as roots (`feat-a-blob-being-read-is-not-swept`), so the reading blob's present pieces are kept and the read finishes; its `touchLastRead` cannot resurrect the deleted record, since `recordAfterRead` returns an absent record unchanged. Correct as designed, by inspection.
- **A blob larger than the budget evicts the whole cache and then itself.** Inherent to a byte budget and no different from any LRU cache; not a finding.
- **`cache:enforce-failed` is the only report of a failed enforcement**, and a read is not failed by one. Deliberate and documented.
- `local-blob-service.ts` is 881 lines. The project declares no size rule, the surrounding blob modules are comparable, and splitting it is not this ticket's business.
- No new test was added for anything else. The implementer's two specs both pin clauses of the specification and were kept as they are.
