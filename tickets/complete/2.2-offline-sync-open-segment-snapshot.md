Second of three tickets for stage 2 of [docs/offline-sync.md §Implementation stages](../../docs/offline-sync.md#implementation-stages); the design is [§Rollback and savepoints only touch the open segment](../../docs/offline-sync.md#rollback-and-savepoints-only-touch-the-open-segment). Implemented in `ticket(implement): offline-sync-open-segment-snapshot`; this review pass settled the one open decision the implementer left and fixed what followed from it.

## What landed

**The snapshot.** `CollectionSnapshot` keeps the whole tracker's transforms (sealed segments' effects included, which `Tree.readView` builds its committed view from), the pins and the context; `pending` is the open segment only, and a new optional `sealedVersion` records `SegmentList.version` at capture. A hand-built snapshot without it reads as "unchanged".

**The restore.** `Collection.restorePending` is async and takes one of three arms from three facts: whether the sealed list changed since the capture, whether the committed boundary moved, and whether anything is sealed now. Sealed changed, or boundary moved over a sealed segment: **replay** (open segment set to the captured actions, tracker rebuilt by `replayActions`; pins not restored, each re-staged update pins from the cache as it is now). Boundary moved with nothing sealed: the two pre-segment arms (empty captured open segment resets the tracker empty; non-empty restores verbatim). Otherwise: **verbatim**, pins included. A refusal of the restored open segment inside the replay arm propagates.

**Callers.** `Tree.restore` is async. The coordinator awaits all five restore sites, and its two partial-commit arms fold every committed participant first in one await-free pass and restore the failed ones after, keeping the committed subset's event-loop atomicity. In the Quereus bridge `DirtyTree.restore` is `Promise<void>`, and `rollbackToSavepoint` (bridge and connection) is async. Quereus types the hook `MaybePromise<void>` and awaits each connection's in turn, so no statement runs until every restore has resolved.

**Settled in review: a rollback restores every collection before it propagates a restore's throw.** `visitEveryThenThrow` in `packages/db-core/src/utility/visit-every.ts` visits every item in turn and throws the first error after the last visit. `TransactionCoordinator.rollback`, the bridge's `rollbackTransaction` and its `rollbackToSavepoint` loop through it. The bridge's `rollbackTransaction` tears the transaction down in a `finally` (Quereus's transaction manager ends its transaction whether or not a connection's rollback throws, so a bridge left active would make the next `BEGIN` a no-op over this one's state) and sets the degraded latch from a restore failure instead of releasing it; the session-mode `session.rollback()` is no longer swallowed silently. `rollbackToSavepoint` discards the deeper savepoints in a `finally`, matching the savepoint stack Quereus has already unwound. The two partial-commit restore loops (`restoreUncommittedTrees`, the legacy sweep) and the failed commit's own rollback log a restore failure (`commit:restore-failed`, `commit:rollback-failed`) rather than let it replace the error they are raising, since a bare error in its place would have sent `commitTransaction` down its clean-rollback path and rewound the committed trees.

**Docs.** `docs/internals.md` §Staged actions are segments has the fifth rule with the arm table, the pins statement per arm, the propagating refusal, the awaiting callers, the two-pass note, and now the visit-every rollback and the logged partial-commit restores; the guards section's rollback paragraph and the *Staged Edits Keep Their Base* bullet say what each arm does with pins. `docs/offline-sync.md` §Rollback and savepoints names the moved-boundary-over-sealed case and the asynchronous, visit-every restore. `docs/transactions.md` §Committed reads refuse a degraded store names the failed rollback restore as the latch's second cause.

## Review findings

**Read first:** the implement diff, then `Collection.restorePending`, `replayActions`, `unstage`, `SegmentList`, the coordinator's five restore sites and `refreshBetweenAttempts`, the bridge's commit, rollback, savepoint and partial-commit paths, Quereus's `_rollbackToSavepointBroadcast`, `_rollbackAndReleaseSavepointBroadcast` and its transaction manager's `rollbackTransaction` and `commitTransaction` (dist), and every doc the diff touched.

**Checked and found correct:**
- The arm selection. `sealedChanged` is version-based and ignores a hand-built snapshot; `boundaryMoved && hasSealed()` covers a sealed list that is only refused segments (replay skips them, equivalent to a reset); an invented collection replays from its baseline; a moved boundary with nothing sealed keeps both pre-segment arms byte for byte. `Tracker.reset()` clears the pin store, so the replay arm's "pins from the cache as it is now" holds by construction.
- The two-pass partial-commit arms. Each participant's handling is independent of the others', so folding all committed first then restoring the failed changes nothing but the interleaving; the await between the passes is under every participant's latch. Confirmed that in a commit span the replay arm cannot fire: `sealPending`, `dropSealed` and the refresh all take the collection latch, and a failed participant's boundary is where the snapshot captured it.
- Quereus awaits the hook, per connection, sequentially, at every call site; its transaction manager ends the transaction in a `finally` and logs a connection's rollback throw, which is what made the bridge's `finally` teardown necessary.
- No un-awaited `restorePending`, `Tree.restore` or `rollbackToSavepoint` remains in any package's source; the only other `DirtyTree` implementor in source is `Tree`.
- The implementer's four tests all pin distinct contracts (drop-then-restore replays what remains and keeps the read view, a refused segment is not retried, an invented collection replays from its baseline, an open refusal propagates after the sealed re-stage); none restates the implementation or verifies a mock. Kept.

**Found and fixed (minor):**
- The three rollback loops stopped at the first throw, leaving every later collection un-restored. For a statement-level savepoint, where Quereus swallows the rollback's throw, a later `COMMIT` would have committed the failed statement's writes on those collections; for a whole-transaction rollback the bridge would also have stayed active. Fixed as described above. Tests: `packages/db-core/test/visit-every.spec.ts` (the loop's contract) and "a restore that throws during rollback stops no other restore, ends the transaction, and latches the degraded state" in `packages/quereus-plugin-optimystic/test/adapter-integration.spec.ts`.
- A restore throw inside the two partial-commit restore loops or the failed commit's rollback would have replaced the structured error and sent the bridge down its clean-rollback path, rewinding committed trees. Fixed by logging the restore failure and raising the original error.
- `session.rollback()`'s failure was swallowed with no log; it now propagates with the rest of the rollback.

**Filed as an arm (major, class-level):** the throwing collection itself stays half re-staged after a restore's replay throws, the same state `debt-a-failed-refresh-can-leave-a-collection-half-restaged` describes for a refresh. Appended the restore entrance as an arm there, with the one difference the fix has to settle for a rollback (the end state is the sealed segments re-staged with the open segment restored, not "as before the restore"). No new ticket: same site, same fix.

**Tripwires:** none new. The implementer's NOTE on a non-empty open segment restored verbatim across a moved boundary with nothing sealed stands.
