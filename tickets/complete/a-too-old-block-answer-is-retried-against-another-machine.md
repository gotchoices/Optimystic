# A below-floor block answer earns a retry against a different coordinator — complete

## What landed

The prerequisite (`refreshed-collection-caches-a-block-older-than-its-log-entry`) gave a collection a per-block **floor**: once a refresh has walked a log entry saying "revision *r* changed block X", a read of X at a context at or above *r* must come back materialized at *r* or later, and an answer under that floor is never kept in memory. It could not, however, ask anybody else — one `ITransactor.get` was all a `TransactorSource` could make, so against a lagging coordinator the old row was still *returned* until the read-repair window expired.

This ticket lets the asker's floor reach the transactor and turns a below-floor answer into one more reason to consult a second coordinator.

- **`BlockGets.floors?: Record<BlockId, number>`** (`packages/db-core/src/network/struct.ts`) — per block, the lowest revision the asker will accept. A **client-side hint that never goes on the wire**: `NetworkTransactor.get` builds its own `{ blockIds, context }` for every downstream `IRepo.get`, so no peer and no coordinator freshness decision sees it, and an `ITransactor` that ignores it stays correct because `TransactorSource` still judges the merged answer.
- **`TransactorSource.tryGet` fills it** from `BlockFloorCheck.applicableTo(id, context)` — the side-effect-free lookup the prerequisite already exposed — and omits the field entirely when no floor applies, so an ordinary read's request shape is unchanged.
- **`NetworkTransactor.get`'s `isAuthoritative` fails a below-floor entry**, so it earns the same single second-chance round (coordinator excluded) that `unavailable` and `unconfirmedAheadRev` earn. Only a **block-carrying** entry can be below a floor; an absent answer is judged by the existing rules, which keeps the single-round `createOrOpen` probe off the retry path.
- **The merge breaks ties on the newer content.** A confirmed block ranks the same whatever revision it is and only a strictly greater rank replaces, so the stale first answer would have beaten the fresh one its own retry fetched. `beats(candidate, held)` now orders by rank first and, at equal rank with both entries carrying a block, by `servedRevision`.

When every reachable coordinator is below the floor, the highest revision anyone served comes back **unflagged** — no throw, no `unconfirmedAheadRev`. That is deliberate: a refused write can leave its log entry behind while its blocks never take that revision anywhere, and then the below-floor content is the correct content.

The motivating failure is closed. Reader B refreshes past a write, picks itself as coordinator for the changed data block, and serves its own revision-6 copy from inside the read-repair window while machine A one hop away holds revision 7. The floor now rides out on the read, B's own answer fails `isAuthoritative`, the second-chance round re-resolves the coordinator with B excluded, A answers at 7, and the merge keeps 7. The reader sees the new row on the **first** read after the refresh.

### Fixed in this pass

- **The seam between the two halves was asserted nowhere.** The floor is knowledge the *collection* holds, and the transactor can only act on it if the read carries it — but no test looked at the request. Every `NetworkTransactor` case hands `floors` to the transactor already built, and every `TransactorSource` case only checks the retention verdict; only the hand-written `TwoReplicaTransactor` double in `refresh-below-floor.spec.ts` covered it, indirectly. Added `transactor-source.spec.ts` › "carries the applicable floor out on the request": one case asserting the per-block minimum revision is sent, and one asserting the field is left off entirely in all three no-applicable-floor shapes (no floors, a floor on a different block, a read pinned below the floor) — which is also the first assertion that an ordinary read's request shape is unchanged. Confirmed load-bearing: removing the `floors` spread from `tryGet` fails both.
- **Nothing pinned that the floor stays off the wire.** The handoff named this as read off the code rather than asserted, and proposed a db-p2p spec; the invariant belongs to `NetworkTransactor`, since it is the thing that rebuilds the downstream request, so the assertion went there. `network-transactor.spec.ts` › "never reaches a repo" records every downstream `IRepo.get` — the retry's included — and asserts its keys are exactly `blockIds` and `context`.
- **The lone-machine path was untested against the real transactor.** With one coordinator and an unmeetable floor, the retry's `findCoordinator` rejects outright; that rejection is adopted into `error`, which is only raised when a block went unanswered. Traced and then pinned: "is still returned when the retry can find no other coordinator to ask". This is the property `docs/transactions.md` states as "refusing the read would make the block unreadable — and so unwritable", and it previously rested on reading the control flow.
- **The newer-content tie-break now decides every `get`, and was only covered through floored reads.** The handoff flagged it for an adversarial look. Checked for order-dependence across ranks 6/5/4/3/2–0 (equal-rank absents and equal-reason `unavailable` entries still resolve first-arrival; a confirmed older block still beats an unconfirmed newer one, and that ordering is consistent under every arrival order) — no defect. Added "breaks a tie on the newer content when a partial response earned the retry", which reaches the tie-break through a retry reason that involves no floor at all. Confirmed load-bearing: restoring the old `rankOf` comparison fails it plus two of the implement-stage cases.
- **A repaired retry left no trace.** `collection:block-below-floor` is reported from the *merged* answer, so a read the retry fixed was the one case that reported nothing anywhere while still costing an extra network round. Added a `get:below-floor` debug line inside the `belowFloor` predicate, which short-circuits before it on any unfloored read.
- **A documentation sentence became wrong when it was generalized.** `docs/internals.md` said the old content is returned "for up to one read-repair window" in the case where *every* reachable coordinator is below the floor — true of a lagging replica, false of the abandoned-log-entry case that the same paragraph elsewhere describes, where no machine can ever meet the floor. Rewritten to separate the two. The pre-change sentence was about a single lagging coordinator, so the inaccuracy was introduced by the widening.

Test helpers `pinnedAt2`, `blockAt` and `servingRepoAt` were hoisted out of the `a below-floor answer` describe to the enclosing one, so the non-floored tie-break case shares them instead of copying them.

### Recorded as tripwires, not filed

- **`servedRevision` also reads `state`**, which the type requires but a decoded wire response could omit. Ranking never touched it, so an entry like that used to fault only the reader of its own block; through `beats` it now faults the whole `get`, siblings included. The change widens the blast radius rather than creating the fault — `TransactorSource` already called `servedRevision` on any block-carrying entry — so it is a clause on the existing `NOTE:` above `beats` in `network-transactor.ts`, naming the remedy (make `servedRevision` tolerate a missing `state`).
- **The retry is per batch, not per block**, so one below-floor block would re-ask every block that shared its coordinator. Costs nothing today because `tryGet` asks for one block per request and the map never holds more than one entry; a batching read source would be the first to pay it. `NOTE:` on the `BlockGets.floors` doc comment in `struct.ts`, where such a producer would look.

### Weighed and left alone

- **The collection-level case runs against a hand-written double.** The handoff asked whether standing `Collection` over a real `NetworkTransactor` over two simulated repos deserves a `debt-` ticket. Not filed: the risk it names is the double and `NetworkTransactor.get` drifting apart, and what they share is the `BlockGets.floors` contract — which is now asserted directly from both sides (the producer, in `transactor-source.spec.ts`; the consumer, including that the field goes no further, in `network-transactor.spec.ts`). That is what a generalized test would have bought, at lower cost than a simulated-network harness.
- **An absent answer is never treated as below-floor.** A reader whose own replica has not yet seen a block an entry created would get "absent" with no retry. Left as is: `CoordinatorRepo.get` already detects `isMissing` and consults cohort peers on every such read before responding, so the reconciliation happens one layer down, and widening `belowFloor` here would put the single-round `createOrOpen` existence probe back on the retry path. The choice and its reason are stated at the code site and in both documents.
- **No new report at the collection level.** `collection:block-below-floor` deliberately reports what the reader had to reject; a read the retry repaired has nothing to reject. The transactor-level `get:below-floor` line added above covers the diagnostic need without putting a report on a successful-recovery path.
- **`TwoReplicaTransactor` swaps `this.lagAt` around its re-ask**, which would misbehave under concurrent gets. The two-replica case reads sequentially, and a concurrent case would fail loudly rather than silently; not worth pre-hardening a test double.
- **File size.** `network-transactor.ts` is 1346 lines (`wc -l`), third-largest in db-core behind `collection.ts` (1806) and `coordinator.ts` (1680) — within this package's norms, no split indicated.
- **Costs remain unmeasured**, as the ticket said they would be. The existing `NOTE:` at `TransactorSource.mayRetain` was extended by the implementer to name the extra round alongside the extra request, with the same remedy; no measurement was attempted here.
