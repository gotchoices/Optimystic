# Complete: `Collection.update()` reports a refresh that fell short of the tail

## What shipped

A refresh (`Collection.update()`) arrives at a revision by two different routes: it reads the
collection's **log tail block**, whose stored state names the newest revision committed under that
collection id, and it separately walks the log chain and advances the collection to whatever that
walk reports. The tail's number was used only to unblock the walk and was then discarded, so a walk
that landed *below* it returned as though nothing newer existed.

`updateInternal` now captures that tail revision and, after the walk, hands it to a new private
`Collection.reportShortfall`. On a shortfall it emits one line on the existing
`optimystic:db-core:collection` channel:

```
optimystic:db-core:collection collection:context-short-of-tail id=test-collection before=1 after=1 tail=6
```

It reports and returns normally — it does not throw and does not adopt the tail's number. Its
sibling `collection:context-not-lowered` (same file, `advanceContext`) covers the opposite
direction, a collection declining to move *backwards*.

`docs/debugging.md` documents the line field by field and wires it into the decision-table row
*"a live refresh ran immediately before the read and `rev` did not move"*. `docs/internals.md`
records the guard alongside the monotonicity rule it sits next to.

## Review findings

### Checked and clean

- **False positives on the healthy path — the risk that mattered most.** Rather than reason about
  it, measured it: ran all three suites that exercise `update()` with the channel switched on
  (`DEBUG='optimystic:db-core:collection'`) and grepped the output. **Zero** `context-short-of-tail`
  lines across 1392 db-core tests, 1927 db-p2p tests and 656 Quereus-plugin tests — covering the
  transaction coordinator's blanket refresh between commit retries, `updateAndSync`, and the
  plugin's live scan path, which is exactly the hand-check the handoff asked for. The only lines the
  channel produced at all were two pre-existing `context-not-lowered` lines from the deliberately
  lagging-peer tests, and the shortfall check correctly stays silent for those.
- **The race I expected to find, and did not.** The header is read unpinned and the tail is read
  unpinned immediately after, so a commit landing between the two could in principle leave the walk
  rooted at a chain tail that predates the revision the tail block claims — a benign shortfall.
  `Chain.getTail` already defends against exactly this by following `nextId` links past a stale
  header pointer, and the walk is pinned at the tail's own revision, so the two are aligned by
  construction. No finding.
- **Correctness of the comparison itself.** `before`/`after` read from `this.source`, `tail` from
  the throwaway local source bootstrapped off the tail block; `advanceContext` assigns a fresh
  context object rather than mutating in place, so the `before` reference captured ahead of the walk
  is not disturbed by it. Adoption stays monotonic, so a collection already holding a revision at or
  above the tail's claim correctly stays silent.
- **Lint and tests.** `npx eslint .` clean repo-wide. `@optimystic/db-core` builds clean and runs
  1393 passing (1392 before, +1 added here); `@optimystic/db-p2p` 1927 passing;
  `@optimystic/quereus-plugin-optimystic` 656 passing — all re-run after the refactor below. No
  pre-existing failures surfaced.

### Fixed in this pass

- **Source hygiene: a twelve-line comment over a four-line check, inside an already ~115-line
  `updateInternal`.** Extracted to a named private `Collection.reportShortfall(id, tailRev, before,
  after)`, placed directly after `advanceContext` so the two sibling guards read together, with the
  rationale moved into its doc comment. Behaviour and log text unchanged.
- **A doc claim that was too strong.** `docs/debugging.md` stated that absence of the line means
  "the refresh had nothing newer to adopt". The comparison is entirely local — it measures the walk
  against the number *this node's own tail read* returned — so a tail read that is itself served a
  lagging view keeps the line quiet while the collection is genuinely behind the cluster. Added a
  **"What silence does not prove"** paragraph saying so, and naming which sibling line fires (and
  when none does). This is not hypothetical: the lagging-peer tests in the suite are that shape.
- **A doc that should have been touched and was not.** `docs/internals.md` § "The revision context
  is monotonic" enumerates the guards at precisely this site (`advanceContext`, the
  `CollectionHeaderVanishedError` contradiction, `collection:invented`) and had no mention of the
  new one. Added a bullet covering what is reported, why it neither throws nor adopts, and a pointer
  to the debugging section for how to read it.
- **A missing regression the motivating scenario depends on.** The originating field case was a
  collection that stayed short across ~100 refreshes over 30 seconds; an operator reads this line by
  seeing it *keep coming*. Nothing pinned that it is a per-call report rather than a
  once-per-collection latch, nor that a collection that fell short still answers reads. Added a
  fifth test asserting three refreshes produce three lines and that the collection still serves what
  it holds.

### Recorded as a tripwire, not a ticket

- **An over-claiming log tail pins a collection permanently.** The handoff flagged that
  `Collection.open` adopts the tail's revision on trust and adoption is one-way, so a tail claiming
  a revision the log never reaches leaves the instance stuck there, emitting `context-not-lowered`
  forever while the new shortfall line stays silent (the held revision is at or above the claim).
  Confirmed that reading; it predates this ticket. But no condition that makes a *real* tail
  over-claim has been demonstrated — it was seen only through a test double built to lie — so it is
  conditional, not a latent defect with a reachable path. Parked as a `NOTE:` at the site
  (`bootstrapContext` in `collection.ts`), stating what was observed, what stays silent, and that
  the fix belongs at adoption rather than in the refresh if it is ever seen in the field.

### Filed as a new ticket

- **`tickets/backlog/debt-three-copies-of-the-log-capture-test-helper.md`** — the same ~10-line
  `debug` capture-and-restore dance exists three times (`db-p2p/test/support/capture-log.ts`, and
  inline in `db-core/test/collection.spec.ts` and `db-core/test/cohort-topic/coldstart.spec.ts`),
  already drifted in what each returns. The handoff raised it; confirmed the db-p2p copy really is
  unusable from db-core (its channel prefix is hard-coded). Filed rather than fixed inline because
  the obvious shared home — `packages/db-core/src/testing/`, published as the
  `@optimystic/db-core/test` entry point — contains **zero** Node built-in imports today by design,
  and two of the three copies use `node:util`'s `format`. The ticket notes that this is likely
  avoidable (return raw argument lists, format at the call site) but says to settle it deliberately
  rather than have a review pass decide a published entry point's platform surface in passing.
  Checked the board first: `debt-service-logs-split-across-two-logger-factories` touches logging but
  is about channel-naming in db-p2p source, not this test plumbing.

### Deliberately not filed

- **The shortfall test fabricates its disagreement rather than reproducing one.** The handoff was
  candid about this: the double inflates `state.latest.rev` by 5 on the unpinned tail read only. I
  looked for a natural shortfall (stale header pointer, invalidation entries taking revision slots,
  a checkpoint bounding the walk) and found each of them already handled — `getTail` follows
  `nextId` past a stale pointer, `getFrom` takes its revision from the newest entry *before* it
  skips actionless ones, and the checkpoint break happens after the revision is captured. That is
  evidence the guard is well-placed, not a gap to file: the line is a diagnostic aimed at a
  suspicion, and whether a shortfall is always a fault stays the evidence-based decision the
  originating ticket deferred.
- **`before=none` / `after=none` are documented but untested.** Reaching that rendering needs a
  collection holding no revision while a tail claims one *and* the walk failing — constructible only
  by stacking a second lie on the double, which would test the double rather than the code. Verified
  the rendering by reading it instead (`?? 'none'` on both `%s` positions); the documented format is
  accurate.
