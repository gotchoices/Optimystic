# Reactivity: the topic is the collection anchor; only the root moves

Third of three tickets for the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-02 (after `cohort-topic-a-topics-root-can-move` and `reactivity-per-root-state-is-keyed-by-the-tail`). A collection's reactivity topic is now `H(utf8(collectionId) ‖ "reactivity")` for its whole life. Its root is the current tail block's storage group at `H(tailId)` and moves when the log starts a new block; the tiers below the root are addressed by the topic and never move. `H(tailId ‖ "reactivity")` is retired. A rotation is "same topic, the root moved".

## What landed (`ticket(implement): reactivity-topic-is-the-collection-anchor`)

- **db-core.** `reactivityCollectionTopicId(collectionId)` replaces the tail-derived topic; the anchor object is deleted. `ReRegistrationPlan` carries only the new tail. The drain gate takes the collection's topic and carries it unchanged on the wire redirect (`RotationRedirectV1.newTopicId` is now informational; no wire change). `BLOCK_FILL_SIZE_DEFAULT` is the log's `EntriesPerBlock` (32), so the fill tracker fires at the counts the log actually rotates at. `ActiveSubscription.tail` is the latest tail followed.
- **db-p2p.** Notifications route by the collection's topic in the forwarder host, the inbound path and local delivery. `markRotated` takes a `RootRotation` carrying the topic, which origination derives from the event's collection id. The subscription manager is one per collection for its life: `followTail(tail)` registers when it has no registration, registers again when its registration is at the root (the service displaces the old record, which expires by TTL at the old root), and calls `moveRoot` when it is below the root; a registration already naming the tail does nothing. The manager holds the latest followed tail, which a resume names, rotation detection compares against, and the recover transports read per request through a tail getter. The watch service keeps one manager and one registry handler per subscription, created at the first attach and released at close; a tail move is a follow, not a re-attach. The mesh harness drives the real `followTail` on rotation and can seed a tier-1 cohort so a subscriber lands below the root.
- **Docs.** `docs/reactivity.md` (anchor, subscription, watch service, forwarder state, rotation steps, rotation cost, configuration, worked scenarios, authentication, coverage table), `docs/cohort-topic.md` (concepts, application policies, reactivity interaction), `docs/internals.md` (the watch and rotation bullets).

## Review findings

**Read first:** the implement commit's full diff, then the handoff. Every source file in `files:` was read in its current state, as were the cohort-topic service's `register`, `moveRoot`, `withdraw` and `startRenewal`, the db-core subscriber's delivery path, the backfill and resume replay paths, the subscriber registry, the recover serve, and the three docs. Lint, docs lint, typecheck and every test suite listed below were run.

### Major: a late delivery from the old root read as a rotation back to it (fixed inline)

The one real defect. Before this ticket a manager existed per tail-derived topic, so it could only ever receive notifications announced at its own tail. Now one manager receives notifications announced at both the old and the new root, and `detectRotation` treated *any* delivered `tailId` other than the followed one as a rotation *to* it, with no sense of direction. The old root's last notifications and the new root's first are sent by different machines, so one announced at the old tail can arrive after the subscription followed the new one (the origination manager already handles the mirror case, an older commit's event landing after the rollover). The manager then surfaced a rotation "to" the old tail, the scheduler fired a plan, `followTail` re-registered at the old root (which announces nothing further), and the recheck registered again at the new one: two needless registrations and a transient at a dead root. Also, a notification that failed verification still drove this path, so any peer that could dial the subscriber could send it walking to a root key of its choosing.

Fix, in `detectRotation` (`packages/db-core/src/reactivity/rotation.ts`) and `ReactivitySubscriptionManager.onNotification`: the function now takes a `FollowedTail` (the followed tail plus the newest revision the subscriber has seen) and reads a differing `tailId` at or below that revision as a late delivery from an earlier tail, not a rotation. Revisions are allocated in order and the tail only moves forward, so no revision at or below the newest seen was announced at a tail newer than the newest tail seen. The manager tracks the newest revision across delivered, duplicate and ahead-of-head verified notifications, host `rebaseline` reads, and the subscriber's contiguity head (which backfill and resume replays advance), snapshotting it *before* delivery so a contiguous notification is not judged against itself (the first draft of the fix made exactly that mistake and the new test caught it). Rotation detection now runs only for a notification that verified. Tests: `rotation.spec.ts` pins the rule; `managers.spec.ts` reproduces the late delivery after a follow and the unverified case. `docs/reactivity.md` no longer claims a delivered tail "never differs" from the followed one; it says it never names a *later* tail, and that an earlier one is told apart by revision.

### Minor (fixed inline)

- Cut `two collections have different topics` from `topic-anchor.spec.ts`: it restates a hash property with no logic behind it. The determinism and domain-separation cases stay (the latter pins that a collection's topic can never alias a root coordinate placed at the same bytes).
- The simulator (`packages/substrate-simulator/src/reactivity.ts`) still models the earlier design with a 64-entry block and a whole-tree wave; a `NOTE:` at its default says so and that its rotation figures bound the current design rather than measure it.

### Checked and accepted (no change)

- **Rebaseline before follow in `moveTo`.** The read revision is committed and the listeners were already woken for it, so moving the contiguity head first is right whether or not the follow lands; the only difference from the ticket's order is which of the two reads a mid-registration notification as contiguous, and this one avoids a spurious backfill.
- **`rootKey` equality guard at every tier in `followTail`.** A cost saving (the tick calls it every 30 s); the real `moveRoot` is idempotent.
- **Close during an in-flight re-registration.** Traced: close withdraws the handle the manager holds (the old one), and the registration that lands afterwards is withdrawn by `moveTo` on landing; the service's identity guard makes both tombstones go to the right record. Same `sub.closed` path the first-registration close test covers.
- **Old root's displaced records.** They keep the subscriber in the old root's direct-subscriber list until TTL; any late old-tail fan-out is now deduped or ignored by the fix above. Documented at `startRenewal` in the cohort-topic service.
- **Tier-1 mesh case.** Depends on a seeded tier-1 cohort and the harness's modeled fan-out; the spec's `NOTE:` says so and names backlog `feat-reactivity-notifications-reach-child-cohorts`.
- **Stale plan for a successor the log already left** (OLD→A→B inside the jitter): tripwire already recorded at `ReactivityCollectionWatch.reRegister`.
- **Stale `treeTier` after a renewal failover re-walk**: tripwire already recorded at `followTail`, pointing at backlog `bug-a-participant-told-its-registration-is-unknown-never-registers-again`, which does describe that the relookup's landing is discarded.
- **Barrel import.** `config.ts` imports `EntriesPerBlock` from `chain/chain.js` directly, not a barrel.
- No stale `reactivityTopicId`, anchor object, `tailIdAtAttach` option or plan `newTopicId` references remain in source, tests or docs; the wire field `tailIdAtAttach` and the redirect's `newTopicId` are unchanged by design.
