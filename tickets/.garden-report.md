> **What already existed, and what did not.** The genesis admission gap (Optimystic#10) is closed;
> founding data is pushed when the cohort grows (`2-replicate-owned-blocks-when-the-cohort-grows`);
> cohorts of one and two read and repair quietly (`1-solo-node-read-repair-never-settles`,
> `1-small-cohort-arming-rule`). Cohort *width* already follows the peers that exist. What nothing
> owned was the **safety yardstick**: `clusterPolicy.assumedClusterSize` fed two consumers and set
> BOTH, so a host raising it to its honest machine count also raised the write-admission floor to
> `ceil(0.75 × N)` declared peers. A cadre of phones cannot promise that many awake machines, so the
> downstream consumer pinned it at 2 for every party size — leaving `corroboratorCapacity` at
> `max(visiblePeers, 1)`, where a routing-shrunk view relaxes the corroboration floor to a single
> voter at any size. The direction a growing cadre needs is the SAFE one (raise the repair floor), and
> it was welded to a floor the host could not afford to raise.
>
> **Design done by a subagent, on the maintainer's instruction.** Its brief was told to confront the
> security tension head-on rather than reach for a setter: the reason the size is declared and not
> observed is that the observed view rides unauthenticated routing, so a mutation path is a new way to
> talk the floor down.
>
> **Recommendation adopted: rebuild, do not mutate.** The only legitimate changer is the in-process
> host on its own signed membership records, which a construction-time argument enforces for free;
> all four consumers hold the number as a `readonly` snapshot by design, so mutation means either a
> shared mutable cell or rebuilding those objects — a restart wearing a hat. Rebuilds are already
> routine downstream (every hibernation wake). Raise-only is the safe direction and a not-yet-applied
> raise leaves the node where it is today, so nothing forces a restart at "add a backup". Recorded as
> an accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` at `resolveClusterPolicy` with its revisit condition.
>
> **Shipped: `clusterPolicy.repairCorroborationClusterSize`** — declare the repair floor without
> touching the write floor. Plus a `--repair-corroboration-cluster-size` reference-peer flag, a
> widened `repair-fault-tolerance` advisory, and doc updates across five files. Board drained,
> 3 transitions.

## Decisions waiting on you

These five are in `backlog/` but their real content is a question only a human should answer. Per the
workflow's own rule ("should we do this at all" belongs in `blocked/`, not `backlog/`), several
arguably belong in your inbox rather than the queue — but moving them is a routing decision I am not
authorised to make on my own initiative, so they are flagged here instead.

1. **`debt-optimystic-key-format-migration` — the highest-leverage decision on the board.** The
   question is one sentence: *does any persisted Optimystic data created before the injective-key-
   encoding change need to survive an in-place upgrade?* If **no**, the ticket closes today (consider
   stamping a format version so the next breaking change is detectable) and **two further tickets
   unblock immediately** — `debt-optimystic-true-key-ordering` and, behind it,
   `debt-optimystic-pk-range-seek`. If **yes**, it needs a real reindex capability covering three
   separate on-disk changes (key framing, the schema catalog's index-column shape, and the moved
   UNIQUE-enforcement tree URIs). One answer either retires a ticket or creates a project.

2. **`debt-mixed-version-identify-incompatibility`** — pre- and post-`849fd94` nodes connect but never
   complete the `identify` handshake, so each half of a staged rollout silently stops picking the
   other for any work. Three genuinely different answers: write it down and upgrade everything at
   once; ship a transition release speaking both protocol strings; or make the silent skip legible.
   The cheap answer is only correct if no long-lived multi-node deployment predates the fix — that is
   the fact only you have.

3. **`feat-phantom-read-protection`** — the ticket's own `tradeoffs:` says the answer may simply be
   "no", in which case the right outcome is to close it having recorded that the removal of the
   accidental protection was deliberate. It is a question about the system's intended isolation level,
   not a piece of work.

4. **`feat-dispute-subsystem-live-activation`** contains an embedded decision that must be settled
   *before* any code is written: should a remote peer need to pass the inbound-authorization hook to
   make this node sign a dispute vote? Gating it protects a node from producing attributable signed
   statements for strangers; not gating it keeps reports from unadmitted peers — who are exactly the
   peers a dispute is usually about — from being silently dropped. The ticket sketches "gate it, but
   separately controllable" as a reasonable resolution.

5. **`feat-no-deployment-validates-transactions-at-pend`** — whether the permissive posture (members
   approve what the coordinator sent without re-deriving anything) is a deliberate staging decision or
   an oversight. It is not recorded as deliberate anywhere, and signature-enforcement work was planned
   on the assumption that flipping a flag was all that stood between it and production. If it *is*
   deliberate, this wants an accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` at
   `ClusterMember.validatePendOperations` and the ticket deleted — which is a decline, and therefore
   yours to authorise.
