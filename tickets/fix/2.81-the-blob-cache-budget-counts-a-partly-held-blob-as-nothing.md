# The blob cache budget counts a partly held blob as nothing

A node keeps a byte budget over the blobs it holds only because it read them, and evicts the least recently read of them once they exceed it (`BlobCacheBudget`, landed by `ticket(implement): feat-blob-cached-copies-stay-within-a-budget`). A blob's contribution to that total is the byte size written on its blob record. A blob the node holds only part of has no size written on its record, so it contributes **zero** — and because the budget's whole decision is "is the total over the limit", a cache made of such blobs is never found to be over the limit and nothing is ever evicted, however much disk the pieces take.

The effect is the one the budget exists to prevent: storage that grows without bound.

## What was observed

Over a real `LocalBlobService` on an in-memory piece store: a blob of 300 KiB whose record holds `cached` alone and carries no `size`, with all three of its pieces present in the piece store. One enforcement with the budget set to **0 bytes** — the strictest setting there is — reports `cachedBytes: 0`, evicts nothing, and leaves all three pieces stored.

## How a cache-only blob comes to have no size

Both by reading the code, not by running it:

- **A read of part of a blob.** `BlobFetchSession.finish` writes `size` (and `whole`) only when the request asked for no byte range at all, so a fetch started for a ranged read never writes one. `BlobReadOptions` has carried `offset` and `length` from the start and `NodeBlobService.read` passes them through to the fetch; nothing in this repository asks for a range yet, but the capability is public and documented, and `blocked/quereus-reads-and-writes-a-range-of-a-large-value` is about putting it to use.
- **A download that never finished.** The `cached` hold is taken before the first piece and nothing removes it, so a fetch that is cancelled, loses its peers, or hits a failing link leaves the hold, the pieces it did store, and no size. A later fetch of the whole blob that succeeds does write the size; one that keeps failing never does.

`LocalBlobService.stat` writes a size back onto the record only when its census finds the blob whole, so nothing else repairs the gap either.

## What should be true

The budget should bound the storage the node actually spends on blobs it keeps only for reads. A blob the node holds part of should count against the budget — not as nothing — so that a cache of partly held blobs is evicted like any other once it is over the limit, and the blobs chosen are still the least recently read.

Two further requirements the fix should respect, both already stated in `docs/blobs.md`:

- **Over-counting is the acceptable direction, under-counting is not.** §Lifetime already accepts that a piece shared between two blobs is counted once for each, "which over-counts and so evicts sooner — the direction that uses less storage".
- **Counting must stay cheap.** The design of record explicitly rejected walking each blob's tree for the pieces actually present, "which costs one piece-store read per piece per enforcement", and rejected the piece store's own total, which cannot tell a cached blob from a pinned one.

What is already known about the cheap routes, for whoever picks one up: a fetch learns the blob's full size from its root list piece on its first plan (`Plan.listSize` in `packages/db-p2p/src/blob/blob-fetch-session.ts`), under the `cached` hold and before any piece is transferred, and `LocalBlobService.correctRecord` can already write a size without claiming the blob is whole. A blob whose root piece never arrived holds nothing worth counting, since the fetch walks the tree from the root.

## Docs

`docs/blobs.md` §Lifetime currently says what counts against the budget is every cache-only blob "at its record's `size`". That clause is what this ticket changes; whatever rule replaces it belongs there, with the reason.
