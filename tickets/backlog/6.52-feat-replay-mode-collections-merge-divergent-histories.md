# Replay-mode collections merge divergent histories

Stage 2 of [docs/crdt-sync.md](../../docs/crdt-sync.md) §Migration Path, with Stage 4 folded in as a later slice, carrying the field evidence the old `6.5-partition-healing` ticket collected (2026-08-30 to 2026-10-14) and the decisions taken on it. It waits for plan `hlc-on-every-transaction` (the order) and plan `a-transaction-carries-its-lane` (which transactions may merge) to ship and be measured, per the doc's own rule.

## The decision of record

**Maintainer, 2026-09-15:** do not pin the lone-survivor rule with a point fix at the solo write path. The fix is a sync layer in which transactions on both sides of a gap proceed and merge, rather than one side being refused or accepted by a race. **Maintainer, 2026-10-15:** no storage rule refuses a lone write either; the fork a lost write followed by a lone write leads to is fixed by merging both writes. Offline mode is the one place a lone write is refused by default (`refuseSolo`, `docs/offline-sync.md` §When to submit), and that stays a property of the queue, not a rule for writes in general. A reviewer who finds the non-determinism below and wants to close it adds evidence here rather than choosing a rule at the call site.

## What the field has shown: five ways into one state

Every route ends the same way: two copies of a collection under one id, each internally consistent, naming different actions at the same revisions. The guard that refuses to move a collection backwards (`Collection.advanceContext`'s `context-not-lowered`) then declines the other side's view, so the split is permanent and silent. In a cohort of two, the next write to a forked block declares a content digest computed on one side's content, the other member previews it against its own and votes `content-digest-mismatch`, and since every member must approve at two, no later write to that block succeeds. Repair sees two claims at one revision and cannot tell which is right (plan `debt-repair-cannot-tell-a-fork-from-a-lagging-cohort`).

1. **A joiner writes before it has ever synced** (sereus, 2026-09-16). With no serving partner in its routing view, `findCluster` answers a self-only cohort, the solo exit reports the missing header as a confirmed absence, `Collection.createOrOpen` invents the collection and the write commits alone. On connect the two copies of `Message` and `Member` shared one id; the joiner's acknowledged message vanished. Largely closed since: the bootstrap-contact wait and the `cohort-unreachable` / `named-by-log` flags (`docs/internals.md`, the three-valued block read), and sereus gates a non-founder's database until its strand header is readable. Two residuals this ticket owns: a machine that holds the header, loses its peer and writes to a table whose collection it never fetched still founds a divergent copy; and the schema catalog is written alone at every launch and is fork-safe only while every machine writes byte-identical content (the `NOTE:` near `SchemaManager`'s record construction in `packages/quereus-plugin-optimystic/src/schema/schema-manager.ts`).
2. **A lone survivor writes while its partner is away** (two machines, default cluster size, `repairCorroborationClusterSize: 2`). Phase 5 of the small-deployment lifecycle spec observes without asserting. Over 16 runs: accepted in 11 (after 11 to 31 seconds, once the routing view dropped the absent member; `commit:solo-cohort` with `cohortSize: 1, soleIsSelf: true`), refused in 5 (after 8 to 12 seconds, `Failed to get super-majority: 1/2 approvals`). Which one happens is a race between the write's retries and the routing table forgetting the peer. Over a circuit relay (phase 3 of the two-phones spec) the same write was refused in all 7 runs within about a second, inferred to be because the relay answers at once that the phone holds no reservation, so the retries are spent before the view forgets the peer. The outcome depends on timing and on transport, not on a rule anyone chose. Every acknowledged write was readable on both machines once both were back.
3. **A joiner that briefly loses its link writes as a lone survivor** (sereus GH #44, 2026-10-11). Past the first-sync gate, connected to nothing but the relay, every block's cohort resolved to the joiner alone, the commit took the solo path at `quorum: 'local'` in about 0.1 s, and the phone was backgrounded. The founder never received the rows. To optimystic this is the same shape as route 2 (a node that remembers a serving member not in its ring), so no rule at the solo short-circuit can admit one and refuse the other. What landed instead is policy-free: the solo write names the members it knows of as unconfirmed and owed, and a SQL commit reports its durability class to the application.
4. **A power cut loses an acknowledged write, and the restarted machine writes alone at the lost revision.** Three of four storage backends never synced a write to stable storage; since then a node may leave a write unsynced for a window computed from its cohort size (zero for a write only one machine holds). The steps, on a phone P and a backup B: (i) write W1 commits revision N of block X on both, acknowledged `full`; (ii) before P has flushed W1, P loses power and recovers without it; (iii) P restarts while B is offline, B leaves P's view, P's cohort for X is P alone; (iv) the app writes W2 on revision N−1 as revision N of X, acknowledged `local` with B owed; (v) B returns, the under-replication drain pushes X to B, B already holds revision N (W1's) and accepts the push as a no-op, so the drain counts B as holding W2; (vi) P and B hold different content under revision N, and every later write to X is refused as above. Not reproduced; a two-node in-process mesh over a batch backend whose power-loss discards unsynced batches would reproduce it, asserting that P and B hold different action ids for revision N of X. `docs/correctness.md` Theorem 2's residual prices step (ii) at roughly 2 in a million per write at the defaults and leaves the fork to this ticket.
5. **A single-machine commit outside the collection latch** was pursued separately (a concurrent refresh could make a commit record a revision storage never assigned, producing the same fingerprint with no partition). If that explained the 2026-08-30 capture, routes 1 to 4 remain and the design gap is unchanged.

## What already exists for a merge to stand on

- Every log entry carries **replayable, guarded intent**: the actions, with their guards (`TreeEntryGuard` kinds re-make every uniqueness and lost-update decision on replay). `Collection.replayActions` and `stageInto` already re-stage actions against committed state, atomically per segment, recording refusals. A merge is a replay of two entry sets, not a new interpreter.
- The HLC (plan `hlc-on-every-transaction`) gives a deterministic total order over the union; the lane (plan `a-transaction-carries-its-lane`) says which transactions may be committed locally.
- `advanceContext`'s lineage comparison names the fork point; `BlockMetadata.lineageFloor` and `IBlockStorage.lineageOf` say per member whether its history contains a write; `judgeCohortLineage` folds the answers.
- The implement ticket `a-lineage-divergence-drops-the-read-cache` settles the adopted-divergence half of the parked `advanceContext` decision; this ticket owns the not-lowered half, where a handle on the losing lineage must come to hold the merged one.

## Questions this pass must settle

1. **The physical representation after a merge.** Two options. (a) The entry set is the CRDT and each node keeps its own physical chain, re-appending the other side's entries in HLC order: simple, but block revisions then differ across nodes for one history, and everything keyed by `(blockId, rev, actionId)` (commit proofs, repair corroboration, the durability gate, the under-replication ledger) stops meaning one thing. (b) Deterministic re-linearization: every node rebuilds the chain identically from the merged entry set, so the keyed machinery survives. (b) needs **block ids minted during replay to be a deterministic function of the action and the mint ordinal** (today `TransactorSource.generateId` is random, and the "fixed function of the write and the revision" rule in `docs/internals.md` §Key Invariants shows how much the system already pays for per-attempt minting). Recommendation: (b), with ids derived as a hash of collection id, action id and the ordinal of the mint within the action; coordinate with backlog `block-id-derivation` (speculative), whose anti-targeting goal can later fold a validated nonce into the same preimage, and which this pass has appended an arm to.
2. **Trigger and procedure.** Detection at the seams that already see a fork: `context-not-lowered` on a different lineage, `content-digest-mismatch` at the vote, repair's equal-revision disagreement. Procedure: the last common revision is the fork point minus one; take both sides' entries above it; order by HLC; re-stage from the common state, each serializable-lane entry's guards deciding; re-materialize the blocks; write the merged chain. Who computes and who commits it: a merge is a deterministic function of two tails, so any member may propose a **merge record** naming both tails through the existing consensus and every other member verifies by recomputing; members holding only one side fetch the other's entries over the sync protocol's archive wire. Decide whether a merge is a consensus record or a replay every node runs independently on receipt of the other tail.
3. **What a serializable-lane transaction does under replay.** Its guards re-decide against the merged state; the loser is recorded in the log but has no effect (`docs/crdt-sync.md` §Convergence via Deterministic Replay). The writer must be told: a status read today answers `committed` or not; it needs a `committed-ineffective` answer, and the SQL layer needs to surface it (the offline queue's `failed` status with a cause is the nearest existing shape). This replaces refusal-at-pend for merged histories only; connected writers keep today's refusal.
4. **Revision numbers after a merge.** The merged chain renumbers one side's entries. A handle holding the old numbering must adopt the merged lineage even when its own revision is higher, which is exactly what `context-not-lowered` refuses today. The rule to design: a handle lowers its revision when the cohort's lineage (via `judgeCohortLineage`) excludes the handle's held action at that revision, drops its read cache and floors (as the implement ticket does for the adopted case), and re-stages its pending actions over the merged state; a committed action of its own that the merge made ineffective is reported, not re-driven.
5. **Admission per lane, and the two integration phases.** A `convergent` transaction commits locally at `local` and is merged later. A `serializable` transaction under a declared denominator (backlog `feat-declared-membership-feeds-cohort-assembly`, which carries the denominator rule) waits for its cohort, as a long-lived pend (backlog `feat-long-lived-pend-completes-as-members-appear`) or a refusal reported as a stall, never as a commit on a shrunken view. That makes the lone-survivor outcome deterministic per lane, and this is where phase 5 of `small-deployment-lifecycle.integration.spec.ts` and phase 3 of `two-phones-over-relay.integration.spec.ts` get pinned, and the comments in `member-leaves-and-returns.spec.ts` and `transaction-node-count-sweep.spec.ts` updated. One option weighed and not adopted at the fix stage: before taking the solo path, wait briefly while a dial or identify to a remembered member is in flight (the write-side counterpart of `bootstrapContactImminent`); it narrows the window without changing the rule, and may be worth adding here as a courtesy to the serializable lane.
6. **Multi-collection transactions** (a SQL table and its indexes). One HLC, each collection replays the same transaction; a merge of one collection must merge the others the entry names (`ActionEntry.collectionIds`) in the same pass or report a partial merge the way `CoordinatorPartialCommitError` reports a partial commit. GATHER and the batched pend remain for `strong` collections; this is Stage 4 of the doc, a later slice of this ticket.
7. **Repair's role.** Under a merge, repair must not pick a side at an equal-revision disagreement (today it picks the higher revision, or the multi-signer proof). It should hand the pair to the merge, or decline and leave a named signal. Coordinate with plan `debt-repair-cannot-tell-a-fork-from-a-lagging-cohort`, which may resolve into this.
8. **Cost.** A merge replays from the last common revision. Checkpoints bound the walk and the `committed` lists (backlog `debt-the-collection-log-never-writes-a-checkpoint`); decide whether that debt is a prerequisite.

## Edge cases & interactions for the implement tickets

- Both sides created the collection from nothing (route 1): no common revision; the merge starts from the empty collection and both headers' inserts become one. The catalog is the first real instance.
- A side that holds the other side's acknowledged write only as a push it accepted as a no-op (route 4, step v): the under-replication ledger believes it is held; the merge must correct the ledger or the full-replication event lies.
- A merge while a head commit is in flight on one handle (offline queue): the handle's in-flight attempt is at a revision the merge renumbers; the refresh-deferred rule and `completeOwnEntry` need a merged-lineage arm.
- Three or more sides (a group of phones that each wrote alone): merges must be associative and commutative over entry sets, which HLC order and deterministic ids give, and the fork point is per pair.
- An invalidation entry (a dispute's compensating entry) on one side names an action the other side never saw; the merge carries it and its effect over the merged state, in HLC order.
- A `strong` collection is never merged; a fork detected on one is reported as today and stays the operator's.
- Mixed builds: a member without the merge treats a merge record as an unknown operation and refuses; decide the version gate.
