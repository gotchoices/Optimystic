# The stability horizon and a degraded-consistency indicator

Stage 3 of [docs/crdt-sync.md](../../docs/crdt-sync.md) §Migration Path, plus the one decision about the partition detector the old `6.5-partition-healing` ticket left open.

## What the stage adds

Under replay mode a transaction is **local** (only its issuer has it), **propagated** (others have it, but a lower-HLC transaction may still arrive and change its effect) or **stable** (no earlier transaction can arrive). Stability needs **watermark gossip**: each peer periodically publishes "I will issue nothing below HLC X", and a transaction is stable once every peer in scope has published a watermark past it. The doc's open questions on gossip frequency and bandwidth are this pass's to settle; the recommendation is to piggyback on the cohort gossip round (`docs/cohort-topic.md` §Cohort gossip), which already sends a frame only when something changed.

Reads then choose: `stable` (slightly stale, final) or `tentative` (fresh, may shift). The Quereus integration needs a connection-level setting and a per-query hint; the committed-read arm (`committed.<Table>`) is the nearest existing shape for "a pinned, final view".

External effects wait for stability. The offline queue is already the client-side outbox `docs/partition-healing.md` §Subset That Lands on Today's Strict-CP Optimystic names, and its statuses are designed to map onto local / propagated / stable; this pass decides whether the queue's `committed` status becomes "stable" for a replay-mode collection, or a fourth status is added.

## The partition detector decision

`ClusterMember` accepts a `PartitionDetector` and does not read it. The old ticket listed three places it might be consumed: reject incoming promises during a suspected partition; skip propagation; report status to coordinators. **Decision (plan pass, 2026-11-01): adopt the third only.** The first two block writes, which is the stance the lane design rejects for the convergent lane (it wants to proceed and merge) and makes redundant for the serializable lane (a declared quorum denominator already stalls it). The detector's consequence under this design is the one `docs/partition-healing.md` §Relation to Current Partition Handling describes: raise the stability threshold for pending transactions, hold stability-gated effects longer, and surface a degraded-consistency indicator so a UI can show "disconnected, changes will reconcile" rather than fail silently. When this lands, rename `_partitionDetector` back to `partitionDetector` and make it a `private readonly` constructor parameter; until then the reservation comments in `cluster-repo.ts` point here.

## Edge cases & interactions

- A peer that never publishes a watermark (gone, or on an older build) holds stability back for its whole scope; decide the scope (the collection's cohort, not the network) and how a departed peer leaves it (cohort membership, as the unsettled-claim memo already uses, not a timer).
- A watermark is a promise about the publisher's own clock; an HLC that jumped forward on receipt of a skewed record makes the publisher's watermark jump too. The skew log line from plan `hlc-on-every-transaction` is the input.
- The durability follower (`DurabilityFollower`) answers "fully held", a storage question; stability is an ordering question. A SQL commit may be fully held and not yet stable, or stable and not fully held. Name both to the application without conflating them.
- A `strong` collection is stable at commit; the API must answer so without a watermark round.

## Tests that pay for themselves

One test of the watermark fold (pure): a transaction becomes stable exactly when the lowest published watermark in scope passes it, and a scope member with no watermark holds it. One end-to-end case on the in-process mesh where a lower-HLC transaction arrives after a higher one was reported propagated and the stable read never showed the overturned effect.
