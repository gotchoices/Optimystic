# A referenced blob is copied to its cohort

After `feat-blob-pieces-move-between-machines` a node can fetch a blob's pieces, and after `feat-committed-rows-register-the-blobs-they-reference` committed state records every reference in a reference tree listed in the network's scope registry. This ticket makes the machines responsible for a blob actually hold it: the writer announces a committed reference, each responsible machine checks committed state for itself and pulls the bytes, the writer tracks who holds the blob until all of them do, and holding follows the cohort as it changes. It is what makes a committed reference survive the writer going offline.

## Requirements

**Placement** (docs/blobs.md §Placement, copying and durability). A blob's routing key is the digest in its reference, through a sibling of `routingKeyForBlock` (provided by `feat-blob-references-and-verified-piece-trees` if it has not already); its cohort is `findCluster` on that key, `clusterSize` members from `resolveClusterPolicy`. A node option `blobs.hold: 'eager' | 'on-demand'` (default `'eager'`) says whether the node takes blobs it is merely responsible for. A peer whose protocol list lacks the blob protocol is treated as a member that does not accept blobs.

**`announce`** — local call and wire request. Locally, `node.blobs.announce(ref, scope)` records a durable owed entry and an `owed` hold, then the drain announces. On the wire the receiver answers with one of five values, decided in this order:

1. `declined` if `blobs.hold` is `'on-demand'`.
2. `not-responsible` if this node's own `findCluster` for the blob does not include it.
3. `unreferenced` if the named scope is not in the scope registry (refused before any reference-tree read), or the scope's reference tree, read from committed state, does not list the blob, or that read failed or could not be confirmed current (`BlockUnavailableError`, `BlockPossiblyStaleError`).
4. `whole` if every piece is already here.
5. Otherwise `fetching`: the receiver takes a `responsible` hold, pulls with the background fetcher, and sends `held` to the announcer when the pull completes.

An announcement grants nothing; it says where to look (§Who may fetch and who may store). Reading committed state is how storing is authorized, so the receiver never pulls on the announcer's word. Announcements are bounded per peer. An announcer is sent to, and a `held` accepted from, only peers `admittedBy` the membership predicate.

**Owed ledger and drain.** The owed entry holds the reference, its scope, and the cohort members not yet known to hold it, in the node's key-value store, surviving restarts. The drain announces to each owed member that is connected, on start, on `connection:open` and `peer:identify`, and on a timer while anything is owed, skipping passes during a detected partition, with the same throttle, per-pass cap and give-up rule (a bounded number of unconfirmed rounds per entry and peer, then abandoned until that peer reconnects) as `UnderReplicationDrain`. **The scheduling code is shared, not copied**: today it is typed to block ids and revisions, and this ticket extracts the part both drains need. Where the extraction boundary falls is the planner's choice; `debt-three-sites-repeat-the-certified-push-loop` covers the neighbouring push loop and should be read before choosing. The owed members are derived from the cohort afresh on each pass, so an entry follows the cohort. A member leaves the entry when it answers `whole` or sends `held`. A member that answers `declined` is remembered as declining until it next connects, and is not owed meanwhile. A member that answers `not-responsible` (the two machines' views of the cohort differ for now) or `unreferenced` (it has not seen the commit, or could not read it) is asked again on a later pass, under the give-up rule.

**Durability.** `BlobDurability` uses the `DurabilityQuorum` classes, counted over cohort members that accept blobs, and carries the count beside the class: `unrouted` when the cohort did not resolve; `local` when this node alone holds it or fewer than a strict majority of the accepting members do (wider than a commit's `local`, because a commit is not acknowledged below a majority and a blob is usable from its first copy); `majority` when a strict majority hold it; `full` when every accepting member holds it and at least one of them is not this node. Members that `absentCohortMembers` names count as owed, as they do for a block write. In a group where no other member accepts blobs the class stays `local` and the writer keeps the blob. Each change is an `onDurability` event; reaching `full` removes the owed entry and the `owed` hold. `stat` reports the current class.

**Cohort changes.** `RebalanceMonitor` is parameterized by the routing key rule and the set it tracks, rather than copied, so held blobs (enumerable from blob records) get the same cohort comparison blocks get. A member that joins a held blob's cohort is announced to, through the confirmation-driven path (`recordGrowthOutcome`), so an announcement that fails is re-detected. A node that leaves a blob's cohort releases its `responsible` hold only after confirming that enough current members hold the blob — the floor `RebalanceMonitor` already uses for releasing a block.

**Readers repair.** A node that is responsible for a blob, accepts blobs, finds it referenced and fetches it for a read keeps it under `responsible` rather than `cached`.

**Committing a reference**, as far as the node is concerned (§Committing a reference): no member checks that bytes exist when it votes; a committed reference is backed by at least the writer's copy because the writer's `owed` hold lasts until `full`.

## Edge cases and interactions

- **Unregistered scope.** An announcement naming a scope not in the registry is answered `unreferenced` without reading any reference tree (test: count tree reads).
- **Announce before the receiver sees the commit.** `unreferenced`, then `fetching` on a later pass once the receiver's read catches up (test on a two-node mesh).
- **Receiver cannot read committed state.** A reference-tree read that throws `BlockUnavailableError` or `BlockPossiblyStaleError` must never lead to a pull; the answer is `unreferenced` and the announcer asks again (inspection plus one test).
- **Different cohort views.** The announcer counts a peer in the cohort whose own `findCluster` excludes itself: it answers `not-responsible`, is not remembered as declining, and is asked again once the views agree (test on a mesh wider than one cohort).
- **On-demand node.** Always `declined`, remembered until it reconnects; still holds what it wrote, pinned or read (test).
- **Restart mid-copy.** The owed entry and pieces already fetched survive; the drain resumes and the receiver resumes from missing pieces (test with a persistent store).
- **Partition.** No drain pass runs while `PartitionDetector.detectPartition()` is true (assertion at the pass).
- **Hostile announcer.** Floods of announcements for real, referenced blobs are bounded per peer; for unreferenced ones nothing is fetched (inspection).
- **Two of five accepting members hold it.** The class is `local` with a count of two, not `majority` (test of the classification).
- **This node is the only accepting member.** The class stays `local` however long the drain runs, and the owed entry stays (test).
- **Leaving a cohort with too few holders.** The `responsible` hold stays until the floor is confirmed, and is retried on later checks (test).
- **Block replication unchanged.** The generalized drain and monitor behave exactly as before for blocks; the existing under-replication and rebalance suites pass unchanged.
